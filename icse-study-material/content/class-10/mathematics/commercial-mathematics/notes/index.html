Topic 1 – Goods and Services Tax (GST)

A. Learning Objectives

After studying this topic you should be able to:

  • Explain what GST is and why it replaced the earlier system of separate taxes such as VAT, service tax and excise duty.
  • Distinguish between CGST, SGST and IGST, and know when each applies.
  • Compute the GST charged on a taxable value for a given GST rate.
  • Find the taxable value (and the tax) when only the GST-inclusive price is given.
  • Use input tax credit to find the net GST payable by a dealer.
  • Solve word problems on billing, invoices and tax payable by a customer.

B. Basic Concepts and Definitions

Goods and Services Tax (GST)

GST is a single indirect tax levied on the supply of goods and services across India. It came into effect on 1 July 2017. It replaced a large number of separate taxes (VAT, service tax, central excise, etc.) with one tax, so that a good or service is taxed only once at each stage.

Taxable value

The value on which GST is calculated. It is usually the price of the goods or services before adding GST. It is also called the base price or the selling price (excluding tax).

CGST, SGST and IGST

For a supply within the same state (intra-state), GST is split into two equal halves:

  • CGST (Central GST) – goes to the Central Government.
  • SGST (State GST) – goes to the State Government.

For a supply between two different states (inter-state), a single tax called IGST (Integrated GST) is charged at the full GST rate.

Input tax credit (ITC)

A dealer pays GST on the goods they purchase (input GST) and collects GST on the goods they sell (output GST). The input GST can be set off against the output GST. The amount actually paid to the government is the net GST = output GST − input GST.

C. Important Formulas

GST on a taxable value
\(\text{GST} = \dfrac{\text{Rate}}{100} \times \text{Taxable value}\)
Rate means the GST rate for that item (for example 5%, 12%, 18% or 28%).
Intra-state supply (CGST + SGST)
\(\text{CGST} = \text{SGST} = \dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{\text{Rate}}{100} \times \text{Taxable value}\right)\)
CGST and SGST are always equal; together they equal the full GST.
Inter-state supply (IGST)
\(\text{IGST} = \dfrac{\text{Rate}}{100} \times \text{Taxable value}\)
IGST is charged at the full rate by the Centre.
GST-inclusive price (reverse working)
\(\text{Taxable value} = \dfrac{\text{Price including GST}}{1 + \frac{\text{Rate}}{100}}\)
Then \(\text{GST} = \text{Price including GST} - \text{Taxable value}\).
Net GST payable (input tax credit)
\(\text{Net GST} = \text{Output GST (on sales)} - \text{Input GST (on purchases)}\)
For intra-state trade, work this out separately for CGST and for SGST.

D. Step-by-Step Solved Examples

Level 1 Direct GST on a taxable value

Example 1

A shop sells an item for a taxable value of ₹2,500. If the GST rate is 18%, find the GST charged and the total amount the customer pays.

Given: Taxable value = ₹2,500, GST rate = 18%.

Required: GST amount and total amount.

1Use \(\text{GST} = \dfrac{\text{Rate}}{100} \times \text{Taxable value}\).
2\(\text{GST} = \dfrac{18}{100} \times 2500 = 18 \times 25 = \text{₹}450\).
3Total amount = taxable value + GST = \(2500 + 450 = \text{₹}2{,}950\).

Answer: GST = ₹450; total amount = ₹2,950. Tip: check that the total is 1.18 times the taxable value.

Level 2 Intra-state supply and bill

Example 2

A dealer in Karnataka sells goods worth ₹4,000 (taxable value) to a customer in Karnataka. The GST rate is 12%. Find the CGST, the SGST and the total bill amount.

Given: Taxable value = ₹4,000, GST rate = 12%. The sale is within the same state, so it is split into CGST and SGST.

1Total GST = \(\dfrac{12}{100} \times 4000 = \text{₹}480\).
2CGST = half of the total GST = \(480 \div 2 = \text{₹}240\).
3SGST = other half = \(\text{₹}240\) (always equal to CGST).
4Total bill = \(4000 + 240 + 240 = \text{₹}4{,}480\).

Answer: CGST = ₹240, SGST = ₹240, total bill = ₹4,480.

Level 3 Input tax credit – net GST payable

Example 3

A trader in Maharashtra buys goods for ₹20,000 and pays GST at 18%. He sells the same goods for ₹30,000 (before tax), also within Maharashtra, at 18%. Find the input GST, the output GST and the net GST he must pay to the government.

Given: Purchase = ₹20,000, sale = ₹30,000, rate = 18%, both intra-state.

1Input GST (on purchase) = \(\dfrac{18}{100} \times 20000 = \text{₹}3{,}600\).
2Output GST (on sale) = \(\dfrac{18}{100} \times 30000 = \text{₹}5{,}400\).
3Net GST = output GST − input GST = \(5400 - 3600 = \text{₹}1{,}800\).

Answer: Input GST = ₹3,600; output GST = ₹5,400; net GST payable = ₹1,800. Check: net GST = 18% of the value added, i.e. 18% of ₹10,000 = ₹1,800.

E. Examination-Oriented Questions

Basic Q1

Find the GST on a taxable value of ₹6,400 at 12%.

Show answer

GST = \(\dfrac{12}{100} \times 6400 = \text{₹}768\).

Basic Q2

The price of an article including 18% GST is ₹5,900. Find the taxable value.

Show answer

Taxable value = \(\dfrac{5900}{1.18} = \text{₹}5{,}000\). (Then GST = \(5900 - 5000 = \text{₹}900\).)

Standard Q3

A dealer in Tamil Nadu sells goods for ₹12,000 within the state. GST is 28%. Find CGST, SGST and the amount payable by the buyer.

Show answer

Total GST = \(0.28 \times 12000 = \text{₹}3{,}360\). CGST = SGST = \(\text{₹}1{,}680\). Amount payable = \(12000 + 3360 = \text{₹}15{,}360\).

Standard Q4

A manufacturer sells to a wholesaler in another state for ₹40,000 (taxable value) at 18% GST. Find the IGST charged and the invoice total.

Show answer

Inter-state supply, so IGST = \(0.18 \times 40000 = \text{₹}7{,}200\). Invoice total = \(40000 + 7200 = \text{₹}47{,}200\).

Challenging Q5

A trader buys goods for ₹25,000 and pays 18% GST. He then sells them for ₹32,000 (before tax) at 18%, within the same state. Find the net CGST and net SGST he pays.

Show answer

Input GST = \(0.18 \times 25000 = \text{₹}4{,}500\) (CGST ₹2,250, SGST ₹2,250). Output GST = \(0.18 \times 32000 = \text{₹}5{,}760\) (CGST ₹2,880, SGST ₹2,880). Net CGST = \(2880 - 2250 = \text{₹}630\); net SGST = ₹630. Total net GST = ₹1,260.

Challenging Q6

The marked price of an article is ₹3,200. A shopkeeper offers a discount of 10% on the marked price, and then charges GST at 18% on the discounted price. Find the amount the customer pays.

Show answer

Discounted (taxable) value = \(3200 - 10\% \text{ of } 3200 = 3200 - 320 = \text{₹}2{,}880\). GST = \(0.18 \times 2880 = \text{₹}518.40\). Amount paid = \(2880 + 518.40 = \text{₹}3{,}398.40\).

F. Common Mistakes

#Common mistakeCorrect approach
1Charging CGST and SGST each at the full GST rate.CGST and SGST are each half the GST rate; together they equal the full rate.
2Charging IGST for a sale within the same state.Use IGST only for inter-state supply; intra-state uses CGST + SGST.
3Applying GST to the GST-inclusive price by multiplying by the rate.First find the taxable value as \(\frac{\text{inclusive price}}{1 + r/100}\), then find GST.
4Adding GST to the purchase and sale both and reporting the sum as tax paid.Use input tax credit: net GST = output GST − input GST.
5Forgetting to subtract a discount before computing GST.Discount is applied to the marked price first; GST is charged on the discounted value.
6Rounding the taxable value wrongly when working backwards.Keep full accuracy until the final rupee value; round money only at the end.
7Writing ₹ instead of the correct unit or leaving units out.State all money answers with the rupee sign and correct rounding to 2 decimals if needed.
8Treating CGST and SGST as payable to the same authority.CGST goes to the Centre and SGST to the State; the amounts are equal but credited separately.

G. Examination Tips

Scoring tips
  • Always write down "Given" and "To find" – ICSE awards marks for correct method, not just the final answer.
  • If a sale is "within the state", immediately write CGST = SGST = half of the GST rate; this earns method marks early.
  • For input tax credit, show output GST and input GST separately, then subtract. Do not just write the final number.
  • When a discount is involved, compute the discount first; GST applies to the price after discount.
  • Include units (₹) in every step. A correct number without the unit can lose a presentation mark.

H. Quick Revision Sheet – GST

Remember
  • GST = (Rate/100) × taxable value.
  • Intra-state: CGST = SGST = half of GST. Inter-state: full IGST.
  • Taxable value from inclusive price = inclusive ÷ (1 + rate/100).
  • Net GST = output GST − input GST.
  • Discount is applied before GST.

Topic 2 – Banking: Recurring Deposit Accounts

A. Learning Objectives

After studying this topic you should be able to:

  • Explain what a Recurring Deposit (RD) account is and how it differs from a fixed deposit.
  • Calculate the interest earned on a recurring deposit using the standard ICSE formula.
  • Find the maturity value of an RD account.
  • Work backwards to find the rate of interest or the number of instalments.
  • Solve problems involving monthly deposits, time periods and interest.

B. Basic Concepts and Definitions

Recurring Deposit (RD) account

A scheme in which a fixed sum of money is deposited every month for a fixed number of months. At the end of the period the depositor receives the total of all deposits plus the interest earned. It encourages regular saving.

Instalment (P)

The fixed amount deposited each month. If a person deposits ₹500 every month, then the instalment P = ₹500.

Maturity value

The total amount the depositor receives on maturity = total of all monthly deposits + interest earned. It is also called the maturity amount.

Interest calculation

Each monthly instalment earns interest for a different length of time. The first instalment stays in the bank for \(n\) months, the second for \(n-1\) months, and so on, down to the last instalment which earns interest for 1 month. Summing these gives the standard formula below.

C. Important Formulas

Interest on a recurring deposit
\(I = P \times \dfrac{n(n+1)}{2} \times \dfrac{r}{100} \times \dfrac{1}{12}\)
\(P\) = monthly instalment, \(n\) = number of months, \(r\) = rate of interest % per annum.
Maturity value
\(MV = (P \times n) + I\)
Here \(P \times n\) is the total of all the monthly deposits.
Sum of months
\(\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2} = 1 + 2 + 3 + \dots + n\)
This counts the number of "instalment-months" over which interest accrues.

D. Step-by-Step Solved Examples

Level 1 Interest and maturity value

Example 1

Mr. Sharma deposits ₹500 every month in a recurring deposit account for 24 months at 6% per annum. Find the interest earned and the maturity value.

Given: \(P = 500\), \(n = 24\), \(r = 6\).

1\(\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2} = \dfrac{24 \times 25}{2} = 300\).
2\(I = 500 \times 300 \times \dfrac{6}{100} \times \dfrac{1}{12} = 500 \times 300 \times 0.005 = \text{₹}750\).
3Total deposits = \(500 \times 24 = \text{₹}12{,}000\).
4Maturity value = \(12000 + 750 = \text{₹}12{,}750\).

Answer: Interest = ₹750; maturity value = ₹12,750.

Level 2 Larger period and rate

Example 2

Ms. Rao deposits ₹1,000 per month for 36 months at 8% per annum. Compute the interest and the maturity value.

Given: \(P = 1000\), \(n = 36\), \(r = 8\).

1\(\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2} = \dfrac{36 \times 37}{2} = 666\).
2\(I = 1000 \times 666 \times \dfrac{8}{1200} = \dfrac{1000 \times 666 \times 8}{1200} = \text{₹}4{,}440\).
3Total deposits = \(1000 \times 36 = \text{₹}36{,}000\).
4Maturity value = \(36000 + 4440 = \text{₹}40{,}440\).

Answer: Interest = ₹4,440; maturity value = ₹40,440.

Level 3 Comparing schemes / finding rate

Example 3

A person deposits ₹800 per month for 20 months and receives a total interest of ₹1,050. Find the rate of interest per annum.

Given: \(P = 800\), \(n = 20\), \(I = 1050\). Find \(r\).

1\(\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2} = \dfrac{20 \times 21}{2} = 210\).
2Use \(I = P \times \dfrac{n(n+1)}{2} \times \dfrac{r}{1200}\): \(1050 = 800 \times 210 \times \dfrac{r}{1200}\).
3\(1050 = \dfrac{168000\,r}{1200} = 140\,r\).
4\(r = \dfrac{1050}{140} = 7.5\).

Answer: The rate of interest is 7.5% per annum. Check: with r = 7.5, I = 800 × 210 × 7.5/1200 = ₹1,050.

E. Examination-Oriented Questions

Basic Q1

Find the interest on a recurring deposit of ₹400 per month for 12 months at 6% per annum.

Show answer

\(\frac{n(n+1)}{2} = \frac{12 \times 13}{2} = 78\). \(I = 400 \times 78 \times \frac{6}{1200} = \text{₹}156\).

Basic Q2

Mr. Iyer deposits ₹600 each month for 10 months at 9% per annum. Find the maturity value.

Show answer

\(\frac{10 \times 11}{2} = 55\). \(I = 600 \times 55 \times \frac{9}{1200} = \text{₹}247.50\). Total deposits = ₹6,000. MV = \(6000 + 247.50 = \text{₹}6{,}247.50\).

Standard Q3

A lady deposits ₹1,200 per month for 30 months at 8% per annum. Find the interest earned and the maturity value.

Show answer

\(\frac{30 \times 31}{2} = 465\). \(I = 1200 \times 465 \times \frac{8}{1200} = 1200 \times 465 \times \frac{1}{150} = \text{₹}3{,}720\). Total deposits = ₹36,000. MV = \(36000 + 3720 = \text{₹}39{,}720\).

Standard Q4

Find the number of months for which ₹500 per month must be deposited at 10% per annum to earn an interest of ₹1,250.

Show answer

\(1250 = 500 \times \frac{n(n+1)}{2} \times \frac{10}{1200}\). So \(\frac{n(n+1)}{2} = \frac{1250 \times 1200}{500 \times 10} = 300\). Then \(n(n+1) = 600 = 24 \times 25\), so \(n = 24\) months.

Challenging Q5

Ravi opens an RD account and deposits ₹1,500 per month for 2 years at 7% per annum. Find the total interest and the maturity value. How much more interest would he have earned at 8% per annum?

Show answer

\(n = 24\), \(\frac{24 \times 25}{2} = 300\). At 7%: \(I = 1500 \times 300 \times \frac{7}{1200} = \text{₹}2{,}625\). MV = \(36000 + 2625 = \text{₹}38{,}625\). At 8%: \(I = 1500 \times 300 \times \frac{8}{1200} = \text{₹}3{,}000\). Extra interest = \(3000 - 2625 = \text{₹}375\).

Challenging Q6

A recurring deposit account pays an interest of ₹6,090 on a monthly deposit of ₹2,000 at 9% per annum. Find the number of months and the maturity value.

Show answer

Use \(I = P \cdot \frac{n(n+1)}{2} \cdot \frac{r}{1200}\): \(6090 = 2000 \times \frac{n(n+1)}{2} \times \frac{9}{1200}\). So \(\frac{n(n+1)}{2} = \frac{6090 \times 1200}{2000 \times 9} = 406\). Then \(n(n+1) = 812 = 28 \times 29\), so \(n = 28\) months. Total deposits = \(2000 \times 28 = \text{₹}56{,}000\). Maturity value = \(56000 + 6090 = \text{₹}62{,}090\).

F. Common Mistakes

#Common mistakeCorrect approach
1Using the simple interest formula \(I = \frac{PRT}{100}\) with \(T = n\) months directly.Use the recurring deposit formula \(I = P \cdot \frac{n(n+1)}{2} \cdot \frac{r}{1200}\), which accounts for each instalment's own time.
2Forgetting the factor \(\frac{1}{12}\) to convert the annual rate to a monthly basis.The rate is per annum, so divide by 12 (equivalently use 1200 in the denominator).
3Using \(n(n+1)\) instead of \(\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\).The sum of the first \(n\) natural numbers is \(\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\).
4Reporting the total deposits as the maturity value.Maturity value = total deposits + interest.
5Taking the number of months as years.Convert years to months first (2 years = 24 months).
6Expecting the last instalment to earn interest for \(n\) months.The last instalment stays only 1 month; that is why the sum is \(\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\) and not \(n^2\).
7Rounding the interest to the nearest rupee too early.Keep the exact value through the working and round only at the final answer.
8Accepting a non-integer number of months as an answer.The number of instalments must be a whole number; check your result.

G. Examination Tips

Scoring tips
  • Write the formula first, then substitute. This secures method marks even if a calculation slips.
  • Compute \(\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\) as a separate step – it is easy to mark and prevents errors.
  • If the question asks for maturity value, always find interest first, then add total deposits.
  • Convert all times to months before substituting.
  • State the final answer clearly with the rupee sign and units (months, % per annum).

H. Quick Revision Sheet – Banking

Remember
  • \(I = P \times \frac{n(n+1)}{2} \times \frac{r}{1200}\) (with \(r\) in % per annum).
  • Maturity value = \(P\,n + I\).
  • Sum of months \(= \frac{n(n+1)}{2}\).
  • Convert years to months before using the formula.
  • \(n\) must always be a whole number of months.

Topic 3 – Shares and Dividends

A. Learning Objectives

After studying this topic you should be able to:

  • Explain what a share is and distinguish between its face value and market value.
  • Recognise when a share is bought at par, at a premium or at a discount.
  • Calculate the number of shares bought for a given investment.
  • Compute the annual dividend from a given rate of dividend on the face value.
  • Find the rate of return (yield) on an investment.
  • Solve problems on buying and selling shares, including profit or loss.

B. Basic Concepts and Definitions

Share

A share is a unit of ownership in a company. A person who buys shares is a shareholder and is entitled to a part of the company's profits, paid as a dividend.

Face value (FV) / Nominal value / Par value

The value printed on the share certificate, for example ₹10 or ₹100. Dividends are always calculated on the face value.

Market value (MV)

The price at which a share is actually bought or sold in the market. It can be above or below the face value.

  • MV > FV → bought/sold at a premium.
  • MV < FV → bought/sold at a discount.
  • MV = FV → bought/sold at par.
Dividend

The part of a company's profit paid to shareholders. A dividend of, say, 8% means 8% of the face value per share, per year – not 8% of the market value.

Rate of return (yield)

The annual income (dividend) expressed as a percentage of the money actually invested (market value paid).

C. Important Formulas

Number of shares bought
\(\text{Number of shares} = \dfrac{\text{Total investment}}{\text{Market value per share}}\)
Use the market value (the price actually paid), not the face value.
Annual dividend (income)
\(\text{Dividend} = \text{Number of shares} \times \dfrac{\text{Dividend rate}}{100} \times \text{Face value}\)
The dividend rate is applied to the face value.
Rate of return (yield)
\(\text{Rate of return} = \dfrac{\text{Total annual dividend}}{\text{Total investment}} \times 100\%\)
Investment is the total money spent buying the shares at market value.
Sale proceeds and profit/loss
\(\text{Sale proceeds} = \text{Number of shares} \times \text{Selling price}\)
Profit/loss = sale proceeds − purchase cost (investment).

D. Step-by-Step Solved Examples

Level 1 Dividend and yield at a premium

Example 1

A man buys 200 shares of face value ₹10 each at a market price of ₹25 per share. The company declares a dividend of 8%. Find his investment, his annual income and his rate of return.

Given: Number of shares = 200, FV = ₹10, MV = ₹25, dividend = 8%.

1Investment = \(200 \times 25 = \text{₹}5{,}000\).
2Dividend per share = \(8\% \text{ of } 10 = \text{₹}0.80\).
3Annual income = \(200 \times 0.80 = \text{₹}160\).
4Rate of return = \(\dfrac{160}{5000} \times 100\% = 3.2\%\).

Answer: Investment = ₹5,000; annual income = ₹160; rate of return = 3.2%.

Level 2 Higher face value and premium

Example 2

Mrs. Nair buys 500 shares of face value ₹100 each at ₹120 per share. The company pays a 9% dividend. Find the premium per share, her investment, her annual income and the rate of return.

Given: Number = 500, FV = ₹100, MV = ₹120, dividend = 9%.

1Premium = MV − FV = \(120 - 100 = \text{₹}20\) per share.
2Investment = \(500 \times 120 = \text{₹}60{,}000\).
3Dividend per share = \(9\% \text{ of } 100 = \text{₹}9\).
4Annual income = \(500 \times 9 = \text{₹}4{,}500\).
5Rate of return = \(\dfrac{4500}{60000} \times 100\% = 7.5\%\).

Answer: Premium = ₹20; investment = ₹60,000; income = ₹4,500; rate of return = 7.5%.

Level 3 Working backwards to the market value

Example 3

A man invests ₹27,000 in shares of face value ₹10 each. The company pays a 15% dividend and he receives ₹1,350 as dividend. Find the market value per share and his rate of return.

Given: Investment = ₹27,000, FV = ₹10, dividend = 15%, total dividend = ₹1,350.

1Dividend per share = \(15\% \text{ of } 10 = \text{₹}1.50\).
2Number of shares = \(\dfrac{1350}{1.50} = 900\).
3Market value per share = \(\dfrac{27000}{900} = \text{₹}30\).
4MV > FV, so the shares were bought at a premium of ₹20.
5Rate of return = \(\dfrac{1350}{27000} \times 100\% = 5\%\).

Answer: Market value = ₹30 per share (premium ₹20); rate of return = 5%.

E. Examination-Oriented Questions

Basic Q1

How many shares of market value ₹40 each can be bought for ₹8,000?

Show answer

Number of shares = \(\dfrac{8000}{40} = 200\).

Basic Q2

Find the annual dividend on 300 shares of face value ₹25 each when the company declares a dividend of 12%.

Show answer

Dividend per share = \(12\% \text{ of } 25 = \text{₹}3\). Annual dividend = \(300 \times 3 = \text{₹}900\).

Standard Q3

Mr. Khan invests ₹15,000 in shares of face value ₹10, buying them at ₹15 each. The dividend is 10%. Find his annual income and rate of return.

Show answer

Number = \(15000 \div 15 = 1000\). Dividend per share = \(10\% \text{ of } 10 = \text{₹}1\). Income = ₹1,000. Rate of return = \(\frac{1000}{15000} \times 100\% = 6.67\%\) (to 2 d.p.).

Standard Q4

A company's ₹100 share is available at ₹80. If the company declares a 12% dividend, find the rate of return.

Show answer

Dividend per share = \(12\% \text{ of } 100 = \text{₹}12\). Investment per share = ₹80. Rate of return = \(\frac{12}{80} \times 100\% = 15\%\). (MV < FV, so the share is at a discount.)

Challenging Q5

A man buys 400 shares of face value ₹50 at a premium of 20%. He receives a dividend of 14%. After a year, he sells all the shares at a price of ₹65 each. Find his net gain or loss (including dividend).

Show answer

Buying price = \(50 + 20\% \text{ of } 50 = 50 + 10 = \text{₹}60\). Investment = \(400 \times 60 = \text{₹}24{,}000\). Dividend per share = \(14\% \text{ of } 50 = \text{₹}7\); income = \(400 \times 7 = \text{₹}2{,}800\). Sale proceeds = \(400 \times 65 = \text{₹}26{,}000\). Net gain = \((26000 - 24000) + 2800 = \text{₹}4{,}800\).

Challenging Q6

An investor wants an annual income of ₹4,800 from shares of face value ₹10 on which a 12% dividend is paid. How much must he invest if the market value is ₹16 per share?

Show answer

Dividend per share = \(12\% \text{ of } 10 = \text{₹}1.20\). Number of shares needed = \(4800 \div 1.20 = 4000\). Investment = \(4000 \times 16 = \text{₹}64{,}000\).

F. Common Mistakes

#Common mistakeCorrect approach
1Calculating the dividend on the market value.The dividend rate is always applied to the face value.
2Using the face value to find the number of shares bought.Divide the investment by the market value per share.
3Confusing premium with dividend.Premium = MV − FV; dividend is the annual profit paid per share.
4Treating a discount as a loss on every purchase.Buying at a discount simply means MV < FV; it is not a loss unless sold below cost.
5Finding the rate of return on the face value.Rate of return = dividend ÷ actual investment (market value) × 100.
6Forgetting to add the dividend when computing total gain.Total gain = (sale proceeds − investment) + dividend received.
7Mixing up "at a premium of 20%" with "at 20% of market value".A premium of 20% means 20% of the face value is added to the face value.
8Leaving the final rate of return as a decimal.Express the rate of return as a percentage (multiply by 100).

G. Examination Tips

Scoring tips
  • Write "FV =" and "MV =" clearly at the start – many errors come from mixing them up.
  • Compute the dividend per share first; it is the key to both income and rate of return.
  • For "at a premium/discount" questions, convert the premium/discount to a rupee market value before doing anything else.
  • For gain/loss questions, show the investment, the sale proceeds and the dividend as three separate lines.
  • Round the rate of return to two decimal places and write the % sign.

H. Quick Revision Sheet – Shares and Dividends

Remember
  • Number of shares = investment ÷ MV.
  • Dividend = number of shares × (dividend rate % of FV).
  • Rate of return = (total dividend ÷ investment) × 100%.
  • Premium: MV > FV. Discount: MV < FV. Par: MV = FV.
  • Total gain = (sale proceeds − investment) + dividend.
