  2. Section A: Questions 1–8, one mark each (8 marks).
  3. Section B: Questions 9–12, two marks each (8 marks).
  4. Section C: Questions 13–16, three marks each (12 marks).
  5. Section D: Questions 17–19, four marks each (12 marks).
  6. All questions are compulsory. Write the full working wherever marks are awarded for method, not just the final answer.
  7. Use of a calculator is not permitted; questions that ask for roots to a given accuracy expect working by hand.

Detailed Solutions

Section A – Multiple Choice Questions

Question 1 – Answer: B (4)

Since 3 is a root, \(3^2 - 3p + 3 = 0\), so \(9 - 3p + 3 = 0\) and \(p = 4\).

Question 2 – Answer: B

Real and equal roots require the discriminant to be zero. Here \(b = -q\), so \(b^2 - 4ac = q^2 - 4pr = 0\), i.e. \(q^2 = 4pr\).

Question 3 – Answer: A (6)

Mean proportional \(m\) satisfies \(4 : m = m : 9\), so \(m^2 = 36\) and \(m = 6\).

Question 4 – Answer: A (5)

By the remainder theorem, remainder \(= P(1) = 1 - 2 + 1 + 5 = 5\).

Question 5 – Answer: A (20th)

\(a = 15\), \(d = 15\), so \(T_n = 15n\). Putting \(15n = 300\) gives \(n = 20\).

Question 6 – Answer: B

\(a = 15\), \(r = 2\), so \(T_n = 15 \times 2^{n-1}\).

Question 7 – Answer: B

\(2A = \begin{bmatrix}4 & 4 \\ 6 & 8\end{bmatrix}\), \(I = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}\). \(2A - I = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 4 \\ 6 & 7\end{bmatrix}\).

Question 8 – Answer: A (24)

\(5 : 8 = 15 : x\) gives \(5x = 8 \times 15 = 120\), so \(x = 24\).

Section B – Short Answer Questions

Question 9
Working

\(P(2) = 2^3 - 2k - 12 = 8 - 2k - 12 = -4 - 2k = 0\). Hence \(k = -2\).

\(k = -2\)
Question 10
Working

\(2x \le 10\), so \(x \le 5\). With \(x \in \mathbb{W}\), the solution set is \(\{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}\).

Solution set = \(\{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}\)
Question 11
Working

\(x^2 - 8x + 15 = (x - 3)(x - 5) = 0\).

\(x = 3\) or \(x = 5\)
Question 12
Working

Split into \(-3 < 2x + 1\) and \(2x + 1 \le 9\). Subtract 1: \(-4 < 2x \le 8\). Divide by 2: \(-2 < x \le 4\).

\(-2 < x \le 4\)

Section C – Application Questions

Question 13
Method

Use the quadratic formula, keeping the surd form until the last step.

\(a = 1, b = 4, c = -8\). \(D = 16 + 32 = 48\).

\(x = \dfrac{-4 \pm \sqrt{48}}{2} = -2 \pm 2\sqrt{3} \approx 1.464 \text{ or } -5.464\).

\(x = 1.5\) or \(x = -5.5\) (to 1 d.p.)
Question 14
Method

Write two equations in \(a\) and \(d\), solve, then use the sum formula.

\(a + 4d = 4\) and \(a + 8d = -12\). Subtracting: \(4d = -16\), so \(d = -4\) and \(a = 20\).

\(S_{16} = \dfrac{16}{2}\big[2(20) + 15(-4)\big] = 8(40 - 60) = -160\).

\(a = 20\); \(d = -4\); \(S_{16} = -160\)
Question 15
Working

\(2A = \begin{bmatrix}4 & 6 \\ 8 & 10\end{bmatrix}\), \(3I = \begin{bmatrix}3 & 0 \\ 0 & 3\end{bmatrix}\).

\(2A - 3I = \begin{bmatrix}4 - 3 & 6 - 0 \\ 8 - 0 & 10 - 3\end{bmatrix}\).

\(\begin{bmatrix}1 & 6 \\ 8 & 7\end{bmatrix}\)
Question 16
Working

Continued proportion: \(2 : x = x : 18\), so \(x^2 = 36\). Since \(x\) is positive, \(x = 6\).

\(x = 6\)

Section D – Structured / Long Answer Questions

Question 17
Method

Add \(B + C\), multiply by \(A\) row × column, then subtract \(14I\).

\(B + C = \begin{bmatrix}5 & 3 \\ 3 & 9\end{bmatrix}\).

\(A(B + C) = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ 2 & 4\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}5 & 3 \\ 3 & 9\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}14 & 30 \\ 22 & 42\end{bmatrix}\).

\(A(B + C) - 14I = \begin{bmatrix}14 & 30 \\ 22 & 42\end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix}14 & 0 \\ 0 & 14\end{bmatrix}\).

\(\begin{bmatrix}0 & 30 \\ 22 & 28\end{bmatrix}\)
Question 18
Method

Take cube roots, then cross-multiply and simplify to a ratio.

\(\dfrac{a + b}{a - b} = \dfrac{\sqrt[3]{64}}{\sqrt[3]{27}} = \dfrac{4}{3}\).

\(3(a + b) = 4(a - b)\) gives \(3a + 3b = 4a - 4b\), so \(7b = a\).

\(a : b = 7 : 1\)
Question 19
Method

Use the factor theorem to find \(k\), then divide and factorise fully.

\(P(1) = 3 + 8 - 15 + k = -4 + k = 0\), so \(k = 4\).

Polynomial: \(3x^3 + 8x^2 - 15x + 4 = (x - 1)(3x^2 + 11x - 4)\).

\(3x^2 + 11x - 4 = (3x - 1)(x + 4)\).

\(k = 4\); complete factorisation \((x - 1)(3x - 1)(x + 4)\)
