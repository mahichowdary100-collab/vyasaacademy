General Instructions

  1. The question paper has 19 questions divided into four sections, carrying a total of 40 marks.
  2. Section A: Questions 1–8, one mark each (8 marks).
  3. Section B: Questions 9–12, two marks each (8 marks).
  4. Section C: Questions 13–16, three marks each (12 marks).
  5. Section D: Questions 17–19, four marks each (12 marks).
  6. All questions are compulsory. Write the full working wherever marks are awarded for method, not just the final answer.
  7. Use of a calculator is not permitted. For the constructions question, use only a ruler and a pair of compasses and show all construction arcs.

Detailed Solutions

Section A – Multiple Choice Questions

Question 1 – Answer: D (\(50^\circ\))

Tangents from an external point are equal, so \(PS = PT\) and \(\angle PST = \angle PTS\). In \(\triangle PST\), \(2\angle PTS = 180^\circ - 80^\circ = 100^\circ\), so \(\angle PTS = 50^\circ\). Since \(SQ \parallel PT\) with \(ST\) as transversal, alternate angles are equal: \(\angle QST = \angle PTS = 50^\circ\).

Question 2 – Answer: A (\(2 : 3\))

For similar figures, the ratio of areas equals the square of the ratio of corresponding sides. \(\left(\dfrac{s_1}{s_2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{4}{9}\), so \(\dfrac{s_1}{s_2} = \dfrac{2}{3}\).

Question 3 – Answer: A (\(6\text{ cm}\))

By the basic proportionality theorem, \(\dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{AE}{EC}\), i.e. \(\dfrac{2.5}{5} = \dfrac{3}{EC}\). Thus \(EC = \dfrac{5 \times 3}{2.5} = 6\text{ cm}\).

Question 4 – Answer: A (perpendicular bisector of \(AB\))

A point equidistant from \(A\) and \(B\) lies on the perpendicular bisector of segment \(AB\).

Question 5 – Answer: A (\(55^\circ\))

The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference on the same arc. A point on the major arc sees the chord via the minor arc, so the angle is \(\dfrac{110^\circ}{2} = 55^\circ\).

Question 6 – Answer: A (\(112^\circ\))

In a cyclic quadrilateral, opposite angles are supplementary: \(\angle A + \angle C = 180^\circ\), so \(\angle C = 180^\circ - 68^\circ = 112^\circ\).

Question 7 – Answer: D (\(90^\circ\))

By the alternate segment theorem, the angle between the tangent \(RT\) and the chord \(PS\) equals the angle in the alternate segment: \(\angle PQS = \angle PST = 30^\circ\).

In \(\triangle PQS\), \(\angle PSQ = 180^\circ - 60^\circ - 30^\circ = 90^\circ\).

Question 8 – Answer: A (\(12\text{ cm}\))

For two chords intersecting inside a circle, \(PA \times PB = PC \times PD\). So \(6 \times 8 = 4 \times PD\), giving \(PD = 12\text{ cm}\).

Section B – Short Answer Questions

Question 9
Working

By the basic proportionality theorem, \(\dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{AE}{EC}\): \(\dfrac{4}{x} = \dfrac{6}{9}\). So \(6x = 36\).

\(x = 6\)
Question 10
Working

Ratio of areas \(= \left(\dfrac{AB}{DE}\right)^2 = \left(\dfrac{5}{3}\right)^2 = \dfrac{25}{9}\). So \(\dfrac{\text{area}(ABC)}{18} = \dfrac{25}{9}\), giving area \((ABC) = 18 \times \dfrac{25}{9} = 50\text{ cm}^2\).

Area \(= 50\text{ cm}^2\)
Question 11
Working

By the tangent–secant theorem, \(PT^2 = PA \times PB\). So \(12^2 = 8 \times PB\), giving \(PB = \dfrac{144}{8} = 18\text{ cm}\).

\(PB = 18\text{ cm}\)
Question 12
Working

The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord, so half-chord \(= 12\text{ cm}\). By Pythagoras, \(d = \sqrt{13^2 - 12^2} = \sqrt{169 - 144} = \sqrt{25}\).

Distance \(= 5\text{ cm}\)

Section C – Application Questions

Question 13
Method

Use the angle in a semicircle, then the angle sum of a triangle.

Since \(AB\) is a diameter, the angle in the semicircle is a right angle: \(\angle ACB = 90^\circ\).

In \(\triangle ABC\), \(\angle BAC = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 35^\circ = 55^\circ\).

\(\angle ACB = 90^\circ\); \(\angle BAC = 55^\circ\)
Question 14
Method

Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary.

\(\angle B + \angle D = 180^\circ\), so \(3x + 2x = 180^\circ\), giving \(5x = 180^\circ\) and \(x = 36\).

Thus \(\angle B = 3 \times 36 = 108^\circ\) and \(\angle D = 2 \times 36 = 72^\circ\).

\(x = 36\); \(\angle B = 108^\circ\); \(\angle D = 72^\circ\)
Question 15
Method

A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.

\(\angle OAP = \angle OBP = 90^\circ\). In quadrilateral \(OAPB\), \(\angle AOB = 360^\circ - 90^\circ - 90^\circ - 70^\circ = 110^\circ\).

In \(\triangle OAB\), \(OA = OB\) (radii), so \(\angle OAB = \angle OBA = \dfrac{180^\circ - 110^\circ}{2} = 35^\circ\).

\(\angle AOB = 110^\circ\); \(\angle OAB = 35^\circ\)
Question 16
Method

Use the intersecting chords theorem for two chords meeting inside a circle.

\(PA \times PB = PC \times PD\), so \(5 \times 12 = 3 \times PD\), giving \(PD = \dfrac{60}{3} = 20\text{ cm}\).

\(CD = PC + PD = 3 + 20 = 23\text{ cm}\).

\(PD = 20\text{ cm}\); \(CD = 23\text{ cm}\)

Section D – Structured / Long Answer Questions

Question 17
Construction

Draw \(BC = 5\text{ cm}\). With centre \(B\) and radius \(4\text{ cm}\), and centre \(C\) and radius \(6\text{ cm}\), draw arcs meeting at \(A\). Join \(AB\) and \(AC\).

Construct the perpendicular bisectors of \(AB\) and \(BC\); let them meet at \(O\). With centre \(O\) and radius \(OA = OB = OC\), draw the circumcircle.

Measuring the radius \(OA\) from the drawing gives approximately \(3.0\text{ cm}\). (Check: \(R = \dfrac{abc}{4\Delta} = \dfrac{4 \times 5 \times 6}{4 \times 9.92} \approx 3.0\text{ cm}\).)

Circumcircle drawn; circumradius \(\approx 3.0\text{ cm}\)
Question 18
Method

Find the ratio of the areas of \(\triangle ADE\) and \(\triangle ABC\), then subtract.

\(AD : DB = 2 : 3\) means \(AD : AB = 2 : 5\). Since \(DE \parallel BC\), \(\triangle ADE \sim \triangle ABC\), so \(\dfrac{\text{area}(ADE)}{\text{area}(ABC)} = \left(\dfrac{2}{5}\right)^2 = \dfrac{4}{25}\).

\(\text{area}(ABC) = 16 \times \dfrac{25}{4} = 100\text{ cm}^2\).

\(\text{area}(DBCE) = 100 - 16 = 84\text{ cm}^2\).

Area of \(DBCE = 84\text{ cm}^2\)
Question 19
Proof

A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact, so \(\angle OAP = \angle OBP = 90^\circ\). In quadrilateral \(OAPB\), the angles sum to \(360^\circ\): \(\angle APB + \angle AOB = 360^\circ - 90^\circ - 90^\circ = 180^\circ\).

Given \(\angle APB = 65^\circ\), then \(\angle AOB = 180^\circ - 65^\circ = 115^\circ\).

In \(\triangle OAB\), \(OA = OB\) (radii), so \(\angle OAB = \dfrac{180^\circ - 115^\circ}{2} = 32.5^\circ\).

\(\angle APB + \angle AOB = 180^\circ\); \(\angle OAB = 32.5^\circ\)
