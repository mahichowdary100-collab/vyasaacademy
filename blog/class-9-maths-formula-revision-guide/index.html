Class 9 Mathematics rewards students who keep formulas alive rather than memorising them once. This guide collects the formulas and key properties of the year in one place, chapter by chapter, with the units and the quick checks that catch slips. Use it to build recall pages, to compare against the revision strategy, and as a reference when practising from the study material.

How to use this guide

Do not read the tables cold. The proven routine is recall-first: write down everything you remember for a chapter on a blank sheet, then open this guide and mark what was missing. Missing items are the day's lesson. This is the exact recall step explained in how to study Class 9 Maths effectively.

Units that catch marks

Half the mark loss on formula questions is a units error:

  • Lengths, distances and perimeters: m, cm.
  • Areas: m², cm². If the question gives lengths in metres, the area is in square metres.
  • Volumes and capacities: m³, cm³.
  • Probability: no unit at all – it is a ratio between 0 and 1.
  • Money: rupees (₹), written with the amount.

Name the quantity before solving. A student who writes "need: area" starts with the right units and the right formula automatically. The common mistakes guide lists this habit in full.

Verification checks for every formula

Every formula above has a cheap check using small numbers. Teach the checks together with the formula:

  • Identity check: (2 + 3)² = 25 against 4 + 12 + 9 = 25. Any mismatch means a wrong expansion.
  • Distance check: from (1, 2) to (4, 6) gives √(9 + 16) = 5. A distance that looks much larger than the graph, or negative, is wrong.
  • Area check: a 3-4-5 triangle has area 6 by both ½bh and Heron's formula – they must agree.
  • Solids check: cylinder with r = 7 and h = 10 gives curved area 2 × (22/7) × 7 × 10 = 440 and volume (22/7) × 49 × 10 = 1540. Both round, both plausible.
  • Probability check: any answer below 0 or above 1 is impossible, and a probability of 1 suggests the event was read as certain.

The remembering system

A guide is only useful if its contents survive the exam hall. Three habits keep the formulas usable:

  1. Produce, then check. Use this guide the way the revision strategy prescribes: recall pages first, guide second.
  2. Use the formula in a story. Formula + a lived context beats formula alone. The competency questions article builds exactly those context links.
  3. Stage the slips. Each wrong formula from a practice set goes into the personal slip log with the correct version beside it. A short list of personal formula slips is worth more than the full table.

Intersperse these habits with the practice sets in the weekly plan so formulas are repaired while still fresh.

Frequently asked questions

Is it enough to memorise the tables?

No. A memorised formula with no unit discipline or verification check still loses marks. The tables give the tool; the checks and units in this guide keep it correct under pressure.

How often should a student review the formulas?

Weekly while a chapter is current, then every week in the rotation review. The Sunday review in the study plan is the natural slot.

Should students write formulas on a sheet during the exam?

Only from memory, as a rough recall page in the first few minutes – exams allow no notes, so a quickly written recall page acts as the student's own reference.

Which formulas are most often forgotten?

Heron's semi-perimeter, the cone's slant height l, the difference between curved and total surface area, and the sign inside an identity such as (a − b)². These four repay extra attention.

Conclusion

This guide is the complete Class 9 Mathematics formula map: coordinates, geometry, numbers, identities, measurement, probability, sequences and solids. Use it recall-first, pair every formula with its units and its small-number check, and repair slips weekly through the revision system.
