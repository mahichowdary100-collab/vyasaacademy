How to Score 90% or Higher in CBSE Class 10 Board Exams

A 90 percent result is a target many Class 10 students set, and the honest framing is that these strategies help students work toward it. No method guarantees a percentage, but the habits below reliably remove the soft marks that keep capable students below their target. The logic is simple: secure everything you already know, then build one layer of skill on top.

Score the paper, not just the subject

The board shares sample papers and the marking scheme, and a student who reads both learns the shape of marks before writing a line. Notice how step marks work in mathematics, how definitions are phrased in science, and how formats are checked in languages. Preparation aimed at how marks are distributed beats equal effort everywhere.

Layer 1: secure the easy marks first

  1. Definitions and formulas learned word-for-word from the textbook.
  2. Diagrams practised until they are clean without help.
  3. Numericals solved with working shown, even the easy ones.
  4. Formats for letters and essays rehearsed to standard.
  5. Units, signs and labels checked in the final minutes.

Most marks lost by average students are lost here, in the marks they already knew. A student who stops losing them is already near the bracket before attempting a single hard topic.

Layer 2: turn the error log into your syllabus

Keep one running list of every mark you have lost in school tests and practice papers. Sort the list into three kinds: concept gaps, careless slips, and presentation problems. Work the list from the top every single study session. The reason this outperforms a random revision plan is that it spends time exactly where your personal marks leak.

Layer 3: answer in the markable shape

The full method lives in our answer-writing guide, but the core is three habits: underline the verb, match length to mark value, and finish with the boxed answer and unit. A five-mark question answered in two lines loses marks no matter how perfect the two lines are.

Layer 4: rehearse full papers, honestly

From two months out, sit one full paper per subject every week in exam conditions, then mark it as strictly as an examiner would. The forty minutes of honest marking afterwards is the most productive time of the week. Students who rehearse the full paper stop losing marks to pace, to panic, and to the last-fifteen-minutes rush.

Protect the last fifteen minutes

Keep a personal rule that the paper must be finished fifteen minutes early, and spend that time only on checks: units, signs, labels, and unanswered questions. In the boards, the last fifteen minutes are where slips get caught and borderline marks are rescued. Train this rule in every practice paper so it runs automatically in the hall.

Balance effort across the three demanding subjects

Mathematics and science carry the heaviest preparation load, so they need the planned rhythm described in the complete study plan. A student who reaches the bracket usually reaches it with balanced strength, not with one brilliant subject and two neglected ones. Keep social science and languages alive in shorter blocks so no subject decays into panic in the final month.

Work toward higher scores, not perfection

The point of every strategy is progress, not perfection. Secure easy marks, correct the logged leaks, answer in the markable shape, and rehearse under exam conditions. Students who run these four layers steadily tend to move their scores toward the bracket they are aiming for, and what matters is giving yourself the best honest chance.

Frequently asked questions

Can these strategies guarantee 90 percent?

No method can guarantee a percentage. The strategies are designed to remove the soft marks that capable students lose, which gives every student a realistic chance to work toward the bracket they are capable of reaching.

Which marks matter most for a high score?

The easy marks: definitions, formulas, diagrams, formats, units and signs. Most students lose the most marks on questions they already knew, so securing those comes before attempting any hard topic.

How often should I practise full papers?

From two months before the boards, one complete paper per subject every week, in exam timing, marked as strictly as an examiner would. The honest marking time afterwards is where most of the improvement happens.

Is it enough to focus only on mathematics and science?

No. Consistent moderate effort in social science and languages protects the total, because a high bracket needs balanced strength. Neglected lighter subjects leak marks exactly in the final month.

What should I do in the last fifteen minutes of a paper?

Train to finish fifteen minutes early and use that window only for checks: units, signs, labels and any unanswered questions. This habit catches slips and rescues borderline marks in every subject.
