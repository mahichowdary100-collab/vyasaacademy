How to Manage Time During Biology Exams

By Vyasa Academy | 26 Aug 2026 | 2 min read

On this page

  • Budget minutes by marks
  • Use a two-pass approach
  • Give diagrams a time limit
  • Keep long answers proportionate
  • Reserve a review buffer
  • Train the budget in mocks
  • The close

Biology papers rarely fail from impossible questions. They fail when a student spends twenty minutes perfecting one answer and then sprints the rest, losing the easy marks along the way. The protection is a budget decided before the exam, not during it.

Budget minutes by marks

Allocate your working time roughly in proportion to marks, with a buffer left for the final review. Write the budget on the cover page before you begin. When a question is worth few marks and starts eating several minutes, the budget tells you to move on. The mark-based approach is the same logic shown in our mathematics time guide; only the paper is different.

Use a two-pass approach

  1. First pass: every question you can answer immediately, in order of your confidence.
  2. Second pass: the remaining questions, using your structural answer shape.
  3. Final minutes: the check pass, scanning for spelling, unlabelled parts and missed verbs.

The first pass banks safe marks early, which settles nerves and guarantees a floor. Most time disasters come from attacking the paper strictly top to bottom.

Give diagrams a time limit

A labelled figure should take under two minutes for the core set. Practise drawing them at speed before the exam so a diagram never becomes a time sink in the hall. If a sketch is still messy after two minutes, move on and return in the second pass. The practised figures come from our diagram value guide.

Keep long answers proportionate

Write according to the marks. A two-mark question wants a tight definition and one support; a five-mark question wants the structure, not a five-minute essay. Answer length that matches mark value keeps the whole paper on schedule and the marker happy.

Reserve a review buffer

Keep the last minutes for the check list: term spelling, straight label lines, every part of two-part questions answered, and arrows pointing the right way. A ten-minute check pass routinely rescues marks that a fast finish loses.

Train the budget in mocks

A budget written only on paper day is hope, not a plan. Run it in every mock: write the numbers, obey the passes, respect the time limits. Within a few timed papers the rhythm becomes automatic and the real exam feels like another rehearsal.

The close

Time in a biology paper is allocated in advance. Budget by marks, run two passes, cap the diagrams, match length to value, and keep a review buffer. Do those and the paper finishes balanced instead of rushed.
