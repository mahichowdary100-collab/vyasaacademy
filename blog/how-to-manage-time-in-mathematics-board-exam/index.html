How to Manage Time in a Mathematics Board Exam

By Mahendra Babu Chennupati | 26 Aug 2026 | 2 min read

On this page

  • Budget minutes by marks, not by order
  • Three passes through the paper
  • A hard limit per question
  • Leave a buffer to check like an examiner
  • Train the budget in mocks
  • The bottom line

Almost every student who runs out of time in a maths paper lost the time to pacing, not to difficulty. They spent twenty minutes on a question worth four marks and then rushed the easy ones. A simple budget fixed before the exam solves this.

Budget minutes by marks, not by order

Count only the minutes you allocate, about one and a half minutes per mark for the working time left after setting aside a buffer. Write your budget on the cover page: section A by the clock, section B by the clock, and so on. The budget is not optional advice; it is the plan you created when calm, and calm plans beat panicked choices.

Three passes through the paper

  1. First pass: answer everything you know immediately.
  2. Second pass: attempt everything you know you can do with some work, with method visible.
  3. Third pass: attempt the remaining questions with partial working, then use the leftover minutes to check signs.

The first pass banks easy marks early, which settles nerves and guarantees a floor score. Most time disasters come from treating questions in printed order.

A hard limit per question

Set a personal red line: if a question has eaten its marks-worth of minutes, mark it and move on. You can return after the first pass, when the mind is freer and the easy marks are safe. Students who obey the red line finish more of the paper almost every time.

Leave a buffer to check like an examiner

  • Reserve the last five minutes for sign and bracket scanning across the whole paper.
  • Spot-check one reworked answer to confirm the method, not the arithmetic.
  • Verify units and final statements on word problems.
  • A tidy paper scans fast, which is why neat layout returns time on the final pass.

Train the budget in mocks

A budget written on paper day but never practised is just hope. Run it in every mock: write the budget, follow the passes, obey the red line. Within three mocks the routine becomes automatic, and the real exam feels like another rehearsal.

The bottom line

Time in a maths paper is a resource you allocate, in advance, calmly. Budget by marks, run three passes, respect the red line, and check like an examiner in the final minutes. Do those and the paper finishes you instead of the other way around.
