10 Common Maths Mistakes Students Make (And How to Fix Them)

By Venu Chennupati | 25 Aug 2026 | 2 min read

  • 2. Not rewriting the question in symbols
  • 3. Ignoring units and the final answer box
  • 4. Plugging values into the wrong identity
  • 5. Rushing the last three minutes
  • 6. Practising without a timing plan
  • 7. Avoiding the chapters you are weak in

  • 9. Letting one hard question eat your time

After years of teaching maths to CBSE and ICSE students, one pattern is undeniable: a small set of mistakes repeats across students, classes, and papers. Fix these ten, and you will see marks climb faster than with any new book or video.

1. Skipping the rough work discipline

Sloppy rough work on the side of the page causes most sign errors and misread digits. Do rough work in a neat column, one step per line, so you can actually check it.

2. Not rewriting the question in symbols

Word problems punish students who save ink. Translate the question into a small diagram, a table, or an equation before touching numbers. The few seconds spent on this prevent the biggest category of wrong answers.

3. Ignoring units and the final answer box

A correct calculation with a missing unit is a lost mark. Box the final answer, write the unit, and underline the quantity the question asked for. Marks are also given for presentation, not just the result.

4. Plugging values into the wrong identity

Students memorise identities but forget the variable names change. Write the identity in symbols first, then substitute. If (a+b) squared becomes (x-3) squared, keep the pattern visible.

5. Rushing the last three minutes

The final minutes cause more silly errors than the entire paper. Reserve five minutes to check signs and signs only: plus/minus, brackets, and powers across the whole answer sheet. Do not re-solve anything. Just scan for sign changes.

6. Practising without a timing plan

Students who practise in unlimited time panic in timed exams. Divide the paper into sections with a time budget before you start, and rehearse that budget in every mock paper.

7. Avoiding the chapters you are weak in

It feels good to revise trigonometry when you are good at it. The marks are in the chapters you avoid. Give your weakest chapter ten extra minutes every single day for three weeks and the gap closes fast.

8. Copying steps without understanding them

Copying a solution feels like learning and is not. After you copy a method, close the book and reproduce it from scratch. If you cannot, you have found a real gap to fix in your notes.

9. Letting one hard question eat your time

A stuck question is a time trap. Mark it, move on, and return only if every other attempt is done. Missing three easy 3-mark questions to attempt one 5-mark question is a bad trade in every exam.

10. Never reviewing marked papers

The paper you got back is your best teacher. Go through every red mark and sort it into concept gap, calculation slip, or presentation issue. Then fix the biggest category first.
