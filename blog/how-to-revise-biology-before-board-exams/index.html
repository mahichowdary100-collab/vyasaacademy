How to Revise Biology Before Board Exams

By Vyasa Academy | 28 Aug 2026 | 2 min read

On this page

  • The shape of the month
  • Write recall pages instead of notes
  • Run the term cards every day
  • Do figure sprints
  • Rewrite the model answers
  • Protect the final days
  • The close

Biology is the subject where revision plans fail most often, because rereading feels productive and stores almost nothing. A working revision month switches to recall: pages written from memory, terms tested by cards, figures drawn cold, and key answers rewritten. Here is that month, week by week.

The shape of the month

  1. Week one: full coverage pass, one recall page per chapter.
  2. Week two: target pass, fix the topics that leaked from every recall page.
  3. Week three: paper rhythm, one previous-year paper every two days under time.
  4. Week four: recall only, term cards, figure sprints, and the leak list.

Write recall pages instead of notes

Close the book and write everything you remember about a chapter on a blank page: terms, processes, figures, and examples. Then compare with the text and add the gaps in a different colour. The gaps are the only content needing your time. A chapter recalled twice is a chapter known; a chapter read twice is a chapter merely recognised.

Run the term cards every day

Keep the full glossary and your term notebooks as a card deck. In the final month, run the deck daily until every card answers instantly. Drop a card only when you have answered it correctly several times in a row, and keep the missed ones at the top. The flashcard method lives with the other core techniques in our study techniques guide.

Do figure sprints

Time box each core figure to two minutes: draw, label, move on. A sprint a day keeps the whole figure list warm. This is the drawing discipline from our diagram value guide, but at exam speed.

Rewrite the model answers

Pick the ten highest-value answers from your chapters and rewrite each from memory. Compare against the textbook shape and fix the drift. Rewriting trains the actual exam action, which is producing clean written answers, not recognising concepts.

Protect the final days

In the last few days, stop fresh topics. Recall, top up the leak list, keep sleep regular, and let the stored material consolidate. A rested student delivers recall cleanly; a exhausted one forgets under pressure, and that is the most avoidable loss in biology.

The close

Revision is the difference between having studied biology and being ready for the paper. Write recall pages, run the term cards, sprint the figures, and rewrite the model answers. Follow the four-week shape and the boards become a rehearsal you have already performed.
