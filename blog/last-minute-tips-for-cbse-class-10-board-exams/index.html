Last-Minute Tips for CBSE Class 10 Board Exams

Everything that can be learned has been learned by the last 48 hours; the final stretch is about arriving sharp and staying calm. These last-minute tips protect the knowledge you already have instead of replacing it with panic.

The last 48 hours are for calm recall, not new work

Something new learned in the final two days rarely survives to the paper, and it can crowd out what the paper will actually ask. Spend the window on recall: summaries, memory lists, the error log. Anything fragile has already decided its fate; keeping the brain rested is the only real task now.

The day before a paper

  1. Revise only the one-page summaries and the memory lists for that subject.
  2. Retell the three hardest ideas in your own words, aloud.
  3. Run the formats or diagrams once more for that subject.
  4. Stop studying at a fixed, early hour.
  5. Pack the admit card, pens and the exam station tonight.

The evening belongs to calm preparation of the bag and the body, never to a fresh chapter.

The morning of the paper

  • Wake with enough margin to eat and travel without rush.
  • Loose-review one page: formulas or definitions, nothing heavy.
  • Leave home on time; lateness is the one stress you can fully prevent.
  • Enter the building breathing normally, not rehearsing notes under your breath.

The morning should feel routine, because boards are routine once you have run mocks for months.

The first ten minutes in the hall

  1. Read the whole paper once, calmly.
  2. Underline the verbs of the questions you know instantly.
  3. Mark the order you decided at home on the question paper.
  4. Start with the easy and known questions to bank time and confidence.

The first ten minutes set the tempo of the whole paper; the plan for them comes from our time management guide.

What to leave at home

  • Notes to your classmates; they worry you and they are useless there.
  • Any extra book; one page of recall is enough for the seat.
  • A phone.
  • Anything the exam room rules do not allow; read those rules in advance and follow them.

Every risk you remove at the door is a minute of calm you keep in the hall.

The sleep decision

There is no revision worth losing the night sleep for. A rested brain recalls faster and thinks more clearly under time, and the paper rewards exactly that. If you have to choose between one more hour of reading and one more hour of sleep, sleep is the better study tool.

The anxious-moment drill

If nerves spike in the hall, stop, breathe slowly for four counts, and return to the first easy question. The drill is the same one rehearsed in focus training: stop, breathe, restart small. Students who practised the drill in mocks walk through the spike without losing the paper.

The honest close

The last 48 hours protect what the year built: calm recall, an early stop, a packed bag, and a known first-ten-minutes plan. Boards reward the rested and the rehearsed. Trust the preparation and let it perform.
