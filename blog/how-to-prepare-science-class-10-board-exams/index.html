How to Prepare Science for Class 10 Board Exams

By Vyasa Academy | 28 Aug 2026 | 2 min read

The Class 10 Science board paper is designed to reward students who know the NCERT and can perform under time. The plan below turns that into a working rhythm, from the first reading to the final mock. It covers all three branches because the paper asks for all three in one sitting.

Step 1: anchor on the NCERT and the syllabus

Print the official syllabus and tick every topic as you finish it. Read the NCERT chapter once completely, then read the second time asking, for every paragraph, how would the paper test this. The board leans on NCERT wording, so collect the definitions exactly as the textbook writes them.

Step 2: physics by numerical and derivation rhythm

Class 10 physics answers live or die on method: given values, formula, substitution with units, answer. Practise the light ray diagram set and the circuit set until they are hand-fast. A weekly timed numerical set keeps the speed where the paper needs it.

Step 3: chemistry by reaction bank

Build one page of named reactions per chapter from the NCERT, each with its balanced equation and conditions. Learn the family rule behind each set so you can write fresh examples under exam stress. Writing the bank from memory once a week makes the reactions automatic.

Step 4: biology by process plus figure

For every biology chapter, learn the process as a flowchart, attach the glossary terms to its steps, and keep the required figures in a rotation. The term-figure pair is what the marks ask for and what memory-only revision misses. The branch-level detail sits in our branch preparation guide.

Step 5: keep the practical component alive

Know the aim, setup, observation and conclusion of each required practical, and keep the practical record orderly. Practical marks are easy marks when the record is clean and the principles are understood, exactly the system in our practical guide.

Step 6: a twelve-week shape with four phases

  1. Weeks 1-4: subject-wise first pass of the entire syllabus, with daily small tasks.
  2. Weeks 5-8: second pass, chapter-wise questions and error log building.
  3. Weeks 9-11: previous-year papers under time, branch-sorted and then whole.
  4. Week 12: recall-only revision, figure bank rotation, and final mock.

If twelve weeks are not available, compress: keep every phase but shrink the time per phase proportionally.

Step 7: mocks under exam conditions

At least four full papers, in the board timing, at a desk, with no phone in reach. Mark them honestly, log every loss, and the error log becomes the revision plan for the last days. This is the same mock discipline the general board plan applies across subjects in our board strategy article.

The final note

Science for the Class 10 board is won by the plan, not by the last-week cram. Anchor on the NCERT, train each branch distinctly, keep the practicals alive, and take honest mocks. Run the twelve-week shape and the paper stops being a mystery.
