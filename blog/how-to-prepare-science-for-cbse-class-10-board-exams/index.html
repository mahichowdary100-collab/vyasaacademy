How to Prepare Science for CBSE Class 10 Board Exams

A Class 10 Science paper mixes definitions, explanations, numericals, diagrams and activity-based questions. Students who prepare the content but not the question types lose marks in every category except the one they know. This guide tackles the paper by its question types, then balances the three branches into a weekly rhythm.

Know the kinds of questions the paper asks

  • Recall: define, name, state, one-line answers from the textbook.
  • Explain: why and how questions that test understanding.
  • Numerical: physics and chemistry calculations with working.
  • Diagram: labelled figures from any of the three branches.
  • Activity or practical: knowledge of experiments, observations and conclusions.
  • Recall of reactions and processes: balanced equations, life-process steps and conditions.

Preparing for each kind is what raises the Science mark, because the paper rewards shape as much as it rewards facts.

Term bank for the recall questions

Keep a running list of definitions and key terms, written in the NCERT wording. Review the bank in short recall sessions: cover the definition, say it, check it. A student who owns the terms owns a big share of the one-mark questions. The technique for building the bank is the same one used in our terminology guide.

Why-first notes for the explain questions

Explain questions punish memorised paragraphs. For every process, learn the one-line why first, then the steps. Practise explaining each why in your own words out loud, then compare with the textbook. The concept-first method behind this is expanded in our conceptual understanding guide.

Method cards for the numericals

Physics numericals in Class 10 Science follow standard methods: light, electricity and magnetic effects. Make one card per problem type with the steps, then solve a fresh set per week with working shown. The value of showing working is covered in the science answer-writing guide; the markable shape is the same for science numericals as for mathematics.

Figure rotation for the diagram questions

Diagrams appear in physics, chemistry and biology, and a clean labelled figure earns marks in all three. Keep a list of every diagram the syllabus asks for and redraw one a day from memory. The full figure bank method lives in our diagrams guide.

Activity and practical knowledge

The activity-based questions test whether you know the setup, the observation and the conclusion of the required practicals. Rehearse each experiment as a short story: aim, setup, what happened, why it happened. The same reasoning runs across the practical components, as explained in the practical preparation guide.

A balanced branch rhythm

  1. Physics: one concept plus one method set per week, with a numerical written out fully.
  2. Chemistry: reaction families written from memory, conditions on every arrow.
  3. Biology: one chapter as a flowchart plus one figure redrawn.
  4. Weekly: one timed mixed set of science questions, marked strictly.

The branch-by-branch detail behind this rhythm is the subject of our three-branch guide.

The science ground rules

  1. NCERT is the base; read it before extra books.
  2. Definitions word-perfect, explanations in your own words afterwards.
  3. Every equation balanced, with conditions.
  4. Every diagram labelled and spelled right.
  5. Every numerical with working and a unit.

The close

Prepare the Science paper by its question types: terms for recall, whys for explanation, methods for numericals, figures for diagrams, and principles for activities. Keep the three branches rotating weekly and mark every timed set honestly. The Science paper then meets a prepared student instead of surprising one.

Frequently asked questions

Should I study physics, chemistry and biology equally every day?

No. Rotate the branches with one branch as the main block each day and a small recall element from the other two. A weekly timed mixed set keeps all three honest, so no branch rests longer than a few days.

How do I prepare for definition questions in science?

Keep a running bank of definitions in exactly the NCERT wording and review it in short recall sessions: cover, say, check. The bank covers a large share of the one-mark questions almost by itself.

What is the best way to learn diagrams?

Keep a list of every diagram the syllabus asks for and redraw one each day from memory with clean pencil labels. Labelling and correct spelling matter, because a drawn figure without labels is treated as incomplete.

How should I handle science numericals?

Make one method card per problem type with the steps and formula order, then solve a fresh set weekly with full working. Every answer needs a unit, and every substitution step should stay visible for step marks.

Do activity and practical questions need separate preparation?

Yes. Rehearse each required experiment as a short story: aim, setup, observation, conclusion. The activity questions test whether you know the setup and the result, not just the name of the experiment.
