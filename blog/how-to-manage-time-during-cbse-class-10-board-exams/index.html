How to Manage Time During CBSE Class 10 Board Exams

By Mahendra Babu Chennupati | 31 Aug 2026 | 3 min read

  • The close

Class 10 board papers are rarely lost to difficulty; they are lost to pace. A student who runs out of minutes leaves marks behind even in chapters they know. Time management in the hall is a trainable skill, and these are its rules.

The per-mark budget

Start with a simple arithmetic: a three-hour paper carries a known total of marks, so minutes per mark is the paper length divided by the total. Spend a little less than that on short questions and the extra on long ones. Keep the budget in your head as two numbers: minutes per mark, and the finishing target. The budget varies per subject, but the habit of setting it before the bell does not.

Decide the order before the bell

  1. Underline the verbs during the first read and mark the questions you know instantly.
  2. Write the easy and known questions first to bank time and confidence.
  3. Then the medium questions, and leave the long ones for the middle of the paper.
  4. Keep the mentally heavy long answers away from the end, where fatigue lives.

The order should be decided at home and rehearsed, not invented in the hall under pressure.

The skip-and-return rule

Two minutes without progress on one question is the signal to skip it and return later. Mark the skipped question with a light sign so the review catches it. Missing three easy marks to chase one hard one is a losing trade in every paper; our analysis of the pattern lives in the mathematics time guide.

Finish fifteen minutes early

Aim to complete the paper fifteen minutes before the end, and spend that window only on checks: units, signs, labels, skipped questions. The last fifteen minutes are where slips get caught and borderline marks are rescued. If you finish exactly on time, you have found your budget; if you finish early, you have a checking ritual.

Per-subject mechanics

  • Mathematics: work every section by its budget, box answers, and never let one numerical eat a whole section.
  • Science: balance across the branches, and keep figure questions to their fair minutes.
  • Social science: write at speed but keep it legible; maps and timelines are quick marks.
  • Language: read the passage questions first, then the passage, then answer in order.

The science paper details are covered in our biology time guide, and the writing shape that keeps answers fast is in the answer-writing guide.

Rehearse the timing at home

Timing is not learned in the hall. Every mock from two months out runs on the same budget you will use, with the finish-early rule enforced. After each mock, log where the time actually went and fix the plan. Three timed mocks per subject usually settle the pacing for good.

The panic-minute drill

If a section runs over, stop, breathe once, and reset: skip to the next section and come back. The drill is: stop, reset, move. Practised at home in mocks, it removes the spiral that expensive minutes.

The close

Class 10 boards are won on the per-mark budget, a decided order, the skip-and-return rule, and fifteen minutes of review. Set the budget at home, rehearse it in mocks, and protect the last fifteen minutes in the hall. Time becomes your ally instead of your opponent.
