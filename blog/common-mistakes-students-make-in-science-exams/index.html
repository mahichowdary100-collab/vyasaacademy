Common Mistakes Students Make in Science Exams

By Vyasa Academy | 2 Sep 2026 | 2 min read

On this page

  • Mistake 1: answering a different question than asked
  • Mistake 2: equations and formulas without their conditions
  • Mistake 3: diagrams that are not labelled properly
  • Mistake 4: no working shown for numericals
  • Mistake 5: memorised answers that panic on change
  • Mistake 6: uneven time across the paper
  • A one-month habit purge
  • The honest summary

Most mark loss in a science exam does not come from not knowing the topic; it comes from a fixed set of habits. Each habit below is a small, specific habit and each has a small, specific fix. Clear these and the same knowledge quietly earns more marks.

Mistake 1: answering a different question than asked

Why, how, what happens, define, draw, compare: each verb asks for a different answer shape. Students often rewrite the whole chapter when the question asked for one sentence. Underline the verb before writing, answer exactly that verb, and move on.

Mistake 2: equations and formulas without their conditions

  • Chemistry: equations need to be balanced, with state symbols and conditions on the arrow.
  • Physics: formulas need units written with the values, not only the numbers.
  • Biology: a process needs its sequence, not just its name.

The condition is where the marks live. A balanced equation with no conditions earns half marks; the same with conditions earns more. Practise writing the conditions as part of the answer, never as an afterthought.

Mistake 3: diagrams that are not labelled properly

A drawn figure with no labels is treated as an incomplete answer, and spelling errors in labels cost marks. Label every key part, write labels in pencil so corrections are clean, and spell laboratory, artery and mitochondria correctly. The full diagram grammar is in our diagrams article.

Mistake 4: no working shown for numericals

Science papers award step marks for showing the method, even when the final answer is wrong. Students who hide their working depend on one perfect final answer, which is the riskiest way to answer. Show given values, the formula, the substitution and the answer, every time.

Mistake 5: memorised answers that panic on change

Textbook questions get rephrased every year, and a memorised answer collapses on the first new wording. The defence is understanding built with the concept methods and practised on varied questions. A student who understands one example handles a hundred phrasings of it.

Mistake 6: uneven time across the paper

Long answers swallow the middle of the paper and short questions are left to the last two rushed minutes. Decide the time budget before the exam: roughly a minute for each mark, with ten minutes saved for review. Clock-watch in practice papers until the rhythm becomes natural, exactly the habit used by our time management guide.

A one-month habit purge

  1. Week 1: underline the verb in every practice question before answering.
  2. Week 2: write conditions with every equation, formula and process answer.
  3. Week 3: label every diagram and review your labels table before tests.
  4. Week 4: time every practice paper, with a reserved review block.

The honest summary

The six mistakes are all habits, and habits change with a month of aimed practice. Fix the verb, fix the conditions, fix the labels, show the working, protect understanding, and time the paper. Repeat that month and the same knowledge meets the examiner in much better shape.
