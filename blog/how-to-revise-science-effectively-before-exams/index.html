How to Revise Science Effectively Before Exams

By Vyasa Academy | 27 Aug 2026 | 2 min read

  • Chemistry revision cycle: write and balance

Re-reading a chapter feels like revision but improves the paper almost nothing. Revision that works is recall: your brain should do the heavy lifting while the book stays shut. Here is a recall-first revision plan for all three branches of science.

The principle: recall, then check

Close the book and try to retrieve the idea: write it, draw it, or say it. Only after the attempt, open the book and check. The retrieval attempt is what strengthens memory; the reading after it is just confirmation. Build the session around many short attempts, not one long look.

Physics revision cycle: derive and solve

  1. List the chapters and the method types inside each.
  2. For each, redo one representative numerical with the working written out.
  3. Redraw the required ray and circuit diagrams from memory.
  4. Attempt a timed set of five unseen problems.

Physics revises itself fastest at the problem, so the cycle ends with solving, not with re-reading notes.

Chemistry revision cycle: write and balance

  1. For each chapter, write the reaction bank from memory.
  2. Balance each equation and write the conditions on the arrow.
  3. Say what family each reaction belongs to and why.
  4. Check against the NCERT page and mark the ones that slipped.

Chemistry that is written from memory is revision; chemistry that is read is only recognition, which evaporates by the lecture the next day.

Biology revision cycle: draw and name

  1. Redraw the chapters key figures from memory.
  2. Label them and correct every label that slipped.
  3. Recount each process as a flowchart from memory.
  4. Write the ten most likely one-marker terms for the chapter.

The term-figure pair makes biology revision fast and leaves the figures hand-ready for the hall, using the routine from our biology revision guide.

Use previous-year papers as the backbone

Attempt previous-year papers under time, one per week, and mark honestly. Use the collected losses to pick the topics for the following weeks revision. The paper tells your revision what to do, better than any reading list.

The final week: recall-only plus rest

  • Switch to pure recall: quick lists, mind maps and figure redraws.
  • Stop new content completely; new material late costs more calm than it earns marks.
  • Sleep at least seven hours each night of that week.
  • Run one full practice paper two days before, then stop heavy testing.

The night-before rush is not revision; it is panic, and the science paper rewards the rested mind.

Turn mistakes into the plan

The mistake log from earlier practice is the most precise revision list you will ever have. Revise exactly the logged losses, in leak order, and the revision effort concentrates where the marks actually leak.

The closing reminder

Recall first, check after, and revise each branch the way it behaves: solve for physics, write for chemistry, draw for biology. Use papers to guide it and the mistake log to aim it. That is science revision that actually raises marks.
