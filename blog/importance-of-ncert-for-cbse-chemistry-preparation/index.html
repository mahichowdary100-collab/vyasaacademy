Importance of NCERT for CBSE Chemistry Preparation

By Vyasa Academy | 27 Aug 2026 | 2 min read

  • Read one section actively
  • Turn the in-text questions into your test set
  • Keep the NCERT language for definitions
  • The revision copy: one book, two passes
  • When to bring in more material
  • The takeaway

Questions in CBSE chemistry papers are built around NCERT material, so the textbook is not optional reading, it is the syllabus made physical. But holding the book is not the same as using it. This article is about the practical difference between owning the NCERT and actually working through it.

Why the NCERT anchors the paper

Board questions are frequently written so that a student who has studied the textbook carefully can answer them, keeping the terminology, the reactions and the examples consistent with what was taught. Reference books add depth, but the core language of the exam is the textbook, so the phrasing matters. Study the NCERT first and use every other source as a supplement, not a replacement, to avoid two different versions of the same answer.

Read one section actively

Split each chapter into its headed sections and read one section at a time. After each section, close the book and write two lines: the main idea and the key reaction or concept. Then reread the section only to collect what you missed. Active reading takes longer per page and stores much more per hour.

Turn the in-text questions into your test set

The questions at the end of each NCERT chapter and the inline examples are a ready-made practice set. Attempt them on paper before looking at solutions, then compare your wording with the model style. Doing this chapter by chapter is the closest legal shortcut to thinking like the question setter.

Keep the NCERT language for definitions

Definitions and named laws are safest in the textbook wording. When an answer needs a definition, produce the NCERT phrasing rather than your paraphrase of it. For explanations, the model sentence structure shown in the book is a reliable template for your own answers.

The revision copy: one book, two passes

  1. First pass: read actively, marking nothing until the section is understood.
  2. Second pass: underline the key lines and build the recall pages.
  3. Revision: recall from your pages, then check the underlined lines only.

Two honest passes beat ten skimmed ones, and the marked copy becomes a fast revision tool for the final month described in our 30-day plan.

When to bring in more material

After the NCERT chapters and their questions are genuinely done, add previous-year questions and one supplementary book if your teacher recommends it. The supplement should extend the textbook, never replace its versions of reactions and definitions. Students who keep this sequence rarely find themselves learning conflicting answers at exam time.

The takeaway

The NCERT is the foundation because the paper speaks its language. Read it actively, practise its questions, keep its definitions, and let supplements extend from it. That is the most reliable chemistry preparation routine a CBSE student can build.
