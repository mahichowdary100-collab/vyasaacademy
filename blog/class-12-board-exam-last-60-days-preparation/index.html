How to Prepare for Class 12 Boards in the Last 60 Days

Sixty days before the Class 12 boards is the most useful window in the whole year: long enough to repair weak topics and short enough to keep the focus sharp. This plan treats the two months as two different jobs: the first month repairs, the second month rehearses. Here is the week-by-week shape with the daily skeleton under it.

The two halves of the window

Month one is repair: clear the weak-topic list, finish the one-page summaries and complete the chapter tests you skipped. Month two is rehearsal: full papers weekly, previous-year questions and memory production. Students who try to rehearse in month one waste the papers, and students who only repair in month two run out of time.

Weeks 1 to 2: the repair list

  1. Pull every weak-topic entry from the error log into a ranked list.
  2. Clear one topic per day using the recall-check-fix cycle.
  3. Finish a one-page summary for every remaining chapter.
  4. Complete any pending chapter tests and mark them strictly.

The repair fortnight is the quiet place where the whole later plan becomes smaller and calmer.

Weeks 3 to 4: chapter-wise previous years

Start working previous-year questions chapter-wise, right after each repaired topic. This shows which question angles each chapter produces and feeds the one-page summaries. The method is the first stage of the previous-year paper guide.

Weeks 5 to 6: the paper cycle begins

  1. One full paper per subject every week, in exam timing, with no help.
  2. Mark each paper as strictly as an examiner would.
  3. Revise exactly the misses on the days between papers.
  4. Keep one daily recall block for formulas, derivations, reactions and figures.

Treat the pre-boards in this window as extra calibration: whatever the pre-board shows, feed the misses into the same list. The full pre-board approach is in the pre-board guide.

Weeks 7 to 8: taper and calm

Shift the paper cycle to every five days and then to pure memory work, summaries, reaction banks, derivations and figure banks. Drop new material completely in the final week and let the 30-day plan take over from this point.

The daily skeleton

  1. Morning: recall block, twenty to thirty minutes, memory lists.
  2. Midday: the day main task, a topic or a paper.
  3. Afternoon: marking and logging the misses.
  4. Evening: light review of the day summaries, then a fixed stop.
  5. Weekly: the Sunday ten-minute adjustment.

Keeping entrance prep alive without bleeding the boards

Keep one fixed weekly block of competitive practice during the first month and let it taper in the final two weeks. The overlap, not the competition, is the point: the same concepts feed both, as the balancing guide explains.

The honest close

Sixty days is repair plus rehearsal: a fortnight of fixing, a fortnight of chapter-wise papers, a full paper cycle, then a taper into calm. Run the two halves in order and the boards will feel like a repeated performance. That is the whole plan, and it is enough.

Frequently asked questions

What is different about the 60-day window compared with the 30-day one?

Sixty days gives room to fix weak topics properly before the paper cycle begins, so the first month is repair and the second month is rehearsal. Thirty days is almost pure rehearsal.

How should I spend the first fortnight?

On the weak-topic repair from the error log, one topic a day, plus building the one-page summaries for every chapter. The fix work reduces the paper phase to real misses instead of everything.

How many full papers should the second month have?

One full paper per subject every week, in exam timing, marked strictly, with the miss list feeding the days between. Pre-boards act as the mid-cycle calibration for this rhythm.

Does this plan work while preparing for entrance exams too?

Yes. Keep one fixed weekly block of competitive practice alive during the first month, then let it taper in the final two weeks. The boards drive the schedule until they are done.
