How to Prepare Biology Using NCERT

By Vyasa Academy | 29 Aug 2026 | 2 min read

On this page

  • Read each section actively
  • Keep definitions in the textbook language
  • Learn the figures from the pages
  • Turn the end-questions into practice sets
  • Use the glossary as a term checklist
  • When to bring in extra material
  • The takeaway

Biology questions are built from the NCERT textbook, so the book is not optional reading, it is the syllabus made physical. The difference between students is rarely the book they hold; it is whether they work the book or just own it. Here is how to work it properly.

Read each section actively

Split the chapter into its headed sections and read one at a time. After each section, close the book and retell the idea in two written lines. Then reread the section to collect only what you missed. Active reading takes longer per page and stores far more per hour than underlining whole paragraphs.

Keep definitions in the textbook language

Biological definitions are marked against a known shape, and the NCERT phrasing is the safest one to reproduce. When an answer needs a definition, aim for the textbook language rather than your paraphrase. For explanations, keep your own words but keep the terms exact.

Learn the figures from the pages

The figures in the textbook are the figures the questions assume. For each one, redraw it from memory and check your labels against the book. The figure discipline is the same one described in our diagram memory guide, applied to every NCERT figure in your syllabus.

Turn the end-questions into practice sets

  1. Answer every chapter exercise on paper before looking at solutions.
  2. Compare your wording with the model answers and mark the drift.
  3. Redo the ones you missed after three days from memory.
  4. Use the inline examples the same way, treating them as warm questions.

The chapter exercises are the closest legal shortcut to thinking like the question setter.

Use the glossary as a term checklist

The glossary at the back is your biology vocabulary for the board. Test yourself across it on a rotation. A term you can define in one line from the glossary is a term you can use in any answer.

When to bring in extra material

Only after the textbook chapters and their questions are genuinely done should previous-year papers and a supplementary book join in. The supplement should extend the textbook, never replace its versions of definitions and figures. That sequence keeps every answer consistent with the boards source of truth.

The takeaway

The NCERT is the biology exam made into a book. Read it actively, reproduce its definitions and figures, and practise its questions. Work the book and the boards stop feeling like a mystery.
