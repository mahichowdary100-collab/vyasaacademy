How to Prepare Physics for CBSE Class 12 Board Exams

Class 12 physics asks three kinds of work: conceptual explanation, derivations and numerical problem solving, and the marks for each are equal in importance to the marks for the others. Students who revise only the theory master the explanation marks and give away the rest. This guide runs the three tracks side by side and fits them into a weekly rhythm.

Track 1: build physics as a story, not a list of formulas

Every topic is an explanation of how something works: how charges move, how fields bend space, how circuits behave. Before memorising any formula, ask what each symbol means, what physics sits behind it and what would break if one term changed. When a formula has a story it stays in memory for months instead of days. The concept-first method behind this is the same one considered in the physics study guide.

Track 2: derivations you can derive

  1. Read the derivation and mark its key assumption and its logic chain.
  2. Write it from memory on a blank page, then check against the textbook.
  3. Redo it from memory again after two days, then after a week.
  4. Log the derivations that still slip into the error log.

A derivation is scored for its chain, not just its result, so practise producing the whole chain cleanly. The students who earn the full marks are the ones who can derive, not the ones who can recognise.

Track 3: numericals with units in view

Physics numericals in Class 12 follow standard methods: electrostatics, current and magnetic effects, optics and modern physics. Make one method card per problem type with the steps, then solve a fresh set weekly with full working. Every answer needs a unit and every substitution step should stay visible for step marks. The full method-card system is in the physics numericals guide.

Keep a figure bank for the diagram questions

Circuit diagrams, ray diagrams, field lines and labelled construction figures appear across the paper. Keep a running list and redraw one a day from memory with clean pencil lines and correct labels. A neat labelled figure is a scored unit, and spelling and labels are the parts students rush most.

Use the sample paper and previous-year papers as the calibration

The published sample paper shows the current question style and the previous-year papers show the pattern of what is asked. Use both as rehearsal material, not as a source of invented weightage claims. Name honestly what you observe about question angles and prepare proportionally, keeping the official syllabus as the only authoritative guide.

Sharpen calculation under time

Physics numericals punish slow arithmetic, so train the same accuracy habits used in mathematics through the calculation accuracy guide. Round the numbers and estimate the answer before computing, then the final result should land close to expectation. A fifteen-second estimate catches a large share of slips before the examiner ever sees them.

A balanced weekly rhythm for physics

  1. One concept topic, read for the why and summarised in one page.
  2. One derivation, written from memory and checked.
  3. One numerical set, solved with full working and units, marked strictly.
  4. One figure from the bank, redrawn and labelled.
  5. One timed set of mixed physics questions weekly.

The physics ground rules

  1. NCERT is the base; read it before extra books.
  2. Formulas paired with their story, not memorised alone.
  3. Derivations produced from memory, not recognised.
  4. Units on every numerical, substitution steps visible.
  5. Figures clean, labelled and spelled correctly.

The close

Prepare the physics paper by its three tracks: concepts as stories, derivations you can derive, and numericals solved with units in view. Keep the figure bank rotating, calibrate with the sample and previous-year papers, and rehearse under time. The physics paper then meets a prepared student instead of surprising one.

Frequently asked questions

Which derivations should I master first?

Master the derivations in your textbook chapter by chapter, and build a derivation list with the key assumption written beside each one. The list becomes your revision spine, because derivations test concepts and presentation together.

Why do I lose marks in numericals I can actually solve?

Almost always because of units, missing substitution steps or careless rounding. Show every step with its unit, keep the calculator work neat and estimate the answer before computing so slips become visible.

How should I read the theory in physics?

Chase the why first: what happens, why it happens, then the definition, then the formula. A formula learned after its reason is remembered for months; a formula learned alone decays in days.

Can derivations be memorised directly?

Memorising a derivation without the logic fails under pressure because one forgotten line breaks the chain. Learn the logic, then write the derivation from memory until it flows.

How do I prepare circuit and ray diagrams?

Keep a figure bank of every diagram the syllabus uses and redraw each from memory on rotation, with components labelled cleanly. Labelled diagrams are scored units in the physics paper.
