How to Revise Before an Exam

By exam week, the learning is finished, and the job that remains is different: converting what you know into recall under time. That is revision. Students who learn well but revise badly leave marks on the table, which makes revision a skill worth its own plan.

Revision is recall, not a second first-read

Re-reading a chapter in the final days feels productive and produces the least. Revision works when the book is closed and the material comes out. The turn everything into questions and answer them from memory, using the same loop as the active recall method at exam scale.

Start weeks ahead, finish early

  1. Three to four weeks before: first revision pass, one chapter a day, marking weak topics.
  2. Two weeks before: redo the topics marked weak, one a day.
  3. Final week: one-page summaries, error log and one light practice paper.
  4. Final two days: memory lists only, and full sleep.

The schedule finishes early on purpose: the last days paddle in calm water instead of swimming upstream.

The recall-check-fix cycle for every chapter

  1. Recall: write the chapter summary from memory on a blank page.
  2. Check: open the one-page notes and mark every missing point.
  3. Fix: re-learn only the misses, not the whole chapter.
  4. Log: add the misses to the error list.
  5. Re-verify: recall the summary again the next day.

A chapter revised through the cycle in twenty minutes holds better than a chapter re-read in forty.

Let past papers run the revision

Attempt at least one full paper per main subject in the final weeks, timed, marked honestly. The parts that bleed marks become the top of the next revision list. The method for reading a paper as training is in the previous-year paper guide.

Revise each subject in its own shape

  • Mathematics: solve problems, recall method cards, redo the missed sums.
  • Science: definitions, processes, reactions and diagrams from memory.
  • Languages: formats, one timed composition, vocabulary in sentences.

The subject fit follows the revision notes guide, where each chapter becomes a one-page summary built for exactly this week.

The night before and the morning of

The night before: summaries once, error log once, sleep. The morning of: a single look at the one-page summary and a calm arrival with ten quiet minutes. The night-before cram is the most common revision mistake, and it is fully avoidable by the finishing-early plan.

Common revision mistakes

  1. Starting fresh material inside the final week.
  2. Revising by reading only.
  3. Practising the chapters you enjoy and skipping the weak ones.
  4. Comparing hours with classmates instead of recall.
  5. Skipping sleep for one more re-read.

The close

Revising for an exam is the last, most public test of how you learned. Start early, run the recall-check-fix cycle chapter by chapter and let papers referee the final weeks. Rested and rehearsed beats exhausted and re-read, every time.

Frequently asked questions

How far before an exam should revision start?

Weeks before, not days. The first pass should finish early so the last days hold light recall and practice papers. Starting late forces cramming, which is why the revision feels so fragile.

What should I revise the night before?

Nothing new. Review the one-page summaries and the personal error list, and sleep fully. Recall at the desk in the morning beats new material learned at 11 p.m.

Should I revise the easier or harder topics first?

Harder topics while you are fresh, in small doses, then the easier material as confident filler. The easy chapters reward steady review more than they reward stress.

Is cramming ever useful?

Cramming places fresh material in short-term memory for the next morning, which helps in an emergency and nothing else. It melts before the next test, so exam seasons deserve spaced revision instead.
