How to Manage Time During Exams

By Venu Chennupati | 29 Aug 2026 | 3 min read

  • Use checkpoints to catch a slow start

Every exam hall has the same clock, and the same number of marks. The students who finish feel the pressure; the students who finish well work to a plan of minutes. Time management inside the exam is a rehearsable skill, and this guide is the rehearsal plan.

Win the first five minutes

Read the full paper once, briskly, before writing. Mark the sections you can answer cleanly and note the order you will attempt them. The read removes surprise and gives the paper a shape you have already seen. Five minutes spent reading saves twenty later.

Spend minutes by marks, not by instinct

A three-hour paper worth eighty marks gives a rough budget: spend near the marks, a little more on the heavy sections, a little less on the light. A pre-written budget on the corner of the sheet keeps the plan visible all paper long. The mark-weighted budget is the same one the Class 10 plan runs at board scale.

Attack in the order of marks

  1. Short and objective items first: they bank marks fast.
  2. Two and three mark questions next.
  3. Long answers in the middle, when the mind is still fresh.
  4. The hardest or most uncertain question last, when everything else is banked.

The order is rehearsed in practice papers, so the hall is not the first place you try it.

Use checkpoints to catch a slow start

Fix two moments: when half the time has gone and when a quarter remains. At each checkpoint compare where you are against the budget and reallocate the minutes left. A slow start caught at the half-hour mark is cheap; the same catch at the last ten minutes is not.

When you are stuck, park the question

  1. Leave a small marker by the question number.
  2. Take the next question and return later.
  3. Blank on a definition: write the nearby material, refine on return.
  4. Never sink more than the budget minutes into one question.

The partial marks on the rest of the paper outscore the perfect answer to one hard question.

The final fifteen minutes are an audit

  1. Stop starting new answers at the fifteen minute mark.
  2. Check every question is attempted and every part labelled.
  3. Scan for slips: signs, units, spellings, skipped steps.
  4. Confirm the name and roll number on every sheet.

The audit habit converts several marks back from the last minutes of the paper, which is where they are usually lost.

Train the skill before the hall

Timing is built in practice papers with a real timer and a strict stop. Each timed paper feeds the mistake audit with time-loss data. Students who rehearse the budget at home walk into the hall managing a system they have already run.

Common time mistakes

  1. Reading the paper while already writing the first answer.
  2. Spending a third of the time on the very first question.
  3. Leaving long answers to fall in the last ten minutes.
  4. Writing answers in question order regardless of marks.
  5. Handing the paper in early with answers unaudited.

The close

Exam time is won in minutes: read first, budget by marks, bank the easy wins, checkpoint the pace and audit the close. Rehearse the whole sequence under a timer at home. On the day it is not a race against the clock, it is a plan you have already run.
