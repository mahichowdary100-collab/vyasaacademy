Learning objectives

After this chapter, you should be able to describe straight-line motion using position, distance, displacement, speed, velocity and acceleration; read position–time and velocity–time graphs; use constant-acceleration equations; and explain uniform circular motion.

Core idea

Motion is change of position with time relative to a chosen reference point. Choosing a clear origin and positive direction makes the signs in calculations and graphs meaningful.

1. Position, distance and displacement

Position states where an object is relative to a reference point. For straight-line motion, choose an origin and one positive direction; positions on the opposite side are negative. An object is moving when its position relative to the reference point changes with time.

Distance travelled is the total length of the path. It is a scalar and is never negative. Displacement is the net change in position: final position minus initial position. In one dimension, its sign indicates direction.

Distance and the magnitude of displacement are equal when an object moves in one direction without turning back. If it returns to its start, displacement is zero even though distance is not.

Sign convention

Write your chosen positive direction at the start. Keep it unchanged throughout the solution; do not treat a negative displacement as a negative distance.

2. Average speed and average velocity

Average speed describes how much path is covered per unit time; average velocity describes the net change in position per unit time and includes direction.

Formulas and SI units

Average speed = total distance ÷ time interval; average velocity = displacement ÷ time interval.

Both have SI unit metre per second (m s−1). The conversion is 1 m s−1 = 3.6 km h−1.

Worked example

A person walks from position 0 m to +100 m and then returns to +40 m in 20 s.

Distance = 100 + 60 = 160 m, so average speed = 160 ÷ 20 = 8 m s−1.

Displacement = +40 − 0 = +40 m, so average velocity = 40 ÷ 20 = +2 m s−1 (positive direction).

For motion in one direction with no turning back, average speed equals the magnitude of average velocity. During a round trip, the average velocity over the complete trip is zero if the object returns to its starting position.

3. Average acceleration

Acceleration measures how quickly velocity changes. A change in speed, a change in direction, or both means velocity changes.

Formula

Average acceleration, (aav = (v − u) / t), where (u) is initial velocity, (v) is final velocity and (t) is the time interval.

SI unit: m s−2. For a fixed positive direction, acceleration is positive when velocity increases algebraically and negative when it decreases algebraically.

Worked example

A cyclist’s velocity changes from +5 m s−1 to +17 m s−1 in 4 s.

(aav = (17-5)/4 = 3 m s−2).

Negative acceleration does not always mean an object is slowing down. It slows when acceleration is opposite the velocity; if velocity and acceleration have the same sign, the speed increases.

4. Position–time graphs

Put time on the horizontal axis and position on the vertical axis. Each plotted point gives the object’s position at a particular instant. A straight line has constant slope and therefore constant velocity; a curve has changing slope and therefore changing velocity.

For two points on a position–time graph, average velocity = change in position ÷ change in time. A horizontal segment means position is constant (the object is at rest during that interval). A downward slope means motion in the negative direction.

5. Velocity–time graphs

Put time on the horizontal axis and velocity on the vertical axis. The slope of a velocity–time graph gives acceleration. The signed area between the graph and the time axis gives displacement; when velocity stays positive, this area is also distance travelled.

For constant velocity, the velocity–time graph is a horizontal line (zero slope, zero acceleration). A straight sloping line indicates constant acceleration. A line sloping downward can represent negative acceleration; whether the object slows depends on the sign of its velocity too.

6. Equations for constant acceleration

For straight-line motion with constant acceleration, let (u) be initial velocity, (v) final velocity after time (t), (a) acceleration and (s) displacement. The equations follow from the velocity–time graph: its slope gives acceleration and its area gives displacement.

Kinematic equations

(v = u + at)

(s = ut + 1/2at2)

(v2 = u2 + 2as)

Use signed values according to the chosen direction. The equations apply only when acceleration is constant.

Graph-based derivation of the first two

Slope of the straight velocity–time line (= (v-u)/t = a), so (v=u+at). The displacement is the area under the line: rectangle (ut) plus triangle (1/2(v-u)t). Substituting (v-u=at) gives (s=ut+1/2at2). Eliminating (t) yields (v2=u2+2as).

Worked example · speeding up

A cart has (u=4 m s−1), accelerates at (a=2 m s−2) for (t=5 s).

(v=u+at=4+(2)(5)=14 m s−1).

(s=ut+1/2at2=(4)(5)+1/2(2)(25)=20+25=45 m).

Check with the third equation: (v2=16+2(2)(45)=196), so (v=14 m s−1) in the chosen positive direction.

Worked example · slowing to rest

A vehicle moving at (20 m s−1) has constant acceleration (-4 m s−2). Find the time and displacement until it stops.

Take the direction of motion as positive. (u=20, v=0, a=-4).

(t=(v-u)/a=(0-20)/(-4)=5 s).

(s=ut+1/2at2=(20)(5)+1/2(-4)(25)=100-50=50 m).

Common mistakes
  • Mixing km h⁻¹ with m s⁻¹ without converting units.
  • Using a constant-acceleration equation when acceleration changes.
  • Dropping the negative sign for acceleration while an object slows in the chosen positive direction.
  • Using distance (s) where the equation requires signed displacement.

7. Uniform circular motion

When an object moves around a circular path at constant speed, it is in uniform circular motion. Its speed remains constant, but its velocity changes because the direction changes continuously. Therefore the motion is accelerated even though the speedometer reading can stay the same.

For radius (R), one complete revolution covers circumference (2π R). If the time for one revolution is (T), average speed for the round is (2π R/T). After one complete revolution the displacement is zero, so average velocity over that full revolution is zero.

Key distinction

Constant speed does not always mean constant velocity. Velocity includes direction; in circular motion the direction changes at every point.

8. Original practice question bank

These questions are original revision practice, not copied NCERT exercise prompts. Answers are included for checking.

Multiple-choice questions — 10

1. A student walks 30 m east and then 10 m west. What is the displacement?
  1. 40 m east
  2. 20 m east
  3. 20 m west
  4. 0 m

Answer: B. Taking east as positive, displacement = +30 − 10 = +20 m.

2. Which quantity can be zero after a non-zero journey?
  1. Distance
  2. Elapsed time
  3. Displacement
  4. Average speed

Answer: C. Returning to the start gives zero net displacement, though distance is positive.

3. The slope of a position–time graph represents:
  1. Acceleration
  2. Velocity
  3. Distance
  4. Time interval

Answer: B. Change in position divided by change in time is velocity.

4. The slope of a velocity–time graph represents:
  1. Displacement
  2. Speed
  3. Acceleration
  4. Position

Answer: C. Slope is change in velocity per unit time.

5. For positive velocity and negative acceleration, the object is:
  1. Speeding up
  2. Slowing down
  3. At rest
  4. Moving in a circle necessarily

Answer: B. Acceleration opposite velocity reduces speed.

6. A horizontal velocity–time line above the time axis means:
  1. Constant positive velocity
  2. Increasing acceleration
  3. Object at rest
  4. Negative displacement only

Answer: A. Velocity is constant and positive; acceleration is zero.

7. Which equation is valid only for constant acceleration?
  1. Δx = xf − xi
  2. v = u + at
  3. Average speed = distance/time
  4. Position changes with time

Answer: B. The kinematic equations assume constant acceleration.

8. An object completes one lap of a circular track and returns to its start. Its displacement is:
  1. The circumference
  2. Twice the radius
  3. Zero
  4. Equal to its speed

Answer: C. Initial and final positions coincide.

9. During uniform circular motion, which statement is true?
  1. Velocity is constant
  2. Speed and direction are both constant
  3. Speed is constant but velocity changes
  4. Acceleration is zero

Answer: C. Direction changes continuously, so velocity changes.

10. Convert 72 km h⁻¹ to m s⁻¹.
  1. 10
  2. 20
  3. 25.9
  4. 259.2

Answer: B. (72 ÷ 3.6 = 20 m s−1).

Numerical and graph practice

  1. A car moves 150 m in 10 s in a straight line without reversing. Find average speed and average velocity.

    Average speed = 150/10 = 15 m s⁻¹. With the direction of motion chosen positive, displacement is +150 m and average velocity = +15 m s⁻¹.

  2. A runner's velocity changes from 3 m s⁻¹ to 15 m s⁻¹ in 6 s. Find average acceleration.

    (a=(15-3)/6=2 m s−2).

  3. A body starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at 3 m s⁻² for 4 s. Find final velocity and displacement.

    (v=0+(3)(4)=12 m s−1). (s=0+1/2(3)(42)=24 m).

  4. A velocity–time graph is a straight line from 4 m s⁻¹ at 0 s to 12 m s⁻¹ at 5 s. Find acceleration and displacement.

    Acceleration = slope = (12−4)/5 = 1.6 m s⁻². Displacement = area of trapezium = [(4+12)/2]×5 = 40 m.

  5. A cyclist travels one full circle of radius 14 m in 22 s. Find average speed for the lap and average velocity.

    Distance = (2π R=28π≈88) m (using π=22/7). Average speed = 88/22 = 4 m s⁻¹. Displacement is zero, so average velocity = 0 m s⁻¹.

Concept questions

  1. Can an object have constant speed but non-zero acceleration? Explain.

    Yes. In uniform circular motion, speed is constant but direction of velocity changes continuously, so acceleration is non-zero.

  2. What does the area under a velocity–time graph represent?

    Signed area gives displacement over the interval. If velocity remains positive throughout, it equals distance travelled.

  3. Why must the sign convention remain fixed in a motion problem?

    The signs of position, displacement, velocity and acceleration depend on the chosen positive direction. Changing it mid-solution makes the quantities inconsistent.

9. Quick revision and concept map

Distance is total path length; displacement is final position minus initial position.

Velocity.

Slope gives acceleration; signed area gives displacement.

For straight-line motion with constant acceleration.

Velocity direction changes while speed stays constant.

Concept map

Choose reference point and direction → describe position → compare positions to get displacement; add path segments to get distance → divide by time for average velocity or average speed → change in velocity per time gives acceleration → graph position or velocity against time → use slope/area → apply constant-acceleration equations when valid → for circular motion, changing direction means changing velocity.
