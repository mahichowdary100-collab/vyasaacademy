Exercise Set 10.1 · Combining averages and mixtures

Question 1 · Combine two section averages

Question: Two sections average 72 and 76 on a test; they have 30 and 25 students. Find the combined average.

  1. Convert each average to a total: A = 30 × 72 = 2,160; B = 25 × 76 = 1,900.
  2. Total marks = 4,060 and total students = 55.
  3. Combined average = 4,060 ÷ 55 = 812/11 ≈ 73.82.

Answer: about 73.82 marks.

Question 2 · Nitrogen fraction in an equal-quantity mixture

Question: Three equal quantities of fertiliser contain nitrogen fractions 1/10, 9/50 and 3/60. Find the mixture’s fraction.

  1. Equal quantities have equal weights, so take the simple mean.
  2. Convert to hundredths: 1/10 = 10/100, 9/50 = 18/100, 3/60 = 5/100.
  3. Mean fraction = (10 + 18 + 5)/(100 × 3) = 33/300 = 11/100.

Answer: 11/100, or 11% nitrogen.

Question 3 · Combined gold purity in varṇa

Question: Combine 9 units at 12 varṇa, 5 units at 10 varṇa and 17 units at 11 varṇa. Find the resulting purity.

  1. Use quantities as weights: total gold units = 9 + 5 + 17 = 31.
  2. Weighted purity = (9×12 + 5×10 + 17×11)/31 = (108 + 50 + 187)/31 = 345/31.
  3. 345/31 ≈ 11.129 varṇa. Since 16 varṇa = 24 carat, this is (345/31)×(24/16) ≈ 16.69 carat.

Answer: 345/31 ≈ 11.13 varṇa (about 16.69 carat).

Question 4 · Average rainfall across three months

Question: Daily average rainfall is 3.5 mm in May, 10 mm in June and 8.7 mm in July. Write an expression for the combined average.

  1. Weight each monthly average by its number of days: May 31, June 30, July 31.
  2. Combined mean = (31×3.5 + 30×10 + 31×8.7)/(31+30+31).
  3. = (108.5 + 300 + 269.7)/92 = 678.2/92 ≈ 7.37 mm per day.

Answer: (31×3.5 + 30×10 + 31×8.7)/92 ≈ 7.37 mm.

Question 5 · Spice concentration in two mixtures

Question: Mix 100, 200 and 300 mL batches at 5%, 10% and 15%; then repeat with the 300 mL and 100 mL volumes swapped. Find each concentration.

  1. (i) Spice amount = 100(0.05)+200(0.10)+300(0.15)=7,000 percentage-mL, over 600 mL. Concentration = 7,000/600% = 11⅔%.
  2. (ii) Spice amount = 300(0.05)+200(0.10)+100(0.15)=5,000 percentage-mL. Concentration = 5,000/600% = 8⅓%.
  3. The same concentrations give different answers because their volumes have different weights in each mixture.

Answer: (i) 11⅔%; (ii) 8⅓%.

Exercise Set 10.2 · Scores on different scales

Question 1 · Choose valid weighted-score expressions

Question: Savitri scores 35/50, 44/60 and 80/100 in assessments weighted 3:4:5. Decide which listed expressions correctly give her annual percentage.

  1. First put each score on a common percentage scale: 35/50×100 = 70%; 44/60×100 = 73⅓%; 80/100×100 = 80%.
  2. Weighted percentage = (3×70 + 4×73⅓ + 5×80)/(3+4+5) = 903⅓/12 = 75.277…%.
  3. The expressions that convert each score to its percentage before weighting, either multiplying by 100 at the end or first, are valid: (iii) and (iv). The raw-mark expression (i) combines scores with different maxima; (ii) divides all marks by 100 and does not produce a percentage.

Answer: (iii) and (iv); annual score ≈ 75.28%.

Exercise Set 10.3 · Custom weights and mixtures

Question 1 · Profit percentage on total sales

Question: A shop sells books for ₹8,000 at 30% profit and covers for ₹1,000 at 50% profit. Find the combined profit percentage.

  1. Profit on books = 0.30×8,000 = ₹2,400; on covers = 0.50×1,000 = ₹500.
  2. Total profit = ₹2,900 and total sales = ₹9,000.
  3. Profit percentage = (2,900/9,000)×100 ≈ 32.22%.

Answer: about 32.22%.

Question 2 · Update a 20-day average

Question: A stork averages 44.5 km per day over 20 days, then flies 55 km on day 21. Find the new daily average.

  1. Distance over the first 20 days = 20×44.5 = 890 km.
  2. Total over 21 days = 890+55 = 945 km.
  3. New mean = 945/21 = 45 km per day. This is slightly above 44.5 because 55 is above the old average.

Answer: 45 km per day.

Question 3 · Salt and sugar in a mixed solution

Question: Mix 600 mL of 5% salt solution with 300 mL of 8% sugar solution. Find the final salt and sugar concentrations.

  1. Salt amount = 600×0.05 = 30 mL; sugar amount = 300×0.08 = 24 mL.
  2. Total mixture = 900 mL. Salt concentration = 30/900×100% = 3⅓%.
  3. Sugar concentration = 24/900×100% = 2⅔%.

Answer: salt ≈ 3.33%; sugar ≈ 2.67% (choice v).

Question 4 · Dilute spicy water

Question: Ten litres of pani is 8% spice. How much plain water reduces its concentration to three-quarters of the original?

  1. Target concentration = ¾×8% = 6%. The spice amount stays 0.08×10 = 0.8 L.
  2. Let x be the litres of water added. Then 0.8/(10+x)=0.06.
  3. So 10+x = 0.8/0.06 = 13⅓, hence x = 3⅓ L.

Answer: add 3⅓ litres of plain water.

Question 5 · Compare weighted fitness scores

Question: Strength, flexibility and agility weights are 4:5:6. Rashi scores 60,65,70; Keerthi scores 55,65,75. Compare without full calculation, then find both final marks.

  1. Relative to Rashi, Keerthi is −5 in strength, equal in flexibility and +5 in agility. The weighted change is 4(−5)+5(0)+6(5)=10, so Keerthi’s weighted total is higher.
  2. Rashi: (4×60+5×65+6×70)/15 = 985/15 = 65⅔.
  3. Keerthi: (4×55+5×65+6×75)/15 = 995/15 = 66⅓.

Answer: Keerthi scores more; Rashi = 65⅔ and Keerthi = 66⅓.

Question 7 · Langur counts and changing averages

Question: Sixty langurs have mean weights 16.5 kg (males), 13.8 kg (females) and 14.925 kg overall. Determine the count and answer the follow-up mean questions.

  1. Let male count be m; female count is 60−m. Then [16.5m+13.8(60−m)]/60=14.925. Since 14.925×60=895.5, we get 2.7m+828=895.5, so m=25. Thus 35 are female.
  2. (i) The correct model is (a): (16.5x+13.8y)/(x+y)=14.925, with x+y=60. Other listed expressions do not represent a weighted mean.
  3. (iii) Female total = 35×13.8=483 kg. Add 15.2 kg: new mean =498.2/36 ≈13.84 kg.
  4. (iv) The two released males weigh 16.9+16.1=33 kg, exactly twice the old mean. Removing them leaves the male mean 16.5 kg.
  5. (v) If one of 25 males loses 1 kg, the total falls by 1 kg; new mean = (25×16.5−1)/25 =16.46 kg.

Answer: 25 males, 35 females; new female mean ≈13.84 kg; male mean after release 16.5 kg; after 1 kg loss 16.46 kg.

Question 8 · Salinity of mixtures

Question: Mix 1 L of Dead Sea water (34% salinity) with purified water (0.001%). Find the salinity with 2 L added; determine whether groundwater salinity (0.01%) is attainable and whether a mixture can be less saline than purified water.

  1. (i) Salt-equivalent amount = 1×34 + 2×0.001 = 34.002. Divide by 3 L: 11.334% approximately.
  2. (ii) Let x litres of purified water produce 0.01%: (34+0.001x)/(1+x)=0.01. Then 33.99=0.009x, so x≈3,776.67 L. It is attainable because 0.01% lies between the two source concentrations.
  3. (iii) A mixture cannot be less saline than its least-saline ingredient. Since purified water is 0.001% and groundwater is 0.01%, this mixture cannot reach any value below 0.001%.

Answer: (i) ≈11.334%; (ii) yes, with ≈3,776.67 L purified water; (iii) no, the mixture cannot be below 0.001%.

This is a mathematical concentration model; it is not advice about water safety.

Exercise Set 10.4 · Reading stacked bar charts

Question 1 · Endangered-species stacked columns

Question: Read an IUCN stacked column chart for 2007–2019: interpret the 2019 total, estimate reptiles in 2016 and identify classes with relatively little growth.

  1. (i) 14,234 labels the combined total of all animal classes shown for 2019.
  2. (ii) The 2016 reptile segment is about 1,100 species. Since this is read from a graph, a nearby estimate (roughly 1,000–1,300) is reasonable.
  3. (iii) The mammal, bird and amphibian bands have comparatively similar thickness in 2007 and 2019, so those classes show relatively little increase compared with the larger-growing bands.

Answer: (i) total species listed in 2019; (ii) about 1,100 reptiles; (iii) mammals, birds and amphibians.

End-of-Chapter Exercises

Question 1 · Cricket run rates

Question: A team scores 6 in over 1 and 12 in over 2. Find its run rate; then find the rate after 20 overs if the first 19 overs averaged 6 and the last over adds 12 runs.

  1. (i) Total after two overs = 6+12=18, so rate=18/2=9 runs per over.
  2. (ii) Runs after 19 overs = 19×6=114; after over 20, total=126. Rate=126/20=6.3.

Answer: (i) 9; (ii) 6.3 runs per over.

Question 2 · Average price of shuttlecocks

Question: Five friends collect different numbers of nylon and feather shuttlecock prices. Express the combined mean using (i) each friend’s average and (ii) type averages, and compare the results.

  1. The friends collected 6, 4, 5, 4 and 4 prices: 23 total. If their means are a_y, a_s, a_k, a_p, a_g, then combined mean = (6a_y + 4a_s + 5a_k + 4a_p + 4a_g)/23.
  2. Count by type: 13 nylon and 10 feather prices. If their respective means are a_n and a_f, combined mean = (13a_n+10a_f)/23.
  3. Both formulas reconstruct the same total of the same 23 observations, so they give the same result.

Answer: use the weighted means above; yes, both methods agree.

Question 3 · Average share price after purchases

Question: Shreyas owns 25 shares averaging ₹150 and buys 10 at ₹30. Vaishnavi owns 5 at ₹150 and buys enough at ₹30 to bring her mean to ₹70. Find both results.

  1. Shreyas’s old cost =25×150=₹3,750. New cost=3,750+10×30=₹4,050 over 35 shares, so mean=4,050/35=₹810/7≈₹115.71.
  2. Let Vaishnavi buy k shares. (5×150+30k)/(5+k)=70.
  3. 750+30k=350+70k, so k=10. Check: (750+300)/15=70.

Answer: Shreyas ≈₹115.71 per share; Vaishnavi buys 10 shares.

Question 4 · Mean depth of a pool

Question: A 30-hasta pool has five sections of lengths 4,5,6,7,8 hastas and depths 9,7,7,3,2 hastas. Find the mean depth.

  1. Weight each depth by its section length.
  2. Mean depth=(4×9+5×7+6×7+7×3+8×2)/30.
  3. =(36+35+42+21+16)/30=150/30=5.

Answer: 5 hastas.

Question 5 · New average gold price

Question: Suvarna has 1 g of gold bought at ₹15,000/g. Find the new average price for each purchase: (i) 1 g at ₹30,000; (ii) 2 g at ₹30,000; (iii) 1 g at ₹10,000; (iv) 10 g at ₹10,000; (v) 0.5 g at ₹30,000; (vi) 0.5 g at ₹15,000.

  1. Measure prices in thousands of rupees. For a purchase of w grams at price p, new mean=(15+wp)/(1+w).
  2. (i) (15+30)/2=22.5; (ii) (15+2×30)/3=25.
  3. (iii) (15+10)/2=12.5; (iv) (15+10×10)/11=115/11≈10.45.
  4. (v) (15+0.5×30)/1.5=20; (vi) (15+0.5×15)/1.5=15.

Answer, in ₹ thousand/g: (i) 22.5, (ii) 25, (iii) 12.5, (iv) ≈10.45, (v) 20, (vi) 15.

Question 6 · Doubling every weight

Question: What happens to a weighted mean if all weights are doubled? Justify algebraically.

  1. For values xᵢ and weights wᵢ, the original mean is Σwᵢxᵢ/Σwᵢ.
  2. After doubling, it is Σ(2wᵢ)xᵢ/Σ(2wᵢ)=2Σwᵢxᵢ/(2Σwᵢ).
  3. The factor 2 cancels, so the mean is unchanged. In fact, multiplying every weight by the same positive constant never changes the result.

Answer: No change.

Question 7 · Objects orbiting Earth

Question: Read the approximate stacked chart of payload and other objects orbiting Earth: estimate the 2003 total and doubling year, split the 2025 total into counts/shares, and describe the trends.

  1. (i) The 2003 bar is about 10,000 objects. The total reaches about twice this, 20,000, around 2021.
  2. (ii) In 2025, total ≈33,000: payload ≈17,000 (about 51%) and other objects ≈16,000 (about 49%). Percentages are 17,000/33,000≈51.5% and 16,000/33,000≈48.5%; rounded chart readings may vary.
  3. (iii) Both counts increase over time. Payload counts rise sharply in recent years; their share grows toward half, while the share of other objects falls even though their count continues to rise.

Answer: estimates—2003 ≈10,000; double around 2021; 2025 ≈17,000 payload and ≈16,000 other. Treat chart readings as approximate.

Question 8 · Playground percentages on a state map

Question: Evaluate four inferences from state/UT playground percentages and decide whether the nationwide percentage can be found from percentages alone.

  1. (a) Cannot conclude that more schools in Haryana have playgrounds than in Uttarakhand from 90% versus 78% alone: their total school counts are unknown.
  2. (b) Correct: 68% in Arunachal Pradesh is close to two-thirds (≈66.7%).
  3. (c) Cannot conclude that Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu has the greatest number of schools with playgrounds from its 99.8% rate: the map gives percentages, and total school counts are missing.
  4. (d) Given Maharashtra has more schools than Telangana and its rate is also higher (93% versus 74%), it has more schools with playgrounds.
  5. (ii) The nationwide percentage is a weighted mean of state rates weighted by each state/UT’s school count. Those counts are needed; a simple average of percentages is not enough.

Answer: (a) and (c) cannot be determined as statements about counts; (b) is supported; (d) follows from the given school-count and rate comparison. State/UT school counts are needed for the national rate.

Question 10 · Triathlon times and chart choice

Question: Three athletes’ swim/cycle/run times are (01:08, 05:00, 03:15), (01:05, 05:10, 03:35) and (01:22, 05:55, 03:50). Choose a chart and say which questions a 100% chart can answer.

  1. Convert to minutes and total each race: Athlete 1 =68+300+195=563 min (9 h 23 min); Athlete 2=65+310+215=590 min (9 h 50 min); Athlete 3=82+355+230=667 min (11 h 7 min).
  2. A stacked bar chart is suitable because it preserves total race time and shows segment contributions.
  3. A 100% chart hides totals, so it cannot show who finished first or which athlete had the greatest absolute running time. It can show (b), the fraction of Athlete 1’s race spent cycling: 300/563≈53.3%.

Answer: use a stacked chart; only (b) can be answered from the 100% chart alone.

Question 11 · Disability shares by age group

Question: Describe observations and questions prompted by a 100% stacked chart of disability categories across age groups 0–19, 20–39, 40–59 and 60+.

  1. Each bar represents 100% of the people in that age group, so compare shares within a group, not numbers of people across groups.
  2. From the approximate bands, seeing and movement-related categories take a larger combined share in older groups; speech-related share is notably smaller among older groups. Hearing-related share stays comparatively steady.
  3. The chart alone does not give group population sizes, so it cannot tell which age group contains the greatest number of people in any category. Useful follow-up questions include how these shares vary by gender, location or access to care.

Answer: report proportional patterns only; counts require the total number of people in each age group. Observations from the chart are approximate.

Question 14 · Combine family average ages

Question: Use each family’s mean age and family size to find the average age of all members in the class’s families.

  1. For family i, with nᵢ people and mean age aᵢ, its total age is nᵢaᵢ.
  2. Add these totals and divide by all members: combined mean = Σ(nᵢaᵢ)/Σnᵢ. Do not simply average the family means unless family sizes are equal.
  3. Example: family sizes 5,4,6 and means 28,35,30 give (5×28+4×35+6×30)/(5+4+6)=460/15≈30.67 years.

Answer: use the weighted mean formula; in the example, ≈30.67 years.

Question 15 · Add the same constant to every weight

Question: What happens if 1 is added to every weight in a weighted mean? Explain algebraically.

  1. Original mean μ=Σwᵢxᵢ/W, where W=Σwᵢ. New mean is μ′=(Σwᵢxᵢ+Σxᵢ)/(W+n).
  2. Since Σwᵢxᵢ=Wμ, μ′=(Wμ+n x̄)/(W+n), where x̄ is the simple mean. So adding 1 to every weight moves the result toward the simple mean; it is not generally unchanged.
  3. Example values 10,20 with weights 1,3 give (10+60)/4=17.5. Add 1 to each weight: (2×10+4×20)/6=100/6≈16.67.

Answer: the weighted mean generally changes, toward the simple mean of the values.

Question 16 · How animal-population shares may change

Question: A farm’s population shares are cows 60%, sheep 25%, chickens 15%. All three populations decrease. Which listed possibilities for the new shares can occur?

  1. The shares always sum to 100%, so they cannot all decrease or all increase. Thus (i) and (ii) are impossible.
  2. (iii) is possible if every count is reduced by the same factor. Example counts 120,50,30 can fall to 60,25,15; shares remain 60%,25%,15%.
  3. (iv) is possible: 120,50,30 can fall to 50,35,15. All counts fell, while cow share fell to 50%, sheep share rose to 35%, chicken share remained 15%.
  4. (v) is possible: 120,50,30 can fall to 65,30,5. Each count fell, but cow and sheep shares rise to 65% and 30%, while chicken share falls to 5%.

Answer: possibilities (iii), (iv) and (v).
