2.1 Introduction

Algebraic expressions use numbers, variables and operations. A polynomial in one variable is a sum of terms whose variable exponents are non-negative whole numbers; its degree is the greatest exponent with a non-zero coefficient. A constant non-zero polynomial has degree 0.

2.2 Linear Polynomials

A degree-1 polynomial has form \(ax+b\), with \(a\ne 0\). Substituting a value gives its output; setting it equal to a constant creates a linear equation. For example, \(2x+3=11\Rightarrow x=4\).

2.3 Exploring Linear Patterns

A sequence with constant consecutive difference is linear. If its first term is \(u_1\) and common difference is \(d\), then \(u_n=u_1+(n-1)d\), which is linear in \(n\).

2.4 Linear Growth and Linear Decay

In \(f(t)=at+b\), \(b\) is the starting value and \(a\) is the fixed change each time unit. Positive \(a\) models growth; negative \(a\) models decay. A model's realistic domain may end before its algebraic values become negative.

2.5 Linear Relationships

Two known input-output pairs can determine \(a\) and \(b\) in \(y=ax+b\). The coefficient \(a\) is change per unit input; \(b\) is the output at input 0.

2.6 Visualising Linear Relationships

Graph facts: \(y=ax+b\) is a straight line with slope \(a\), y-intercept \((0,b)\), and x-intercept \((-b/a,0)\) when \(a\ne 0\). Two points determine its graph.

Same slope and different intercepts give parallel lines. If the intercept is 0, the line passes through the origin.

Question 1 · Degrees

Find the degree of each of the four given polynomials.

The degree is the highest power with a non-zero coefficient. For a non-zero constant, the exponent is 0.

  • (i) \(2x^2-5x+3\): degree 2.
  • (ii) \(y^3+2y-1\): degree 3.
  • (iii) \(-9\): degree 0.
  • (iv) \(4z-3\): degree 1.

Answers: 2, 3, 0, 1 respectively.

Question 1 · Evaluate a linear polynomial

Evaluate 5x − 3 at x = 0, −1 and 2.

Substitute each value in \(5x-3\).

  • (i) \(x=0:\ 5(0)-3=-3\).
  • (ii) \(x=-1:\ 5(-1)-3=-5-3=-8\).
  • (iii) \(x=2:\ 5(2)-3=10-3=7\).

Answers: (i) \(-3\), (ii) \(-8\), (iii) \(7\).

Question 2 · Evaluate a quadratic polynomial

Evaluate 7s² − 4s + 6 at s = 0, −3 and 4.

Substitute into \(7s^2-4s+6\), keeping a negative input in parentheses.

  • (i) \(s=0:\ 6\).
  • (ii) \(s=-3:\ 7(9)-4(-3)+6=63+12+6=81\).
  • (iii) \(s=4:\ 7(16)-16+6=112-16+6=102\).

Answers: 6, 81, 102.

Question 3 · Present ages

Use the stated age relationship to find Salil and his mother present ages, then their age sum after five years.

Let Salil's present age be \(s\). His mother's age is \(3s\); after five years their ages are \(s+5\) and \(3s+5\).

  1. \((s+5)+(3s+5)=70\).
  2. \(4s+10=70\Rightarrow4s=60\Rightarrow s=15\).
  3. Mother's age is \(3(15)=45\).

Answer: Salil is 15 years old; his mother is 45 years old. Five years later their ages sum to 70.

Question 5 · Two-rupee and five-rupee coins

Find the counts of two-rupee and five-rupee coins from their stated count ratio and total value.

Let the number of five-rupee coins be \(x\), so the number of two-rupee coins is \(3x\). The total value equation is \(5x+2(3x)=88\).

  1. \(11x=88\Rightarrow x=8\).
  2. Two-rupee coins: \(3x=24\).
  3. Check: \(8(5)+24(2)=40+48=88\).

Answer: 8 five-rupee coins and 24 two-rupee coins.

Question 6 · Dividing a fence

Split a 300-foot fence into two pieces when the longer piece is four times the shorter.

Let the shorter piece be \(x\) feet; the longer is \(4x\). Since the total is 300 feet, \(x+4x=300\).

Thus \(5x=300\), so \(x=60\); the longer piece is \(4(60)=240\).

Answer: 60 ft and 240 ft; they total 300 ft and the longer is four times the shorter.

Question 7 · Rectangle dimensions

Find rectangle dimensions when its length is three more than twice its width and its perimeter is 24 cm.

Let width be \(w\) cm. Then length is \(2w+3\). The perimeter equation is \(2(l+w)=24\).

  1. \(2((2w+3)+w)=24\Rightarrow 6w+6=24\).
  2. \(6w=18\Rightarrow w=3\) cm.
  3. \(l=2(3)+3=9\) cm.

Answer: Width 3 cm; length 9 cm. Check: \(2(3+9)=24\).

Question 1 · Plant growth

A plant starts at 1.75 feet and grows 0.5 feet monthly; find its height after seven months and complete a table.

Starting height 1.75 ft, increasing by 0.5 ft/month: \(h(t)=1.75+0.5t\).

  1. At 7 months: \(h(7)=1.75+3.5=5.25\) ft.
  2. For \(t=0,1,\ldots,10\), heights are 1.75, 2.25, 2.75, 3.25, 3.75, 4.25, 4.75, 5.25, 5.75, 6.25, 6.75 ft.
  3. The increase is always 0.5 ft per month, so this is linear growth.

Answer: \(h=1.75+0.5t\); after 7 months, 5.25 ft.

Question 2 · Phone depreciation

A ₹10,000 phone loses ₹800 of value yearly; find its value after three years and tabulate the pattern.

\(v(t)=10000-800t\) rupees, where \(t\) is years.

  1. After 3 years: \(v(3)=10000-2400=7600\) rupees.
  2. At \(t=0,1,\ldots,8\), values are ₹10,000, ₹9,200, ₹8,400, ₹7,600, ₹6,800, ₹6,000, ₹5,200, ₹4,400, ₹3,600.
  3. The value drops by ₹800 each year, so it is linear decay.

Answer: \(v=10000-800t\); after 3 years, ₹7,600.

Question 3 · Village population

Model a population that starts at 750 and grows by 50 yearly; find the population after six years and make a table.

\(P(t)=750+50t\), where \(t\) is years.

  1. After 6 years: \(P(6)=750+300=1050\).
  2. For \(t=0\) through 10, the population values are 750, 800, 850, 900, 950, 1000, 1050, 1100, 1150, 1200, 1250.
  3. The increase is 50 each year, so this is linear growth.

Answer: \(P=750+50t\); after 6 years, 1,050 people.

Question 4 · Prepaid balance decay

A prepaid balance of ₹600 decreases by ₹15 daily; find when it runs out and tabulate the pattern.

Starting balance ₹600 and daily reduction ₹15 give \(b(x)=600-15x\).

  1. Set the balance to zero: \(600-15x=0\Rightarrow x=40\) days.

  3. The balance falls by ₹15 daily, so the model is linear decay.

Question 1 · Learning platform bill

Use bills for 10 and 14 modules to find a learning platform fixed fee and per-module rate.

Pairs are \((10,400)\) and \((14,500)\): \(400=10a+b\), \(500=14a+b\).

  2. \(b=400-10(25)=150\).

Answer: \(a=25,b=150\); \(y=25x+150\).

Question 3 · Celsius as a function of Fahrenheit

Use the freezing and boiling points to determine the linear relationship between Celsius and Fahrenheit temperatures.

The equation is \(C=aF+b\). The melting point gives \(0=32a+b\); the boiling point gives \(100=212a+b\).

  1. Subtract: \(100=180a\Rightarrow a=\frac{5}{9}\).
  2. \(b=-32(\frac{5}{9})=-\frac{160}{9}\).
  3. Therefore \(C=\frac{5}{9}F-\frac{160}{9}=\frac{5}{9}(F-32)\).

Answer: \(a=\frac{5}{9},b=-\frac{160}{9}\); \(C=\frac{5}{9}(F-32)\).

Question 3 · Solving a number equation

Solve the stated number condition involving multiplication and addition.

Let the number be \(x\). “Multiply by \(\frac{5}{2}\), then add \(\frac{2}{3}\)” gives \(\frac{5}{2}x+\frac{2}{3}=-\frac{7}{12}\).

Question 4 · Two related positive numbers

Find two positive numbers in ratio 2:5 with difference 63, then examine the effect of adding 21.

Let the smaller number be \(2k\) and the larger be \(5k\), as specified by the ratio 2:5. Their difference is \(5k-2k=63\), so \(3k=63\) and \(k=21\).

  1. The numbers are \(2(21)=42\) and \(5(21)=105\).
  2. Add 21 to each: 63 and 126.
  3. Check: \(126=2(63)\), so one new number is twice the other.

Answer: 42 and 105. Their difference is 63, their ratio is 2:5, and after adding 21 the larger is twice the smaller.

Question 6 · Reversing two digits Starred

Find the two-digit numbers whose digits differ by 3 and whose sum with the reversed number is 143.

Let tens digit be \(a\), units digit \(b\). The two numbers are \(10a+b\) and \(10b+a\). Their sum is \(11(a+b)=143\), so \(a+b=13\). Their digits differ by 3, so \(|a-b|=3\).

Solving gives digit pairs \((a,b)=(8,5)\) or \((5,8)\).

Answer: The numbers are 85 and 58. They sum to 143 and their digits differ by 3.

Question 8 · Kelvin and Fahrenheit Starred

Convert 313 K to Fahrenheit and 158°F to Kelvin.

Use \(F=\frac{9}{5}(K-273)+32\).

  1. At \(K=313\): \(F=\frac{9}{5}(40)+32=72+32=104^\circ F\).
  2. For \(F=158\): \(158-32=\frac{9}{5}(K-273)\), so \(126×\frac{5}{9}=70=K-273\), giving \(K=343\).

Answer: 313 K = 104°F; 158°F = 343 K under the textbook's 273 offset.

For the given force, relate work to distance, graph the relation and find work at distance 2.

With constant force 3 units, work is force times distance: \(w=3d\). This is linear in \((d,w)\).

  1. At \(d=0\), \(w=0\); at \(d=2\), \(w=6\).
  2. Plot \((0,0)\) and \((2,6)\) and join with a straight line.
  3. Substitution check: \(6=3(2)\).

Answer: \(w=3d\); at distance 2, work is 6 units.

Question 10 · Recovering a linear polynomial Starred

Find a polynomial passing through (1, 5) and (3, 11), its intercepts, and its graph.

Let \(p(x)=ax+b\). The points give \(a+b=5\) and \(3a+b=11\).

  1. Subtract: \(2a=6\Rightarrow a=3\), so \(b=2\). Hence \(p(x)=3x+2\).
  2. For x-intercept set \(y=0\): \(3x+2=0\Rightarrow x=-\frac{2}{3}\).
  3. For y-intercept set \(x=0\): \(y=2\).
  4. Graph through the given points \((1,5),(3,11)\) and intercepts \((-\frac{2}{3},0),(0,2)\).

Answer: \(p(x)=3x+2\); intercepts \((-\frac{2}{3},0)\) and \((0,2)\).

Question 11 · Determine two polynomials Starred

Determine p and q from the stated polynomial value, root and sum conditions.

From \(p(0)=5\), write \(p(x)=ax+5\). Since \(p-q\) has root 3, \(p(3)=q(3)\). Also \(p+q=6x+4\).

  1. At \(x=3\), the sum is 22 and the two equal values must each be 11.
  2. \(p(3)=3a+5=11\Rightarrow a=2\).
  3. Then \(q=6x+4-p=4x-1\).

Answer: \(p(x)=2x+5\), \(q(x)=4x-1\). Their difference \(-2x+6\) is zero at 3.

Question 12 · Hexagons and matchsticks Starred

Find the matchstick rule for a chain of hexagons, the total for 15, and whether 200 is possible.

For the straight chain described, one new hexagon shares one side with the previous hexagon. The first needs 6 sticks; each additional one contributes 5.

  1. Stages 1–5 use 6, 11, 16, 21, 26 sticks.
  2. General term: \(m_n=6+5(n-1)=5n+1\).
  3. Stage 15: \(5(15)+1=76\).
  4. For 200 sticks, \(5n+1=200\Rightarrow n=39.8\), not a whole stage number.

Answer: Next stages need 21 and 26 sticks; \(m_n=5n+1\); Stage 15 uses 76; 200 cannot form a complete stage.

Question 13 · Parallel polynomial graphs Starred

Find two parallel polynomials through the given points and their x-intercepts.

For \(p(x)=ax+b\), the two points give slope \(a=\frac{11-3}{6-2}=2\), then \(3=2(2)+b\Rightarrow b=-1\). Thus \(p=2x-1\).

Since q is parallel, its slope is also 2. Passing through \((4,-1)\) gives \(-1=2(4)+d\Rightarrow d=-9\), so \(q=2x-9\).

  • q meets x-axis when \(2x-9=0\): \((\frac{9}{2},0)\).

Question 14 · Family \(f(x)=ax+a\), \(a>0\) Starred

Describe the common feature of the line family f(x) = ax + a for positive a.

Factor: \(f(x)=a(x+1)\). For every positive a, the zero is \(x=-1\); hence every graph passes through \((-1,0)\). Its y-intercept is \((0,a)\), and slope is a.

Answer: All lines pass through \((-1,0)\), with positive slopes and y-intercepts \((0,a)\). Distinct positive a values produce distinct, non-parallel lines.

Common mistakes and examination tips

  • Degree: use the highest power with a non-zero coefficient, not the number of terms.
  • Missing terms: the coefficient of an absent power is 0.
  • Negative substitution: put the negative value in parentheses before squaring.
  • Word problems: define the variable and its units before forming an equation.
  • Patterns: distinguish the starting term from the first change; check the formula against given terms.
  • Growth or decay: respect realistic domains and avoid interpreting negative counts or balances literally.
  • Graphing: isolate y first; then report slope and y-intercept from the simplified equation.
  • Parallel lines: equal slopes imply parallel lines only when intercepts differ.
Final check: substitute answers back into each condition, preserve signs and units, and show enough working to make the model and arithmetic clear.
