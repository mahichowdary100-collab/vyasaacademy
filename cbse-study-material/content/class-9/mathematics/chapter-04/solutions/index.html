Chapter overview

Chapter 4 develops algebraic identities, their geometric meaning, and their use in expansion, factorisation, mental calculation, and simplifying rational expressions.

  • \((a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2\) and \((a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2\)
  • \((a+b+c)^2=a^2+b^2+c^2+2ab+2bc+2ca\)
  • \((a+b)(a-b)=a^2-b^2\), and identities for cubes
  • Factorisation by pattern matching, algebra tiles, and splitting the middle term
  • Cancel common factors in rational expressions while preserving domain restrictions

Question 1 · Expanding six binomial squares

Expand six two-term squares with the square-of-a-sum identity, including fractional, decimal, and reciprocal terms.

  1. \((7x+4y)^2=49x^2+56xy+16y^2\).
  2. \((\frac75x+\frac32y)^2=\frac{49}{25}x^2+\frac{21}{5}xy+\frac94y^2\).
  3. \((2.5p+1.5q)^2=6.25p^2+7.5pq+2.25q^2\).
  4. \((\frac34s+8t)^2=\frac9{16}s^2+12st+64t^2\).
  5. \((x+\frac1{2y})^2=x^2+\frac{x}{y}+\frac1{4y^2}\).
  6. \((\frac1x+\frac1y)^2=\frac1{x^2}+\frac2{xy}+\frac1{y^2}\).

Each follows \( (A+B)^2=A^2+2AB+B^2\). The reciprocal forms require \(x\ne0\) and \(y\ne0\).

Answers: The six expansions are listed above.

Question 4 · Testing a proposed identity

Decide whether the stated sum of three squared expressions equals twice the sum of the three individual squares for all variables.

Expand the left-hand side:

\((a+b-c)^2+(a-b+c)^2+(a-b-c)^2=3a^2+3b^2+3c^2-2ab-2ac-2bc\).

This is not generally \(2a^2+2b^2+2c^2\). A single counterexample is \(a=1,\ b=0,\ c=0\): the left side is \(3\) and the right side is \(2\).

Answer: It is not an identity.

Question 1 · Simplifying six rational expressions

Factor each numerator and denominator, cancel common nonzero factors, and preserve the original denominator restrictions.

  1. \(\frac{3p^2-3pq-18q^2}{p^2+3pq-10q^2}=\frac{3(p-3q)(p+2q)}{(p+5q)(p-2q)}\). No factor cancels. Require \(p\ne-5q,2q\).
  2. \(\frac{n^3-3n^2m+3nm^2-m^3}{5m^2-10mn+5n^2}=\frac{(n-m)^3}{5(n-m)^2}=\frac{n-m}{5}\), with \(n\ne m\).
  3. Let \(A=w+x-v\). The numerator is \((w+x-v)(w^2+x^2+v^2-wx+wv+xv)\); the denominator is \(A^2\). The result is \(\frac{w^2+x^2+v^2-wx+wv+xv}{w+x-v}\), with \(w+x-v\ne0\).
  4. \(\frac{(2y-5z)^2}{(5z-2y)(5z+2y)}=\frac{5z-2y}{5z+2y}\), with \(5z\ne2y\) and \(5z\ne-2y\).
  5. Factor all quadratics: numerator \((x+3)(x-2)(x-3)(x-4)\); denominator \((x-2)(x-4)(x-3)(x+3)\). The value is \(1\), for \(x\notin\{-3,2,3,4\}\).
  6. \(\frac{p^4-16}{p^2-4p+4}=\frac{(p-2)(p+2)(p^2+4)}{(p-2)^2}=\frac{(p+2)(p^2+4)}{p-2}\), with \(p\ne2\).
Domain check: Cancellation simplifies a formula; it does not restore values that made the original denominator zero.

Answers: The simplified forms and restrictions are given in each part.

Question 4 · Simplifying three rational expressions

Question 5 · Finding rectangle dimensions from area

Question 7 · Area of a path around a square

Express the area of an outer walking path of width \(s\) around a square playground whose side is 40 metres.

The outer square has side \(40+2s\), because the path adds width \(s\) on each of two opposite sides. Subtract the playground area:

\((40+2s)^2-40^2=1600+160s+4s^2-1600=4s^2+160s\).

Answer: \(4s^2+160s\text{ m}^2\).

Question 8 · A number and its reciprocal

Find the real number values when a nonzero number plus its reciprocal is \(10/3\).

Let the number be \(x\ne0\). Then \(x+\frac1x=\frac{10}{3}\). Multiplying by \(3x\) gives \(3x^2-10x+3=0=(3x-1)(x-3)\).

Thus \(x=\frac13\) or \(x=3\); each value satisfies the original equation.

Answer: \(3\) or \(\frac13\).

Question 9 · Length of a rectangular pool

Find the pool’s length from its quadratic area expression and given linear width.

Factor the area: \(2x^2+7x+3=(2x+1)(x+3)\). The width is \(2x+1\), so the other factor gives the length.

Answer: \(x+3\) hastas.

Question 10 · Comparing two roots of a quadratic Challenge

Use the two stated linear factors of \(px^2+5x+r\) to show its leading and constant coefficients are equal.

Since the polynomial has roots \(2\) and \(\frac12\), the product of its roots is \(\frac{r}{p}\) (with \(p\ne0\)). But \(2\cdot\frac12=1\). Hence \(\frac rp=1\), so \(r=p\).

Answer: \(p=r\).

Question 11 · Using a three-variable cube identity Challenge

Use \(a+b+c=5\) and \(ab+bc+ca=10\) to evaluate the cubic expression.

Apply \(a^3+b^3+c^3-3abc=(a+b+c)((a+b+c)^2-3(ab+bc+ca))\).

\(5(5^2-3\cdot10)=5(25-30)=-25\).

Answer: \(-25\).

Question 12 · Proving divisibility by six Challenge

Factor \(n^3-n\) and explain why the result is divisible by 6 for every natural number \(n\).

\(n^3-n=n(n-1)(n+1)\), the product of three consecutive integers. One is divisible by 3, and at least one is even. Therefore their product is divisible by \(2\cdot3=6\).

Answer: \(6\mid(n^3-n)\) for every natural number \(n\).

Question 13 · Evaluating two cubic expressions under constraints Challenge

Evaluate each expression using the three-variable identity and its given linear condition.

  1. \(x^3+y^3-12xy+64=x^3+y^3+4^3-3xy(4)\). It factors with \(x+y+4\), which is zero when \(x+y=-4\). The value is \(0\).
  2. \(x^3-8y^3-36xy-216=x^3+(-2y)^3+(-6)^3-3x(-2y)(-6)\). Its sum factor is \(x-2y-6=0\) when \(x=2y+6\). The value is \(0\).

Answers: (i) \(0\); (ii) \(0\).

Common mistakes and exam tips

  • In square and cube expansions, keep every cross term and its sign.
  • After factoring, multiply the factors back out to verify the expression.
  • In rational expressions, cancel common factors, never individual terms across addition.
  • Keep original denominator restrictions after cancellation.
  • For an outside path around a square, add the path width on both sides of each dimension.
