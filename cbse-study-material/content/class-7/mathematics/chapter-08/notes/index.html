Fractions as quantities, numbers and operators

Half a pizza is part of a whole. Sharing three chocolate bars equally among four people gives \(\frac{3}{4}\) of a bar each, so a fraction can also describe division. On a number line \(\frac{3}{4}\) is a number between zero and one. In “\(\frac{3}{4}\) of a 20-minute session,” the same fraction is an operator that gives 15 minutes.

Fractions let us scale recipes, measure distances, divide money fairly and describe parts of a practice session. In this chapter you will use pictures to explain multiplication and division, calculate with mixed numbers and check answers with benchmarks.

In \(\frac{a}{b}\), the numerator a counts parts and the denominator b tells how many equal parts make one whole. A zero numerator means no parts were taken. A zero denominator makes division undefined. For example, there is no number that multiplied by zero gives 3, and zero divided by zero has no single quotient.

FormMeaningExample
Unit fractionNumerator is 1\(\frac{1}{7}\)
Proper fractionNumerator is smaller than denominator\(\frac{5}{7}\)
Improper fractionNumerator is at least as large as denominator\(\frac{9}{7}\)
Mixed numberWhole number plus a proper fractional part\(1\,\frac{2}{7}\)

\(1\,\frac{2}{7} = \frac{1\times 7+2}{7} = \frac{9}{7}\)

To reverse the conversion, divide 9 by 7. The quotient 1 counts wholes, and the remainder 2 counts sevenths. In a number line each unit has the same length, even past 1.

Review · Equivalence, comparison, addition and subtraction

Equivalent fractions and simplest form

Splitting every part into the same number of smaller parts changes both the count of shaded parts and the total count. It preserves the fraction’s value. Dividing numerator and denominator by their greatest common factor returns the simplest name.

\(\frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{4}{8}\)

Comparison

With equal denominators, compare numerators. With equal positive numerators, the smaller denominator gives larger parts. For different numerators and denominators, use a common denominator. For example, \(\frac{5}{6}\) and \(\frac{3}{4}\) become \(\frac{10}{12}\) and \(\frac{9}{12}\). Zero, one half and one are useful benchmarks. On the number line, values increase to the right. In ascending order, write \(\frac{1}{4} < \frac{1}{2} < \frac{5}{6}\); reverse that order for descending.

Add and subtract pieces of the same size

Keep the denominator when adding or subtracting like fractions. For unlike fractions, choose a common denominator, preferably the least common multiple. Two thirds plus one fourth becomes eight twelfths plus three twelfths. The twelfths are all the same size, so their counts add.

\(\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{8}{12} + \frac{3}{12} = \frac{11}{12}\)

\(\frac{5}{6} - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{10}{12} - \frac{3}{12} = \frac{7}{12}\)

For mixed numbers, convert both to improper fractions or work with the whole and fractional parts. For \(3\,\frac{1}{4} - 1\,\frac{3}{4}\), regroup one whole as four quarters to get \(2\,\frac{5}{4} - 1\,\frac{3}{4} = 1\,\frac{2}{4} = 1\,\frac{1}{2}\). This uses equal quarter pieces throughout.

8.1 · Multiplication of fractions

A whole number of equal fractional groups

Four lengths of \(\frac{2}{3}\) metre contain eight thirds. Write \(4\times \frac{2}{3}=\frac{8}{3}=2\,\frac{2}{3}\). To find a fraction of a whole number, divide into equal parts first: \(\frac{3}{5}\times 20\) means split 20 into fifths of 4 and take three, giving 12. Likewise, \(\frac{2}{7}\times 35=10\).

A fraction of a fraction

To find two thirds of three quarters, mark three columns out of four. Then mark two rows out of three. The rectangle has 12 equal cells and six belong to both selections, giving \(\frac{6}{12}=\frac{1}{2}\). The row count and column count multiply, and so do the selected row and column counts.

\(\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a\times c}{b\times d}\)

For positive fractions, multiplying by a factor between zero and one shrinks the starting amount. A factor of one keeps it unchanged, and a factor above one enlarges it. Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions before multiplying, or distribute a factor across the whole and fractional parts. A factor common to a numerator and a denominator can be cancelled before multiplying because it divides the product’s top and bottom equally.

\(1\,\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{2}{3} = \frac{3}{2} \times \frac{2}{3} = 1\)

Fold a fraction of a fraction

Goal. Discover the overlap rule with one sheet of paper.

  1. Mark three equally spaced folds to make four equal columns. Colour three columns lightly.
  2. In the other direction make three equal rows and stripe two rows.
  3. Count cells in both colours and compare with the total cells.
Reveal the observation and conclusion

Six of twelve cells have both colours. In a b-row by d-column grid the two selections overlap in a × c of b × d cells.

Try next. Predict the overlap for \(\frac{3}{4}\) of \(\frac{2}{5}\) before drawing it.

Compare the rectangular sides and the product

Goal. Use a unit grid for proper and improper side lengths.

  1. Draw a rectangle \(\frac{3}{2}\) units high and \(\frac{2}{3}\) units wide. Mark half-unit horizontal bands and third-unit vertical bands.
  2. Compare each cell with the unit square. Count the cells and add their areas.

Known multiplication properties still apply

Switch the two factors to see the commutative property by rotating an area grid. In a product of three fractions, grouping either pair first gives the same numerator and denominator products. Distributing multiplication over a sum splits a rectangle into two adjacent rectangles.

\(\frac{2}{3}\times (1+\frac{1}{4})=\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{6}=\frac{5}{6}\). A factor of 1 preserves the amount. Zero leaves zero groups or zero overlap.

8.2 · Division of fractions

Sharing and measuring

Sharing \(\frac{3}{4}\) litre among two people asks for the size of each share. Split each quarter in half. There are six eighths and each person gets \(\frac{3}{8}\) litre. Measuring asks how many groups of a fixed size fit. Six litres contain twelve half-litre portions, so \(6\div \frac{1}{2}=12\).

Measure with equal-sized pieces

To find \(\frac{2}{3} \div \frac{4}{5}\), express both quantities in fifteenths. The dividend contains ten pieces and a full divisor-sized group contains twelve pieces. Ten pieces make ten twelfths of such a group, so the quotient is five sixths.

\(\frac{2}{3} \div \frac{4}{5} = \frac{10}{12} = \frac{5}{6}\)

In general, rewrite both fractions as counts of pieces of size \(\frac{1}{bd}\). Then divide the dividend count ad by the divisor count bc. The counts give the same calculation as multiplying by the reciprocal of the divisor.

\(\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{ad}{bc} = \frac{a}{b} \times \frac{d}{c}\)

Another check is to multiply the quotient by the divisor. For example, \(\frac{5}{6}\times \frac{4}{5}=\frac{2}{3}\). The inverse works because \(\frac{4}{5}\times \frac{5}{4}=1\). A nonzero fraction, whole number or improper fraction has a reciprocal. Convert a mixed number before finding its reciprocal. Zero has no reciprocal because multiplying by zero cannot give one. A divisor must be nonzero.

A positive divisor less than one gives more groups than the same number of unit groups, so the quotient is larger than the dividend. With a divisor larger than one, the quotient is smaller. A quotient can still be below one, as when one divisor-sized group exceeds the dividend.

Compare a quotient with its divisor separately. For instance, \(\frac{1}{4}\div \frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\) equals the divisor, and \(\frac{1}{8}\div \frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{4}\) is below it. Multiplying the proposed quotient by the divisor is a reliable check.

Jump along a number line

Goal. Compare complete and partial groups.

  1. Draw a line from zero to 2 and mark every fourth.
  2. Make jumps of \(\frac{3}{4}\) from zero until another full jump would pass 2.
  3. Measure the distance still uncovered as a fraction of a single \(\frac{3}{4}\) jump.
Reveal the observation and conclusion

Two full jumps reach \(1\,\frac{1}{2}\). The remaining \(\frac{1}{2}\) is two thirds of a full three-quarter jump. Therefore 2 ÷ \(\frac{3}{4}\) = \(2\,\frac{2}{3}\).

Try next. Try \(1\,\frac{1}{4}\) ÷ \(\frac{1}{2}\) and label the part of the last jump that fits.

8.3 · Some problems involving fractions

Identify what the question asks you to find. Combine amounts with addition, compare or remove amounts with subtraction, scale an amount by multiplication, and share or count groups by division.

Multiple operations and estimation

Work inside brackets first. Then perform multiplication and division from left to right, followed by addition and subtraction from left to right. Estimate with zero, one half and one before calculating. For example, adding \(\frac{5}{6}\) and \(\frac{7}{8}\) gives just under 2, while subtracting them gives a small difference. \(= \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{2}{3} = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{5}{6}\)

Fractions in Indian mathematics

The chapter traces non-unit fractions through the Śulbasūtras and fractional arithmetic through Brahmagupta’s Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta (628 CE). It illustrates his multiplication and division rules, reducing common factors, and later explanations by Bhāskara I and Bhāskara II.

Try a related tiling problem. A square tile with side \(\frac{1}{4}\) unit covers \(\frac{1}{16}\) square unit. A rectangular surface with area \(3\,\frac{1}{2}\) square units needs \(\frac{7}{2}\div \frac{1}{16}=56\) tile areas. For a \(2\times 1\,\frac{3}{4}\) rectangle, the rows-and-columns check gives eight by seven tiles.

Investigate branching and a product pattern

Goal. Keep track of which whole each fraction refers to.

  1. Draw a branch splitting a group in halves. Split one half again, then join the untouched half and just one quarter at the same destination.
  2. Now expand the factors (1 − \(\frac{1}{2}\)), (1 − \(\frac{1}{3}\)), (1 − \(\frac{1}{4}\)) and (1 − \(\frac{1}{5}\)). Multiply the reduced fractions in sequence.
Reveal the observation and conclusion

The destination gets \(\frac{1}{2}\) + \(\frac{1}{4}\) = \(\frac{3}{4}\) of the original group. The product is (\(\frac{1}{2}\)) × (\(\frac{2}{3}\)) × (\(\frac{3}{4}\)) × (\(\frac{4}{5}\)) = \(\frac{1}{5}\). Each intermediate denominator cancels the next numerator. Ending at (1 − 1/n) leaves the fraction 1/n.

Try next. Extend the product to (1 − \(\frac{1}{8}\)) and check your prediction.

Chapter puzzle · Non-attacking queens

Goal. Place queens with no shared row, column or diagonal.

  2. Place one queen in each successive row. Reject any column already occupied and any square on a marked diagonal.
  3. Repeat on an 8 by 8 board.
Reveal the observation and conclusion

One 4 by 4 solution uses columns 2, 4, 1, 3 in row order. One 8 by 8 solution uses columns 2, 4, 6, 8, 3, 1, 7, 5. In each arrangement the columns, row-minus-column values and row-plus-column values are all distinct.

Important rules at a glance

Equivalent fractions

\(\frac{a}{b} = \frac{ak}{bk}\)

Multiply both counts by the same positive integer k. To simplify, divide both by a common factor.

\(\frac{1}{2} = \frac{4}{8}\)

Watch for. Changing only one count changes the value.

Compare with equal units

\(\frac{5}{6} > \frac{3}{4}\)

Convert to a common denominator or use the same unit interval.

\(\frac{10}{12} > \frac{9}{12}\)

Watch for. A larger denominator alone does not make a larger fraction.

Add

\(\frac{a}{m}+\frac{b}{m}=\frac{a+b}{m}\)

Match the denominators before adding.

\(\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{11}{12}\)

Watch for. Keep the equal-piece size fixed.

Subtract

\(\frac{a}{m}-\frac{b}{m}=\frac{a-b}{m}\)

Match the denominators before subtracting. Regroup mixed numbers when needed.

\(\frac{5}{6} - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{7}{12}\)

Watch for. Subtract the piece counts only.

Multiply

\(\frac{a}{b}\times \frac{c}{d}=\frac{ac}{bd}\)

Use positive denominators. An area model counts equal overlapping cells.

\(\frac{2}{3}\times \frac{3}{4}=\frac{1}{2}\)

Watch for. Cancel only common factors across the product.

Reciprocal

\(\frac{a}{b}\times \frac{b}{a}=1\)

Both numerator and denominator must be nonzero.

\(\frac{3}{5}\times \frac{5}{3}=1\)

Watch for. Zero has no reciprocal.

Divide

\(\frac{a}{b}\div \frac{c}{d}=\frac{a}{b}\times \frac{d}{c}\)

Use a nonzero divisor and multiply by its reciprocal.

\(\frac{2}{3}\div \frac{4}{5}=\frac{5}{6}\)

Watch for. Leave the dividend in its original order.

Mixed and improper forms

\(w+\frac r d=\frac{wd+r}{d}\)

Use whole units w and a remainder r smaller than d. Divide the improper numerator by d to reverse.

\(1\,\frac{2}{7}=\frac{9}{7}\)

Watch for. Multiply the whole number by the denominator first.

Estimate and check

\(0<\frac{3}{4}\times \frac{2}{3}<\frac{3}{4}\)

Each proper positive factor takes part of the other amount. Reverse the operation to check division.

\(\frac{2}{3}\times \frac{3}{4}=\frac{1}{2}\)

Watch for. Decide how the fraction changes the starting value.

30 step-by-step worked examples

Basic

Example 1 · Read a shaded model

Given. A circle has eight equal pieces and five are shaded.

Find. Write the shaded fraction.

Rule. Count selected and total equal pieces.

  1. The total is 8, so the part size is one eighth.
  2. The shaded count is 5.

Answer. \(\frac{5}{8}\)

Example 2 · Locate an improper fraction

Given. The number line has seven equal parts per unit.

Find. Locate \(\frac{9}{7}\).

Rule. Each step represents one seventh.

  1. Seven steps reach 1.
  2. Take two more steps.

Answer. \(1\,\frac{2}{7}\)

Example 3 · Simplify a fraction

Given. \(\frac{18}{24}\)

Find. Give the simplest form.

Rule. Divide both terms by their greatest common factor.

  1. Both 18 and 24 are divisible by 6.
  2. 18 ÷ 6 = 3 and 24 ÷ 6 = 4.

Answer. \(\frac{3}{4}\)

Example 4 · Compare two fractions

Given. \(\frac{3}{8}\) and \(\frac{5}{8}\)

Find. Find the larger fraction.

Rule. Compare counts of same-sized pieces.

  1. Both fractions count eighths.
  2. Five eighths is farther to the right than three eighths.

Answer. \(\frac{5}{8}\)

Example 5 · Add like fractions

Given. \(\frac{3}{10}\) and \(\frac{4}{10}\)

Find. Find the sum.

Rule. Add counts and keep the unit size.

  1. There are 3 + 4 = 7 pieces altogether.
  2. Each piece is one tenth. 7 and 10 have no common factor greater than one.

Answer. \(\frac{7}{10}\)

Example 6 · Convert a mixed number

Given. \(2\,\frac{3}{5}\)

Find. Write an improper fraction.

Rule. Replace each whole with five fifths.

  1. Two wholes give ten fifths.
  2. Add the extra three fifths.

Answer. \(\frac{13}{5}\)

Example 7 · Multiply repeated groups

Given. Four bands, each \(\frac{2}{3}\) m long

Find. Find their total length.

Rule. Add four copies of two thirds.

  1. 4 × 2 = 8 thirds.
  2. Write \(\frac{8}{3}\) as two wholes and two thirds.

Answer. \(2\,\frac{2}{3}\) m

Example 8 · Find a fraction of a class

Given. \(\frac{3}{5}\) of 20 students

Find. Find the number of students.

Rule. Divide into equal groups, then count the selected groups.

  1. 20 ÷ 5 = 4 students per fifth.
  2. 3 × 4 = 12 students.

Answer. 12 students

Example 9 · Find a reciprocal

Given. \(2\,\frac{1}{3}\)

Find. Find the reciprocal.

Rule. The product of a nonzero number and its reciprocal is one.

  1. Convert \(2\,\frac{1}{3}\) to \(\frac{7}{3}\).
  2. Reverse numerator and denominator. Check \(\frac{7}{3}\) × \(\frac{3}{7}\) = 1.

Answer. \(\frac{3}{7}\)

Intermediate

Example 10 · Compare unlike fractions

Given. \(\frac{7}{10}\) and \(\frac{2}{3}\)

Find. Find which value is greater.

Rule. Use equal-sized pieces.

  1. LCM of 10 and 3 is 30.
  2. \(\frac{7}{10}\) = \(\frac{21}{30}\) and \(\frac{2}{3}\) = \(\frac{20}{30}\).
  3. 21 is greater than 20.

Answer. \(\frac{7}{10}\) > \(\frac{2}{3}\)

Example 11 · Order fractions

Given. \(\frac{3}{4}\), \(\frac{2}{5}\) and \(\frac{5}{8}\)

Find. Write the fractions in ascending order.

Rule. Choose the common denominator 40.

  1. \(\frac{3}{4}\) = \(\frac{30}{40}\), \(\frac{2}{5}\) = \(\frac{16}{40}\) and \(\frac{5}{8}\) = \(\frac{25}{40}\).
  2. Arrange the numerators 16, 25, 30 and restore the original names.

Answer. \(\frac{2}{5}\) < \(\frac{5}{8}\) < \(\frac{3}{4}\)

Example 12 · Add two mixed quantities

Given. \(1\,\frac{3}{4}\) L and \(2\,\frac{2}{3}\) L

Find. Find the total.

Rule. Convert to improper fractions, then add like pieces.

  1. The amounts are \(\frac{7}{4}\) L and \(\frac{8}{3}\) L.
  2. Use twelfths: \(\frac{21}{12}\) + \(\frac{32}{12}\) = \(\frac{53}{12}\).
  3. Divide 53 by 12: quotient 4, remainder 5.

Answer. \(4\,\frac{5}{12}\) L

Example 13 · Subtract with regrouping

Given. A rope is \(3\,\frac{1}{5}\) m. Remove \(1\,\frac{4}{5}\) m.

Find. Find the length left.

Rule. Regroup a whole as five fifths.

  1. \(3\,\frac{1}{5}\) is 2 wholes and 6 fifths.
  2. Subtract 1 whole and 4 fifths separately. Equivalently, \(\frac{16}{5}\) − \(\frac{9}{5}\) = \(\frac{7}{5}\).

Answer. \(1\,\frac{2}{5}\) m

Example 14 · Read an area product

Given. \(\frac{2}{3}\) of \(\frac{3}{4}\)

Find. Find the shaded part of the original unit square.

Rule. Count cells in the overlap.

  1. There are 3 rows and 4 columns, for 12 cells.
  2. Two selected rows meet three selected columns in 6 cells.
  3. \(\frac{6}{12}\) simplifies by dividing by 6.

Answer. \(\frac{1}{2}\)

Example 15 · Cancel before multiplying

Given. \(\frac{14}{15}\) × \(\frac{25}{21}\)

Find. Find the product in simplest form.

Rule. Divide out common factors across the numerator and denominator.

  1. Divide 14 and 21 by 7 to get 2 and 3.
  2. Divide 25 and 15 by 5 to get 5 and 3.
  3. Multiply 2 × 5 and 3 × 3.

Answer. \(\frac{10}{9}\) = \(1\,\frac{1}{9}\)

Example 16 · Multiply mixed side lengths

Given. Rectangle sides \(1\,\frac{3}{5}\) m and \(2\,\frac{1}{4}\) m

Find. Find its area.

Rule. Convert side lengths first, then multiply.

  1. The sides are \(\frac{8}{5}\) m and \(\frac{9}{4}\) m.
  2. Cancel 8 and 4 by 4: \(\frac{2}{5}\) × 9 = \(\frac{18}{5}\).
  3. 18 fifths equal 3 wholes and 3 fifths.

Answer. \(3\,\frac{3}{5}\) square metres

Example 17 · Share a fractional amount

Given. Share \(\frac{3}{4}\) L equally between two people.

Find. Find each share.

Rule. Express quarters as eighths.

  1. \(\frac{3}{4}\) L is \(\frac{6}{8}\) L.
  2. One half of six eighths is three eighths.

Answer. \(\frac{3}{8}\) L each

Example 18 · Count half-size portions

Given. 6 L of juice, cups of \(\frac{1}{2}\) L

Find. Count the filled cups.

Rule. Count two halves in each litre.

  1. Each litre fills two cups.
  2. 6 × 2 = 12. Check 12 × \(\frac{1}{2}\) = 6.

Answer. 12 cups

Example 19 · Divide two fractions

Given. \(\frac{2}{3}\) ÷ \(\frac{4}{5}\)

Find. Find the quotient.

Rule. Use a common unit, or the reciprocal of the divisor.

  1. \(\frac{2}{3}\) = \(\frac{10}{15}\) and \(\frac{4}{5}\) = \(\frac{12}{15}\).
  2. A group has 12 fifteenths and 10 of them fit.
  3. \(\frac{10}{12}\) = \(\frac{5}{6}\). Check \(\frac{5}{6}\) × \(\frac{4}{5}\) = \(\frac{2}{3}\).

Answer. \(\frac{5}{6}\)

Example 20 · Divide mixed numbers

Given. A \(2\,\frac{1}{2}\) m ribbon is divided into \(1\,\frac{1}{4}\) m portions.

Find. Find the number of portions.

Rule. Convert both mixed numbers before inverting the divisor.

  1. \(2\,\frac{1}{2}\) = \(\frac{5}{2}\) and \(1\,\frac{1}{4}\) = \(\frac{5}{4}\).
  2. \(\frac{5}{2}\) × \(\frac{4}{5}\) = \(\frac{4}{2}\) = 2.

Answer. 2 portions

Example 21 · Use the distributive property

Given. \(\frac{4}{7}\) × \(1\,\frac{3}{4}\)

Find. Compute mentally using a sum.

Rule. Distribute over one whole and three quarters.

  1. \(\frac{4}{7}\) × 1 = \(\frac{4}{7}\).
  2. \(\frac{4}{7}\) × \(\frac{3}{4}\) = \(\frac{3}{7}\) after cancelling 4.
  3. Add \(\frac{4}{7}\) + \(\frac{3}{7}\) = \(\frac{7}{7}\).

Answer. 1

Advanced and competency-based

Example 22 · Scale a recipe

Given. Four servings need \(\frac{3}{4}\) cup of flour.

Find. Find the amount for six servings.

Rule. Multiply by six fourths.

  1. The scale factor is \(\frac{6}{4}\) = \(\frac{3}{2}\).
  2. \(\frac{3}{4}\) × \(\frac{3}{2}\) = \(\frac{9}{8}\).
  3. As a check, four servings need six eighths and two more need three eighths.

Answer. \(1\,\frac{1}{8}\) cups

Example 23 · Use the new whole

Given. A class used \(\frac{2}{5}\) of 30 sheets, then \(\frac{1}{3}\) of the sheets left.

Find. How many sheets remain?

Rule. Apply the second fraction to the remaining amount.

  1. \(\frac{2}{5}\) × 30 = 12, leaving 18 sheets.
  2. \(\frac{1}{3}\) × 18 = 6 are used in the second step.
  3. 18 − 6 = 12 sheets.

Answer. 12 sheets

Example 24 · Find a fractional last group

Given. 2 L of water; each bottle holds \(\frac{3}{4}\) L

Find. How many bottle capacities are available and how many full bottles can be filled?

Rule. Divide, then interpret the fractional part of the quotient.

  1. 2 ÷ \(\frac{3}{4}\) = 2 × \(\frac{4}{3}\) = \(\frac{8}{3}\) = \(2\,\frac{2}{3}\).
  2. Two full bottles use \(\frac{3}{2}\) L.
  3. The remaining \(\frac{1}{2}\) L is two thirds of another bottle.

Answer. 2 full bottles and \(\frac{1}{2}\) L left

Example 25 · Check the order of operations

Given. \(\frac{1}{2}\) + (\(\frac{3}{4}\) − \(\frac{1}{4}\)) × \(\frac{2}{3}\)

Find. Find the value.

Rule. Brackets first, then multiplication, then addition.

  1. Inside brackets, \(\frac{3}{4}\) − \(\frac{1}{4}\) = \(\frac{1}{2}\).
  2. \(\frac{1}{2}\) × \(\frac{2}{3}\) = \(\frac{1}{3}\).
  3. Use sixths: \(\frac{3}{6}\) + \(\frac{2}{6}\) = \(\frac{5}{6}\).

Answer. \(\frac{5}{6}\)

Example 26 · Find a missing multiplier

Given. A positive fraction times \(\frac{5}{6}\) gives \(\frac{15}{24}\).

Find. Find the missing fraction.

Rule. Recover a factor by dividing the product by the known factor.

  1. \(\frac{15}{24}\) simplifies to \(\frac{5}{8}\).
  2. \(\frac{5}{8}\) ÷ \(\frac{5}{6}\) = \(\frac{5}{8}\) × \(\frac{6}{5}\).
  3. Cancel the fives and reduce \(\frac{6}{8}\).

Answer. \(\frac{3}{4}\)

Example 27 · Find a missing divisor

Given. \(\frac{3}{5}\) ÷ x = \(\frac{9}{10}\)

Find. Find the positive divisor.

Rule. Dividend = divisor × quotient.

  1. Rewrite x × \(\frac{9}{10}\) = \(\frac{3}{5}\).
  2. Divide by \(\frac{9}{10}\): \(\frac{3}{5}\) × \(\frac{10}{9}\).
  3. Cancel 10 and 5 by 5, and 3 and 9 by 3.

Answer. \(\frac{2}{3}\)

Example 28 · Tile a rectangle

Given. A 2 m by \(1\,\frac{3}{4}\) m surface uses square tiles with side \(\frac{1}{4}\) m.

Find. Find the number of tiles.

Rule. Divide total area by one tile area.

  1. Total area is 2 × \(\frac{7}{4}\) = \(\frac{7}{2}\) square metres.
  2. One tile has area \(\frac{1}{4}\) × \(\frac{1}{4}\) = \(\frac{1}{16}\) square metre.
  3. \(\frac{7}{2}\) ÷ \(\frac{1}{16}\) = \(\frac{7}{2}\) × 16 = 56. Eight columns and seven rows confirm it.

Answer. 56 tiles

Example 29 · Follow a split route

Given. A group splits into halves. One half splits into quarters of the original.

Find. Find the share going to the destination reached by one half and one quarter.

Rule. Combine shares of the same original whole.

  1. Each part of the split half is \(\frac{1}{2}\) × \(\frac{1}{2}\) = \(\frac{1}{4}\).
  2. At the destination combine \(\frac{1}{2}\) + \(\frac{1}{4}\) = \(\frac{2}{4}\) + \(\frac{1}{4}\).
  3. The remaining destination receives \(\frac{1}{4}\), so the parts total 1.

Answer. \(\frac{3}{4}\) of the original group

Example 30 · Explain cancellation across a product

Given. (1 − \(\frac{1}{2}\)) × (1 − \(\frac{1}{3}\)) × (1 − \(\frac{1}{4}\)) × (1 − \(\frac{1}{5}\))

Find. Simplify and predict the effect of adding the next factor.

Rule. Simplify each bracket and cancel common factors in the product.

  1. The factors are \(\frac{1}{2}\), \(\frac{2}{3}\), \(\frac{3}{4}\) and \(\frac{4}{5}\).
  2. The 2, 3 and 4 factors cancel, leaving \(\frac{1}{5}\).
  3. Adding the next factor \(\frac{5}{6}\) cancels the 5 and leaves \(\frac{1}{6}\).

Answer. \(\frac{1}{5}\), then \(\frac{1}{6}\)

Common mistakes and how to fix them

Swap numerator and denominator

Incorrect approach. Write \(\frac{8}{3}\) for three out of eight pieces.

Correction. First count all eight parts. Write 8 below the bar. Count three chosen parts and put 3 above it.

Add both counts

Incorrect approach. \(\frac{1}{3}\) + \(\frac{1}{6}\) = \(\frac{2}{9}\).

Correction. Convert \(\frac{1}{3}\) into \(\frac{2}{6}\), then add \(\frac{1}{6}\) to get \(\frac{3}{6}\) = \(\frac{1}{2}\). Keep the common piece size.

Subtract denominators

Incorrect approach. \(\frac{7}{8}\) − \(\frac{2}{8}\) = \(\frac{5}{0}\).

Correction. Both fractions count eighths. Remove two eighths to leave \(\frac{5}{8}\).

Operate on unlike parts

Incorrect approach. \(\frac{5}{6}\) − \(\frac{1}{4}\) = \(\frac{4}{6}\).

Correction. Use twelfths: \(\frac{10}{12}\) − \(\frac{3}{12}\) = \(\frac{7}{12}\). Check that every term counts twelfths.

Choose a number that is not a common multiple

Incorrect approach. Use 10 for the denominators 4 and 6.

Correction. List multiples of 4 as 4, 8, 12 and of 6 as 6, 12. Choose 12, the first common length.

Use addition rules for multiplication

Incorrect approach. \(\frac{1}{3}\) × \(\frac{2}{3}\) = \(\frac{2}{3}\) because both factors have denominator 3.

Correction. A 3 by 3 grid has nine cells. The selected row and two selected columns overlap in two cells, so the product is \(\frac{2}{9}\).

Expect every product to grow

Incorrect approach. \(\frac{3}{4}\) of \(\frac{1}{2}\) should exceed \(\frac{1}{2}\).

Correction. A factor of \(\frac{3}{4}\) takes three quarters of the half. The result \(\frac{3}{8}\) is below \(\frac{1}{2}\).

Expect every quotient to shrink

Incorrect approach. 3 ÷ \(\frac{1}{2}\) should be below 3.

Correction. Each whole contains two half-sized portions. Three wholes contain six halves.

Invert the dividend

Incorrect approach. \(\frac{2}{3}\) ÷ \(\frac{4}{5}\) becomes \(\frac{3}{2}\) × \(\frac{4}{5}\).

Correction. Keep \(\frac{2}{3}\). Invert \(\frac{4}{5}\) to \(\frac{5}{4}\), then multiply and simplify to \(\frac{5}{6}\).

Try to invert zero

Incorrect approach. The reciprocal of 0 is \(\frac{1}{0}\).

Correction. A reciprocal must make the product 1. Zero times any number gives 0, so no value qualifies.

Convert a mixed number by adding the whole and numerator

Incorrect approach. \(2\,\frac{3}{5}\) = \(\frac{5}{5}\).

Correction. Two wholes are ten fifths. Add three fifths to get \(\frac{13}{5}\).

Cancel through a sum

Incorrect approach. Cancel a 2 in (2 + 3) divided by 2.

Correction. First combine the numerator to get five halves, or split the fraction into \(\frac{2}{2}\) + \(\frac{3}{2}\). Only common factors of a whole numerator and denominator can cancel.

Cancel both numerators

Incorrect approach. Cross out the two 3s in \(\frac{3}{4}\) × \(\frac{3}{7}\).

Correction. The 3s are both factors of the numerator. Only a common factor in a top and a bottom can cancel. The product is \(\frac{9}{28}\).

Stop before simplifying

Incorrect approach. Leave \(\frac{6}{12}\) as the simplest answer.

Correction. Find the common factor 6. Divide both terms by it to get \(\frac{1}{2}\). Both describe the same amount.

Treat multiplication as higher priority than division

Incorrect approach. Calculate \(\frac{3}{4}\) ÷ \(\frac{1}{2}\) × 2 starting with \(\frac{1}{2}\) × 2.

Correction. Work left to right. \(\frac{3}{4}\) ÷ \(\frac{1}{2}\) = \(\frac{3}{2}\), then \(\frac{3}{2}\) × 2 = 3.

Multiply when the question asks for a group count

Incorrect approach. Find bottles by multiplying 2 L by \(\frac{1}{4}\) L.

Correction. Set up quantity ÷ capacity. Two litres contain eight quarter-litre groups.

Take a fraction of the starting amount again

Incorrect approach. After spending a third of 120 rupees, save half of 120.

Correction. Subtract the spending first. One third is 40, leaving 80. Half of the remaining 80 is 40.

Exam preparation and quick revision

Show the operation and the unit

Underline the whole quantity and the words that signal the operation. Show a common denominator for addition or subtraction. For multiplication show any common factors you cancel. For division write the change to multiplication with the reciprocal. Simplify the result and attach the unit or interpret the number of complete groups.

Mind map

  • Fraction
    • Parts and number lines → equivalence and comparison
    • Common piece size → add or subtract
    • Scaling or area → multiply and cancel factors
    • Sharing or group count → divide and check with multiplication
    • Practical questions → choose the whole, check units and interpret the answer
