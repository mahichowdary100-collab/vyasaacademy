1.1 · Geometric twins

Two figures are congruent when they have the same shape and size. They can coincide exactly after one is moved, turned or flipped. The symbol is \(\cong\). Identical coins and floor tiles are familiar examples. Playing cards can have different printed faces while their outlines are congruent. For jigsaw pieces, compare the full outlines, including every tab and notch.

In these notes Chapter 9 is the opening chapter of Ganita Prakash Part II. You will learn how to use superposition, match corresponding parts and choose a triangle congruence criterion from the given information.

Translation slides all points by the same amount. Rotation turns a figure about a point. Reflection flips it across a line. All three preserve lengths and angle measures. Changing scale by a positive factor other than 1 changes the lengths, so a larger copy of the same shape is a different size.

Superposition

Lay a tracing over the second outline. First match a point and the direction of one edge. Turn the tracing or flip it if necessary, then check that every corresponding point and edge coincides. Matching just one corner or one edge gives too little evidence.

Segments and angles

Line segments are congruent when they have the same length. Tick marks show which lengths match. Angles are congruent when their measures agree. Their rays may be drawn with different visible lengths. Use matched arcs or a protractor, with its centre at the vertex and its zero along one ray. For a refresher on angle markings see Parallel and Intersecting Lines.

Investigate a bent sign or support

Goal. Determine the measurements that fix a bent outline.

Materials. Card strips, fastener, tracing paper and protractor.

  1. Join strips 5 cm and 7 cm long at one end. Make a tracing.
  2. Keep both lengths and change the bend. Compare with the tracing.
  3. Restore the original bend, then slide, turn and flip the tracing to fit.
Reveal observations and conclusion

Fixing the two lengths leaves a movable joint. Fixing the included angle as well locks the shape and size, while allowing different orientations.

1.2 · Congruence of triangles

When \(\triangle ABC\cong\triangle PQR\), the vertex order says A matches P, B matches Q and C matches R. Both lists may be reordered provided they change together. For example, BCA matches QRP.

Parts of ABCParts of PQR
A, B, CP, Q, R respectively
AB, BC, CAPQ, QR, RP respectively
∠A, ∠B, ∠C∠P, ∠Q, ∠R respectively

Corresponding parts of congruent triangles are equal. First justify congruence using the given measurements. Then use the matching order to infer a new length or angle. For example, if the above congruence is established and BC = 6 cm, then QR = 6 cm.

SSS · Three corresponding sides

Three pairs of equal corresponding sides guarantee congruence. The lengths must first form a valid triangle. The sum of the two shorter sides must exceed the longest.

Construction and verification

Construct the triangle from the measurements shown. Begin with a rough labelled sketch. Repeat the instructions to construct its twin, replacing A, B and C with P, Q and R.

The arc from A fixes AC. The arc from B fixes BC. Two crossings on opposite sides of AB are reflections. Fold along AB to see why their triangles coincide. For the construction and triangle inequality see A Tale of Three Intersecting Lines.

A quick example

If AB = PQ = 7 cm, AC = PR = 5 cm and BC = QR = 6 cm, then △ABC ≅ △PQR by SSS. Therefore ∠B = ∠Q.

Remember. SSS has three side pairs and requires no stated angle.

Why equal angles alone leave the size open

Three corresponding angles can agree even when all side lengths are doubled. For instance an equilateral triangle with side 3 cm and one with side 6 cm each have three 60° angles, but their sides differ. AAA cannot establish congruence.

Test angles alone

Goal. Find what remains free when angles are fixed.

Materials. Ruler, protractor and tracing paper.

  1. Draw a 4 cm base and a 7 cm base.
  2. At their ends mark the same two positive angles. Extend the rays until they meet.
  3. Compare the third angles and then compare the bases.
Reveal observations and conclusion

Each third angle follows from the 180° sum. The triangles have matching angles but different base lengths, so a tracing cannot fit both.

SAS · Two sides and the included angle

The included angle is at the shared endpoint of the two given sides. If AB and AC are given, their included angle is ∠BAC. Two equal side pairs and the equal included angle give SAS.

Construction and verification

Construct the triangle from the measurements shown. Begin with a rough labelled sketch. Repeat the instructions to construct its twin, replacing A, B and C with P, Q and R.

The baseline fixes B relative to A. The included angle fixes the second ray. Marking AC fixes C on that ray. There is one shape, with a reflected option on the other side of the base.

A quick example

AB = PQ, AC = PR and ∠A = ∠P imply △ABC ≅ △PQR by SAS. Then BC = QR.

Remember. Read the initials in order around the parts: side, angle between them, side.

Why a non-included angle can give two triangles

Suppose AB = 9 cm and ∠A = 30°, and BC = 6 cm is fixed. Start the ray AC at A and sweep a 6 cm compass arc from B. It meets the ray twice. Both candidates have the given data, but their AC lengths differ. Thus SSA does not in general guarantee congruence. Two sides alone also leave the angle between them free.

ASA · Two angles and the included side

The included side joins the two given angle vertices. For ∠A and ∠B, the included side is AB. If the two angles and that corresponding side agree, ASA applies.

Construction and verification

Construct the triangle from the measurements shown. Begin with a rough labelled sketch. Repeat the instructions to construct its twin, replacing A, B and C with P, Q and R.

Both angle rays start at the specified base endpoints. Their crossing determines C. Positive base angles must add to less than 180° so that the two rays can meet above the base. The unused angle is found from the angle sum.

A quick example

If ∠A = ∠P = 48°, ∠B = ∠Q = 67° and AB = PQ = 7 cm, △ABC ≅ △PQR by ASA, and ∠R = 65°.

Remember. The side connects the two marked angle vertices.

AAS · Two angles and a non-included side

Two corresponding angles and one corresponding side guarantee congruence even if that side is not between the stated angles. This is AAS.

Construction and verification

Construct the triangle from the measurements shown. Begin with a rough labelled sketch. Repeat the instructions to construct its twin, replacing A, B and C with P, Q and R.

When ∠A = 44° and ∠C = 65°, ∠B = 180° − 44° − 65° = 71°. The same calculation applies to the twin. AB is now the included side for ∠A and ∠B, so the construction reduces to ASA.

A quick example

AB = PQ, ∠A = ∠P and ∠C = ∠R give △ABC ≅ △PQR by AAS. Then AC = PR.

Remember. Find the unmarked angles and use ASA at the ends of the given side.

RHS · Right angle, hypotenuse and one leg

In each right triangle the hypotenuse is opposite the right angle. Equal hypotenuses and a pair of equal corresponding legs guarantee congruence.

Construction and verification

Construct the triangle from the measurements shown. Begin with a rough labelled sketch. Repeat the instructions to construct its twin, replacing A, B and C with P, Q and R.

The leg AB sets the baseline and the right angle at A fixes a perpendicular. An arc centred at B with radius BC fixes the hypotenuse. Its two intersections with the perpendicular give reflected twins. A positive hypotenuse must be longer than the chosen leg.

A quick example

With right angles at A and P, hypotenuses BC = QR = 5 cm and legs AB = PQ = 4 cm, △ABC ≅ △PQR by RHS.

Remember. Mark the right angles first. Trace across to the hypotenuses, then match one leg.

1.3 · Angles of isosceles and equilateral triangles

Let AB = AC in △ABC. Draw the altitude AD to base BC. The right triangles ADB and ADC have AB = AC as hypotenuses and AD as their common leg. Hence △ADB ≅ △ADC by RHS. The corresponding base angles ∠B and ∠C are equal. The same match shows BD = DC and ∠BAD = ∠CAD.

If ∠A = 74°, each base angle is (180° − 74°) ÷ 2 = 53°. In an equilateral triangle, apply the equal-side property to any two pairs of sides. All three angles are equal. They add to 180°, so each is 60°.

Fold a triangular tile

Goal. Check the same matches by superposition.

Materials. An isosceles paper triangle, pencil and ruler.

  1. Fold so the equal sides AB and AC meet.
  2. Mark the crease AD and compare the endpoints B and C.
  3. Unfold and check that D is the midpoint and the crease makes right angles with the base.
Reveal observations and conclusion

Reflection across AD fixes A and D and exchanges B and C. This agrees with the RHS proof and with the equality of the angles opposite equal sides.

Congruence in designs and puzzles

Congruent roof truss panels can be made from one template and installed in different orientations. A triangular glazing panel can be ordered using three side measurements. In a tile pattern, repeated pieces may be slid, turned or flipped around a gap. A folded paper design reflects one half onto the other. In manufacturing, congruent profiles allow parts to fit the same space.

Congruence criteria at a glance

SSS

All three side pairs match.

Check triangle inequality and locate C by two arcs.

7, 5 and 6 cm fix the triangle.

Remember. Three S marks means three side pairs.

SAS

Two side pairs and their included angles match.

Match the angle at the shared endpoint.

AB, AC and ∠A fix C on a specified ray.

Remember. Circle the common endpoint.

ASA

Two angle pairs and their included sides match.

The angle sum must leave a positive third angle.

Angles 48°, 67° and the 7 cm base give third angle 65°.

Remember. Draw the joining side.

AAS

Two angle pairs and a non-included side pair match.

Use the angle sum to reduce to ASA.

Angles 44°, 65° and AB give ∠B = 71°.

Remember. Find the missing angle first.

RHS

Right angles, hypotenuses and one leg pair match.

Identify the hypotenuse opposite the right angle.

A 5 cm hypotenuse and a 4 cm leg fix the right triangle.

Remember. Right angle → hypotenuse → side.

AAA

Equal angles leave size free.

Try a scaled triangle with the same angles.

Two equilateral triangles can have sides 3 and 6 cm.

Remember. Check whether any length is fixed.

SSA

A non-included angle does not always guarantee congruence.

A ray can meet a compass arc at two points.

With the stated 9 cm and 6 cm sides and a 30° non-included angle, two different third side lengths are possible.

Remember. Look for two possible crossings.

30 step-by-step worked examples

Basic

Example 1 · Compare two segments

Given. AB and PQ each measure 6 cm.

Find. Decide whether AB and PQ are congruent.

Reasoning. Equal segments have equal length.

  1. Match A with P and turn AB until its direction coincides with PQ.
  2. The endpoints B and Q then coincide because both lengths are 6 cm.

Answer. AB ≅ PQ.

Example 2 · Compare angles after turning

Given. ∠ABC and ∠PQR each measure 48°.

Find. Decide whether the angles are congruent.

Reasoning. Angle measure fixes the opening.

  1. Match B with Q, then align BA with QP.
  2. The other rays also align because the openings are equal.

Answer. ∠ABC ≅ ∠PQR.

Example 3 · Compare two floor tiles

Given. One 4 cm square is turned. A second comparison uses a 6 cm square.

Find. Identify the twin of the original 4 cm square.

Reasoning. Rigid moves preserve shape and size.

  1. Align one corner and one edge of the turned 4 cm square.
  2. All four edges match. The 6 cm square extends beyond those edges.

Answer. The turned 4 cm square is congruent; the 6 cm square is larger.

Example 4 · Read the vertex order

Given. △ABC ≅ △PQR.

Find. Name the side matching BC and the angle matching ∠A.

Reasoning. Correspondence follows the order of the vertices.

  1. B ↔ Q and C ↔ R, so BC maps to QR.
  2. A ↔ P, so angle A maps to angle P.

Answer. BC = QR and ∠A = ∠P.

Example 5 · Identify SSS

Given. AB = PQ = 7 cm, AC = PR = 5 cm and BC = QR = 6 cm.

Find. State the criterion.

Reasoning. SSS.

  1. The two sides meeting at A match the two sides meeting at P.
  2. All three corresponding sides are equal.

Answer. △ABC ≅ △PQR by SSS.

Example 6 · Identify the included angle

Given. AB = PQ = 7 cm and AC = PR = 5 cm. ∠A = ∠P = 52°.

Find. Prove congruence.

Reasoning. SAS.

  1. AB and AC meet at A. PQ and PR meet at P.
  2. Both included angles are 52°, so the given side pairs and angle pairs meet SAS.

Answer. △ABC ≅ △PQR by SAS.

Example 7 · Identify the included side

Given. ∠A = ∠P = 48°, ∠B = ∠Q = 67° and AB = PQ = 7 cm.

Find. Prove congruence.

Reasoning. ASA.

  1. AB joins A and B; PQ joins P and Q.
  2. The given angles are the angles at the two endpoints in both triangles.

Answer. △ABC ≅ △PQR by ASA.

Example 8 · Find the hypotenuse

Given. ∠A and ∠P are right; BC and QR measure 5 cm; AB and PQ measure 4 cm.

Find. Name the hypotenuses and establish congruence.

Reasoning. RHS.

  1. BC and QR face the right angles.
  2. The hypotenuse pair and corresponding leg pair are equal.

Answer. The hypotenuses are BC and QR. △ABC ≅ △PQR.

Example 9 · Check whether AAA fixes size

Given. Both triangles have corresponding angles 52°, 61° and 67°. Their bases have different lengths.

Find. Decide whether the triangles are congruent.

Reasoning. Matching angles do not fix length.

  1. A ↔ P, B ↔ Q and C ↔ R by angle.
  2. A rigid move cannot turn the shorter base into the longer base.

Answer. AAA is insufficient, and these triangles differ in size.

Intermediate

Example 10 · Recover the matching length

Given. AB = PQ = 8 cm, AC = PR = 5 cm and BC = QR = 7 cm. RS is an extension of RQ with S beyond Q and QS = 2 cm.

Find. Find RS.

Reasoning. Use SSS, then segment addition.

  1. Match A ↔ P, B ↔ Q, C ↔ R by the three equal side pairs.
  2. From congruence, RQ = BC = 7 cm.
  3. Q lies between R and S, so RS = RQ + QS.

Answer. △ABC ≅ △PQR; RS = 9 cm.

Example 11 · Find a hidden angle using SAS

Given. AB = PQ = 6 cm, AC = PR = 6 cm and ∠A = ∠P = 50°.

Find. Find ∠R.

Reasoning. SAS and equal base angles.

  1. The two equal sides meet at A and P, so △ABC ≅ △PQR.
  2. AB = AC makes ∠B = ∠C. Each is (180° − 50°) ÷ 2.
  3. C ↔ R, so their measures agree.

Answer. ∠R = 65°.

Example 12 · Find a third angle after ASA

Given. In both triangles the corresponding base angles are 43° and 62°, and each joining side is 6 cm.

Find. Find the remaining angle in each triangle.

Reasoning. ASA and the triangle angle sum.

  1. Match A ↔ P at 43°, B ↔ Q at 62° and AB ↔ PQ.
  2. Apply ASA, then subtract the given angles from 180°.

Answer. △ABC ≅ △PQR; ∠C = ∠R = 75°.

Example 13 · Turn AAS into ASA

Given. AB = PQ = 8 cm, ∠A = ∠P = 44° and ∠C = ∠R = 65°.

Find. Show the ASA route to congruence.

Reasoning. Sum of angles, then ASA.

  1. B and Q both measure 180° − 44° − 65° = 71°.
  2. AB and PQ now join the two known base angles.

Answer. △ABC ≅ △PQR by ASA after deriving the missing angle.

Example 14 · Use RHS on folded triangles

Given. ∠A and ∠P are right. The 10 cm hypotenuses and one 8 cm leg in each triangle match.

Find. Which criterion guarantees a fit?

Reasoning. RHS.

  1. Name the hypotenuses BC and QR. Match the legs AB and PQ.
  2. The 10 cm arc meets the perpendicular at reflected points, fixing one shape.

Answer. △ABC ≅ △PQR by RHS.

Example 15 · Construct an SSS twin

Given. AB = 7 cm, AC = 5 cm, BC = 6 cm.

Find. Construct PQR so it matches ABC.

Reasoning. Two fixed distances locate an arc crossing.

  1. Draw PQ of length 7 cm.
  2. Draw arcs of radius 5 cm at P and 6 cm at Q.
  3. Join an intersection R to P and Q. The arc radii give the required equal lengths.

Answer. △ABC ≅ △PQR by SSS.

Example 16 · Construct an SAS twin

Given. AB = 7 cm, AC = 5 cm and ∠A = 52°.

Find. Construct a matching triangle.

Reasoning. Two joined side lengths and their opening fix the shape.

  1. Draw PQ = 7 cm.
  2. Measure 52° at P from PQ and draw the ray.
  3. Mark R five centimetres from P along the ray and join RQ.

Answer. △ABC ≅ △PQR by SAS.

Example 17 · Construct an ASA twin

Given. AB = 7 cm, ∠A = 48° and ∠B = 67°.

Find. Construct PQR.

Reasoning. Two marked rays meeting above a fixed base give one triangle.

  1. Draw PQ = 7 cm.
  2. At P draw the 48° ray from PQ. At Q draw the 67° ray from QP.
  3. Name their intersection R and check ∠R = 65°.

Answer. △ABC ≅ △PQR by ASA.

Example 18 · Construct an AAS twin

Given. AB = 8 cm, ∠A = 44° and ∠C = 65°.

Find. Construct PQR.

Reasoning. Use the angle sum to expose ASA.

  1. Find ∠B = 71°.
  2. Draw PQ = 8 cm and rays at 44° and 71° from its ends toward the same side.
  3. The intersection R has angle 65° by the angle sum.

Answer. △ABC ≅ △PQR by AAS; the construction uses ASA.

Example 19 · Construct an RHS twin

Given. Right angle at A, AB = 4 cm, BC = 5 cm.

Find. Construct a matching right triangle.

Reasoning. A perpendicular and a circle fix the third vertex.

  1. Draw PQ = 4 cm and the perpendicular at P.
  2. Draw a 5 cm radius arc from Q to meet the perpendicular at R.
  3. Join QR; the arc fixes the 5 cm hypotenuse.

Answer. △ABC ≅ △PQR by RHS.

Example 20 · Find an isosceles base angle

Given. AB = AC and ∠A = 74°. AD is the altitude.

Find. Find ∠B and ∠C.

Reasoning. RHS and corresponding angles.

  1. △ADB and △ADC share leg AD and have equal hypotenuses AB and AC.
  2. They are congruent by RHS. Therefore B and C have equal angles.
  3. Subtract 74° from 180°, then divide by 2.

Answer. △ADB ≅ △ADC; ∠B = ∠C = 53°.

Example 21 · Explain equilateral angles

Given. AB = BC = CA.

Find. Find all three angles.

Reasoning. Equal sides face equal angles.

  1. AB = AC makes angles B and C equal. AB = BC also makes angles A and C equal.
  2. The common angle is one third of 180°.

Answer. ∠A = ∠B = ∠C = 60°.

Advanced and competency-based

Example 22 · Use a reflection and the right angle

Given. In the isosceles diagram AB = AC and AD ⟂ BC. BD = 5 cm.

Find. Find BC and explain why D is its midpoint.

Reasoning. RHS, then corresponding parts.

  1. △ADB ≅ △ADC because AD is common and AB = AC.
  2. Thus BD = DC. D lies on BC.
  3. Add BD and DC.

Answer. BC = 10 cm.

Example 23 · Find the scale error in a pane order

Given. Two shop drawings have corresponding angles 52°, 61° and 67°. AB = 4 cm, and the other drawing has PQ = 6.6 cm.

Find. Can they describe the same frame outline?

Reasoning. A congruence preserves lengths.

  1. Connect A ↔ P and B ↔ Q by the marked angles.
  2. The connecting sides are different, so exact overlap is impossible.

Answer. The drawings are not congruent.

Example 24 · Check a manufacturing instruction

Given. A triangle is described by AB = 9 cm, BC = 6 cm and ∠A = 30°.

Find. Decide whether the instruction fixes one shape.

Reasoning. SSA can admit two solutions.

  1. Draw the 30° ray at A and the 6 cm compass arc from B.
  2. It crosses the ray twice, creating C and C′ at different distances from A.
  3. AB and BC have the same prescribed lengths in both candidates, but AC differs.

Answer. Add a suitable measurement, such as AC, to fix the shape.

Example 25 · Recover a length from equal perimeters

Given. △ABC and △PQR are congruent by SSS. The perimeter of ABC is 21 cm. In PQR, PQ = 8 cm and PR = 6 cm.

Find. Find QR.

Reasoning. Congruent triangles have the same perimeter.

  1. Match A ↔ P, B ↔ Q, C ↔ R in the diagram.
  2. The perimeter of PQR is also 21 cm because each corresponding length matches.
  3. Subtract PQ and PR from the perimeter.

Answer. △ABC ≅ △PQR; QR = 7 cm.

Example 26 · Check a vertex-order mistake

Given. AB = PQ = 7 cm, AC = PR = 5 cm and BC = QR = 6 cm. A student writes △ABC ≅ △QPR.

Find. Correct the statement.

Reasoning. SSS and correspondence.

  1. The 7 cm and 5 cm sides meet at A. Their matches meet at P.
  2. B maps to Q and C maps to R. The proposed ordering sends A to Q and mismatches the 5 cm side.

Answer. Write △ABC ≅ △PQR.

Example 27 · Find a base angle from an exterior angle

Given. In △ABC, AB = AC. Extend BC through C so that the exterior angle at C is 124°.

Find. Find all interior angles.

Reasoning. RHS for base angles, linear pair and angle sum.

  1. The altitude AD produces RHS twins ADB and ADC, so B = C.
  2. Interior C = 180° − 124° = 56°. Thus B = 56°.
  3. Subtract 112° from 180° to find A.

Answer. △ADB ≅ △ADC; ∠A = 68°, ∠B = ∠C = 56°.

Example 28 · Combine congruence with a linear pair

Given. ∠A = ∠P = 55°, ∠B = ∠Q = 45° and AB = PQ = 8 cm. Extend PR through P to T.

Find. Find exterior angle QPT.

Reasoning. ASA, then the linear-pair property.

  1. AB and PQ join the given angle vertices, so △ABC ≅ △PQR by ASA.
  2. PR and PT are opposite rays. ∠QPR and ∠QPT add to 180°.
  3. Subtract 55° from 180°.

Answer. △ABC ≅ △PQR; ∠QPT = 125°.

Example 29 · Verify an AAS guarantee under changed orientation

Given. ∠A = ∠P = 35°, ∠C = ∠R = 80° and AB = PQ = 9 cm. PQR is flipped.

Find. Find ∠Q and decide whether the flipped triangle fits.

Reasoning. AAS and angle sum.

  1. Subtract 35° and 80° from 180° to get ∠B = ∠Q = 65°.
  2. Use AAS or use ASA with the inferred angle. Reflection preserves all matching lengths.

Answer. △ABC ≅ △PQR; ∠Q = 65°.

Example 30 · Distinguish SAS from RHS

Given. The two triangles have right angles at A and P. AB = PQ = 6 cm and AC = PR = 8 cm.

Find. Choose the criterion directly available from the stated measurements.

Reasoning. SAS with the right angle included between the legs.

  1. Each pair of measured sides meets at the right-angle vertex.
  2. The hypotenuses are not among the stated data, so directly use SAS.

Answer. △ABC ≅ △PQR by SAS.

Common mistakes and how to fix them

Match shape while ignoring size

Incorrect approach. Call a larger same-angle triangle congruent.

Correction. Compare at least one corresponding length. Scaling changes every length.

Reject a pair because one faces the other way

Incorrect approach. Require both to have the same orientation.

Correction. Slide, turn or flip a tracing before comparing.

Reject a rotated copy

Incorrect approach. Use the direction of an edge as evidence of different size.

Correction. Rotate the outline and compare every edge.

Reject a mirror image

Incorrect approach. Assume a flip changes distances.

Correction. Flip a tracing across the reflection line, then align it.

Match vertices by left-to-right position

Incorrect approach. Pair A with whichever right-hand vertex is lowest.

Correction. Match marked sides and angles and read their common endpoints.

Reverse one order

Incorrect approach. Write ABC ≅ QPR despite A matching P.

Correction. Write both vertex lists to preserve A ↔ P, B ↔ Q, C ↔ R.

Mix up SSS and SAS

Incorrect approach. Read an angle arc as a third side mark.

Correction. Count three side pairs for SSS. SAS uses the angle between two sides.

Confuse ASA and AAS

Incorrect approach. Call any side between an angle pair.

Correction. The side must join the two marked angle vertices for ASA. Otherwise use AAS or calculate the missing angle.

Use the wrong angle for SAS

Incorrect approach. Mark ∠C when the known sides are AB and AC.

Correction. Use ∠A, their common vertex. A non-included angle can allow two constructions.

Use RHS on an oblique triangle

Incorrect approach. Apply RHS without given or proved right angles.

Correction. Establish a right angle in both triangles first.

Choose a leg as the hypotenuse

Incorrect approach. Pick the side next to the right angle.

Correction. Trace from the right angle to its opposite side.

Assume AAA is enough

Incorrect approach. Use three equal angles to claim equal dimensions.

Correction. Compare the bases of a scaled pair to see the gap.

Assume SSA always works

Incorrect approach. Ignore a second circle-ray intersection.

Correction. Draw both possible third vertices before deciding if the data suffice.

Use corresponding parts prematurely

Incorrect approach. Claim every unmarked pair agrees from appearance.

Correction. Establish the triangle congruence, then use the stated order.

Assume equality from a sketch

Incorrect approach. Trust rough diagrams as measurements.

Correction. Use the given data, tick marks and confirmed properties only.

Omit the reason

Incorrect approach. Write AB = PQ without evidence.

Correction. Record the criterion or construction that guarantees the equality.

Read an instrument from the wrong baseline

Incorrect approach. Start B’s protractor scale from the ray pointing away from A.

Correction. Align the centre to B and choose the zero on BA. Verify lengths and angles when finished.

Write a proof in this order

Given → corresponding measurements → criterion → congruence in matching order → required measurement or property. Put the reason beside the claim it justifies.
