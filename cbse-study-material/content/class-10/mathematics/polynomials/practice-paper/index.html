Section A – Multiple Choice Questions

8 questions × 1 mark = 8 marks

Each question below has four options (A, B, C, D). Choose the one correct answer.

1.

The graph of a quadratic polynomial \(p(x)\) touches (but does not cross) the x-axis at exactly one point. How many distinct real zeroes does \(p(x)\) have?

1 Mark
  • A. Exactly one distinct real zero
  • B. Two distinct real zeroes
  • C. Two real zeroes, both equal
  • D. No real zero
2.

Which of the following is a quadratic polynomial?

1 Mark

  • B. \(3x^2 - 2x + 5\)
  • C. 8
  • D. \(x^2 - \frac{3}{x}\)
3.

The area of a rectangle is \((x^2 + 9x + 20)\) square units. If its length is \((x + 5)\) units, its breadth is

1 Mark

  • C. \((x - 4)\) units
  • D. \((x + 5)\) units

The zeroes of the quadratic polynomial \(x^2 + bx + c\) are 5 and \(-3\). Then

1 Mark

  • B. \(b = -2,\ c = -15\)
  • C. \(b = 2,\ c = 15\)
  • D. \(b = -2,\ c = 15\)

Can the numbers \(-3\) and \(-4\) be the zeroes of the polynomial \(x^2 - 7x + 12\)?

Detailed Solutions
Solution 1

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Geometrical meaning of the zeroes — where the graph meets the x-axis.

A quadratic polynomial has at most two zeroes. When its graph touches the x-axis at exactly one point, the two zeroes are real and equal (both equal to the x-coordinate of the point of contact). It does not cross the axis, so the two zeroes are equal, not distinct.

Final Answer: (C) Two real zeroes, both equal
Solution 2

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Definition of a polynomial and its degree.

A quadratic polynomial has degree 2, so it must contain a term in \(x^2\) with a non-zero coefficient. \(4x + 7\) has degree 1, 8 has degree 0, and \(x^2 - \frac{3}{x}\) is not a polynomial because it contains division by the variable.

Final Answer: (B) \(3x^2 - 2x + 5\)
Solution 3

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Area of a rectangle = length × breadth, and factorisation.

Breadth = \(\frac{\text{Area}}{\text{Length}} = \frac{x^2 + 9x + 20}{x + 5}\).
\(x^2 + 9x + 20 = (x + 5)(x + 4)\), so breadth = \(x + 4\) units.

Final Answer: (A) \((x + 4)\) units
Solution 4

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Sum of zeroes \(= -\frac{b}{a}\) and product of zeroes \(= \frac{c}{a}\).

Sum = \(5 + (-3) = 2\), so \(-b = 2 \Rightarrow b = -2\).
Product = \(5 \times (-3) = -15\), so \(c = -15\).

Final Answer: (B) \(b = -2,\ c = -15\)
Solution 5

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Testing candidate zeroes against the sum and product of zeroes.

For \(x^2 - 7x + 12\), sum of zeroes \(= -\frac{b}{a} = 7\) and product \(= \frac{c}{a} = 12\).
If the zeroes were \(-3\) and \(-4\), their sum would be \(-7\), but the required sum is 7. The product condition would be satisfied, but the sum condition is not, so they cannot be the zeroes.

Final Answer: (B) No, because their sum would be \(-7\), but the sum of the zeroes is 7.
Solution 6

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Forming a quadratic from its zeroes: \((x - \alpha)(x - \beta)\).

\(p(x) = (x - (-2))(x - 5) = (x + 2)(x - 5) = x^2 - 5x + 2x - 10 = x^2 - 3x - 10\).

Final Answer: (B) \(x^2 - 3x - 10\)
Solution 7

Source: CBSE PYQ (2023)

Concept Used: Product of zeroes of \(ax^2 + bx + c\) is \(\frac{c}{a}\); reciprocal zeroes have product 1.

Let the zeroes be \(\alpha\) and \(\frac{1}{\alpha}\). Then their product is 1.
\(\frac{c}{a} = \frac{-(k - 2)}{6} = 1\)
\(-(k - 2) = 6 \Rightarrow k - 2 = -6 \Rightarrow k = -4\).

Final Answer: (D) \(k = -4\)
Solution 8

Source: Original question

Concept Used: If a graph cuts the x-axis at \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\), the zeroes are \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\).

Zeroes are \(-2\) and 4, so \(p(x) = (x + 2)(x - 4) = x^2 - 4x + 2x - 8 = x^2 - 2x - 8\).

Final Answer: (B) \(x^2 - 2x - 8\)
Solution 9

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Sum \(= -\frac{b}{a}\), product \(= \frac{c}{a}\).

Sum \(= -\frac{2}{k}\), product \(= \frac{3k}{k} = 3\).
Given sum = product: \(-\frac{2}{k} = 3 \Rightarrow k = -\frac{2}{3}\).
So the Assertion (which claims \(k = 1\)) is false. The Reason correctly states the standard relationships, so it is true.

Final Answer: (D) A is false, but R is true.
Solution 10

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Zeroes of a quadratic and the points where its graph meets the x-axis.

\(x^2 - 5x + 6 = (x - 2)(x - 3)\), so the zeroes are 2 and 3, two distinct real numbers. The graph of a quadratic therefore meets the x-axis at these two distinct points. Both A and R are true and R correctly explains A.

Final Answer: (A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
Solution 11

Source: Original question (based on NCERT Exercise 2.2, Q1)

Concept Used: Factorising a quadratic and verifying sum/product of zeroes.

\(6x^2 - 3 - 7x = 6x^2 - 7x - 3\).
Splitting the middle term: \(6x^2 - 9x + 2x - 3 = 3x(2x - 3) + 1(2x - 3) = (3x + 1)(2x - 3)\).
Zeroes: \(3x + 1 = 0 \Rightarrow x = -\frac{1}{3}\) and \(2x - 3 = 0 \Rightarrow x = \frac{3}{2}\).

Verification:
Sum \(= -\frac{1}{3} + \frac{3}{2} = \frac{-2 + 9}{6} = \frac{7}{6} = -\frac{b}{a} = \frac{7}{6}\). ✓
Product \(= -\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{3}{2} = -\frac{1}{2} = \frac{c}{a} = \frac{-3}{6} = -\frac{1}{2}\). ✓

Final Answer: Zeroes are \(-\frac{1}{3}\) and \(\frac{3}{2}\); relationship verified.
Solution 12

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Area = length × breadth, factorisation by splitting the middle term.

\(x^2 + 11x + 30 = (x + 6)(x + 5)\).
Breadth = \(\frac{(x + 6)(x + 5)}{x + 6} = x + 5\) metres.

Final Answer: Breadth is \((x + 5)\) metres.
Solution 13

Source: Original question

Concept Used: A number \(\alpha\) is a zero of \(p(x)\) if \(p(\alpha) = 0\).

\(p(-3) = (-3)^2 + 2(-3) + k = 9 - 6 + k = 3 + k = 0 \Rightarrow k = -3\).
So \(p(x) = x^2 + 2x - 3 = (x + 3)(x - 1)\).
The other zero is given by \(x - 1 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 1\).

Check: Sum of zeroes \(= -3 + 1 = -2 = -\frac{b}{a}\). ✓

Final Answer: \(k = -3\) and the other zero is 1.
Solution 14

Source: Original question

Concept Used: The graph meets the x-axis at the zeroes of the polynomial.

The zeroes are 1 and 5, so \(p(x) = (x - 1)(x - 5) = x^2 - 5x - x + 5 = x^2 - 6x + 5\).

Final Answer: \(p(x) = x^2 - 6x + 5\)
Solution 15

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Area of a rectangle, forming and solving a quadratic, rejecting non-meaningful roots.

(a) Area = \((x + 4)(x + 2) = x^2 + 2x + 4x + 8 = x^2 + 6x + 8\).

(b) Given area is 48: \(x^2 + 6x + 8 = 48 \Rightarrow x^2 + 6x - 40 = 0\).
\(x^2 + 6x - 40 = (x + 10)(x - 4) = 0 \Rightarrow x = -10\) or \(x = 4\).
Hence \(x = 4\), so length = \(4 + 4 = 8\) m and breadth = \(4 + 2 = 6\) m.

(c) \(x = -10\) must be rejected because it would make the length \((-10 + 4) = -6\) m and breadth \((-10 + 2) = -8\) m, which are negative and meaningless as dimensions.

Check: \(8 \times 6 = 48\) m². ✓

Final Answer: (a) \(x^2 + 6x + 8\)    (b) \(x = 4\), garden is 8 m × 6 m    (c) \(x = -10\) rejected (negative dimensions)
Solution 16

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Perimeter and area of a rectangle; forming a quadratic from a real-world constraint.

(a) Perimeter = 34, so half the perimeter (length + breadth) = 17. With length \(L\), breadth = \(17 - L\).
Area = \(L(17 - L) = 60 \Rightarrow 17L - L^2 = 60 \Rightarrow L^2 - 17L + 60 = 0\).

(b) \(L^2 - 17L + 60 = (L - 12)(L - 5) = 0 \Rightarrow L = 12\) or \(L = 5\).
Taking the longer side as length, \(L = 12\) and breadth = \(17 - 12 = 5\). The two zeroes 12 and 5 are simply the two side lengths — whether we call either one "length" or "breadth", the rectangle is the same (12 × 5).

Check: Perimeter \(= 2(12 + 5) = 34\) and area \(= 12 \times 5 = 60\). ✓

Final Answer: (a) breadth \(= 17 - L\); quadratic \(L^2 - 17L + 60 = 0\)    (b) length 12, breadth 5
Solution 17

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Forming and solving a quadratic from a word problem.

(a) Smaller number \(= x\), next consecutive integer \(= x + 1\). Their product: \(x(x + 1) = 240 \Rightarrow x^2 + x - 240 = 0\).

(b) \(x^2 + x - 240 = (x + 16)(x - 15) = 0 \Rightarrow x = -16\) or \(x = 15\).
Since the integers are positive, \(x = 15\), and the numbers are 15 and 16.

Check: \(15 \times 16 = 240\). ✓

Final Answer: (a) \(x^2 + x - 240 = 0\)    (b) 15 and 16 (rejecting \(x = -16\) because the integers are positive)
Solution 18

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Zeroes of a polynomial and the sign of a quadratic between its zeroes.

(a) \(P(x) = 90x - x^2 = x(90 - x)\). Zeroes are \(x = 0\) and \(x = 90\).
Interpretation: the profit is ₹0 when no cups are sold (\(x = 0\)) and again when 90 dozen cups are sold — the first zero is the starting point, and the second is the point where the rising-then-falling profit returns to zero.

(b) The graph of \(P(x) = -x^2 + 90x\) is a downward-opening parabola, which lies above the x-axis between its two zeroes. Since \(x\) (dozens) cannot be negative, the profit is positive for \(0 < x < 90\). Beyond 90 dozen, profit becomes negative (a loss).

Final Answer: (a) zeroes \(x = 0\), \(x = 90\)    (b) profit positive for \(0 < x < 90\)
Solution 19

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Sum of zeroes \(= -\frac{b}{a}\), product \(= \frac{c}{a}\); forming a quadratic from new zeroes.

(a) For \(x^2 - 7x + k\): sum of zeroes \(= \alpha + \beta = 7\) and product \(= \alpha\beta = k\).
Given \(\alpha - \beta = 3\). Adding the two equations:
\((\alpha + \beta) + (\alpha - \beta) = 7 + 3 \Rightarrow 2\alpha = 10 \Rightarrow \alpha = 5\).
Then \(\beta = 7 - 5 = 2\). Hence \(k = \alpha\beta = 10\).

(b) New zeroes are \(\alpha - 1 = 4\) and \(\beta - 1 = 1\).
Sum of new zeroes = 5, product = 4.
Polynomial = \(x^2 - (\text{sum})x + \text{product} = x^2 - 5x + 4\).

Final Answer: (a) \(k = 10\)    (b) \(x^2 - 5x + 4\)
Solution 20(a)

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Area of a rectangle, solving a quadratic, verifying with the sum of zeroes.

Area = \((x + 8)(x + 3) = x^2 + 3x + 8x + 24 = x^2 + 11x + 24\).
Given area 84: \(x^2 + 11x + 24 = 84 \Rightarrow x^2 + 11x - 60 = 0\).
\(x^2 + 11x - 60 = (x + 15)(x - 4) = 0 \Rightarrow x = -15\) or \(x = 4\).
Since dimensions are positive, \(x = 4\). Length = \(4 + 8 = 12\) m, breadth = \(4 + 3 = 7\) m.

Verification using zeroes: the two zeroes of \(x^2 + 11x - 60\) are \(-15\) and 4, and their sum is \(-11\). The sum of zeroes of \(ax^2 + bx + c\) is \(-\frac{b}{a} = -\frac{11}{1} = -11\). ✓ (Also \(12 \times 7 = 84\) m².)

Final Answer: (a) quadratic \(x^2 + 11x - 60 = 0\); \(x = 4\); dimensions 12 m × 7 m; verified using sum of zeroes = \(-11\).
Solution 20(b)

Source: CBSE PYQ (Delhi 2019)

Concept Used: Reciprocal zeroes have product 1 = \(\frac{c}{a}\); verifying sum/product relationships.

For \(3x^2 - 10x + k\): product of zeroes \(= \frac{k}{3}\).
For reciprocal zeroes, product \(= 1\), so \(\frac{k}{3} = 1 \Rightarrow k = 3\).

With \(k = 3\), \(3x^2 - 10x + 3 = 3x^2 - 9x - x + 3 = 3x(x - 3) - 1(x - 3) = (3x - 1)(x - 3)\).
Zeroes are \(\frac{1}{3}\) and 3 (which are reciprocals).

Verification:
Sum \(= \frac{1}{3} + 3 = \frac{10}{3} = -\frac{b}{a} = \frac{10}{3}\). ✓
Product \(= \frac{1}{3} \times 3 = 1 = \frac{c}{a} = \frac{3}{3} = 1\). ✓

Final Answer: (b) \(k = 3\); zeroes \(\frac{1}{3}\) and 3; relationship verified.
Solution 21

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Zeroes of a quadratic, rejecting non-meaningful roots, forming a quadratic from a given area.

(a) \(2x^2 + 5x + 2 = 2x^2 + 4x + x + 2 = 2x(x + 2) + 1(x + 2) = (2x + 1)(x + 2)\).
Zeroes: \(2x + 1 = 0 \Rightarrow x = -\frac{1}{2}\) and \(x + 2 = 0 \Rightarrow x = -2\).
Both zeroes are negative. A dimension (length or breadth) must be positive, so neither zero can represent a side of the ground — in fact the polynomial above represents the area in terms of the positive quantity \(x\), whose valid value is found in part (b).

(b) If area is 77: \(2x^2 + 5x + 2 = 77 \Rightarrow 2x^2 + 5x - 75 = 0\).
\(2x^2 + 5x - 75 = 2x^2 + 15x - 10x - 75 = x(2x + 15) - 5(2x + 15) = (2x + 15)(x - 5)\).
So \(x = 5\) (the value \(x = -\frac{15}{2}\) is rejected as negative).
Length = \(2(5) + 1 = 11\) m and breadth = \(5 + 2 = 7\) m.

Check: \(11 \times 7 = 77\) m². ✓

Final Answer: (a) zeroes \(-\frac{1}{2}\) and \(-2\), both rejected for a ground    (b) \(x = 5\); ground is 11 m × 7 m
