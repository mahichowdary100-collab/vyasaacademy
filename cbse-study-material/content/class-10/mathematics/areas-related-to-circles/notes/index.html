Class 10 Mathematics – Chapter 11: Areas Related to Circles

Learn how a circle is divided into sectors and segments, and how to calculate their areas, arc lengths and perimeters. Original worked examples and clear diagrams build each formula step by step.

1. Circle measurements you will use

A circle has radius \(r\), diameter \(2r\), circumference \(2\pi r\) and area \(\pi r^2\). A central angle measures the turn made by two radii at the centre. The full turn around the centre is \(360^\circ\).

Keep the units consistent

If the radius is in centimetres, arc lengths are in centimetres and areas are in square centimetres. Convert units before substituting if the question mixes units.

2. Sectors and arc length

A sector is the region enclosed by two radii and the arc between their endpoints. The smaller region is the minor sector; the remaining region is the major sector. Unless specified, “sector” usually means the minor sector.

Sector formulas

For radius \(r\) and central angle \(\theta^\circ\):

\[\text{Sector area}=\frac{\theta}{360}\pi r^2,\qquad \text{Arc length}=\frac{\theta}{360}(2\pi r).\]

The perimeter of a sector is the arc plus both radii: \(2r+\frac{\theta}{360}(2\pi r)\).

These formulas follow by taking the same fraction \(\theta/360\) of the full circle. For a semicircle, \(\theta=180^\circ\); for a quadrant, \(\theta=90^\circ\).

3. Segments of a circle

A segment is the region between a chord and its corresponding arc. A chord divides the circle into a minor segment and a major segment.

Segment area

\[\text{Minor segment area}=\text{area of corresponding sector}-\text{area of triangle formed by the two radii and chord}.\]

\[\text{Major segment area}=\pi r^2-\text{minor segment area}.\]

In a 90-degree sector, the two radii are perpendicular, so the triangle area is \(\frac12r^2\). In a 60-degree sector the triangle is equilateral with side \(r\), and in a 120-degree sector its included angle is \(120^\circ\). These are the three central angles specified for segment-area problems in the 2026–27 CBSE Class X Mathematics curriculum.

4. Major sectors and segments

The minor and major central angles add to \(360^\circ\). If the minor angle is \(\theta^\circ\), the major angle is \(360^\circ-\theta^\circ\).

For a major segment, it is often quickest to subtract the minor segment from the total circle area. For a major sector, use either the reflex angle in the sector formula or subtract the minor sector area.

5. Step-by-step examples

Example 1: A quarter-circle sector

Find the area, arc length and perimeter of a sector with radius \(14\text{ cm}\) and angle \(90^\circ\). Use \(\pi=\frac{22}{7}\).

The sector is \(\frac{90}{360}=\frac14\) of the circle.

\[\text{Area}=\frac14\pi(14)^2=49\pi=154\text{ cm}^2.\]

\[\text{Arc}=\frac14(2\pi\times14)=7\pi=22\text{ cm}.\]

\[\text{Perimeter}=2(14)+22=50\text{ cm}.\]

Answer: area \(154\text{ cm}^2\), arc length \(22\text{ cm}\), perimeter \(50\text{ cm}\).

Example 2: A 120-degree sector

A sector has radius \(21\text{ cm}\) and central angle \(120^\circ\). Find its area and arc length using \(\pi=\frac{22}{7}\).

\(\frac{120}{360}=\frac13\) of the full circle.

\[\text{Area}=\frac13\pi(21)^2=147\pi=462\text{ cm}^2.\]

\[\text{Arc}=\frac13(2\pi\times21)=14\pi=44\text{ cm}.\]

Answer: area \(462\text{ cm}^2\), arc length \(44\text{ cm}\).

Example 3: A minor segment with a right angle

Find the minor-segment area when \(r=14\text{ cm}\) and the central angle is \(90^\circ\). Use \(\pi=\frac{22}{7}\).

Sector area: \(\frac14\times\frac{22}{7}\times14^2=154\text{ cm}^2\).

The radii are perpendicular, so triangle area is \(\frac12\times14\times14=98\text{ cm}^2\).

\[\text{Segment area}=154-98=56\text{ cm}^2.\]

Answer: \(56\text{ cm}^2\).

Example 4: A 60-degree minor segment

Find the minor-segment area for \(r=14\text{ cm}\) and central angle \(60^\circ\), using \(\pi=\frac{22}{7}\). Give the answer to two decimal places.

The sector is one-sixth of the circle:

\[\text{Sector area}=\frac16\times\frac{22}{7}\times14^2=\frac{308}{3}\text{ cm}^2.\]

The triangle is equilateral with side \(14\text{ cm}\), so its area is \(\frac{\sqrt3}{4}(14)^2=49\sqrt3\text{ cm}^2\).

\[\text{Segment area}=\frac{308}{3}-49\sqrt3\approx17.80\text{ cm}^2.\]

Answer: approximately \(17.80\text{ cm}^2\).

Example 5: A 120-degree minor segment

Find the minor-segment area for \(r=14\text{ cm}\) and central angle \(120^\circ\). Use \(\pi=\frac{22}{7}\), rounding to two decimal places.

The sector area is one-third of the circle: \(\frac13\times\frac{22}{7}\times14^2=\frac{616}{3}\text{ cm}^2\).

Drop a perpendicular from the centre to the chord. It bisects the \(120^\circ\) angle into two \(60^\circ\) angles. The half-chord is \(14\sin60^\circ=7\sqrt3\text{ cm}\), and the perpendicular height is \(14\cos60^\circ=7\text{ cm}\). Thus the full triangle area is \(2\times\frac12\times7\sqrt3\times7=49\sqrt3\text{ cm}^2\).

\[\text{Segment area}=\frac{616}{3}-49\sqrt3\approx120.46\text{ cm}^2.\]

Answer: approximately \(120.46\text{ cm}^2\).

Example 6: A major sector

Find the area of the major sector when \(r=7\text{ cm}\) and the minor central angle is \(90^\circ\). Use \(\pi=\frac{22}{7}\).

The major angle is \(360^\circ-90^\circ=270^\circ\), three-fourths of a circle.

\[\text{Major sector area}=\frac34\pi(7)^2=\frac{147\pi}{4}=115.5\text{ cm}^2.\]

Answer: \(115.5\text{ cm}^2\).

6. Common mistakes

  • Using \(\theta/180\) instead of \(\theta/360\) in sector and arc formulas.
  • Substituting the diameter in place of the radius.
  • Giving a sector perimeter as just its arc; both radii are part of the boundary.
  • For a segment, adding the triangle area instead of subtracting it from the sector.
  • Using the minor angle to calculate a major sector without taking its complement.
  • Rounding intermediate values too early or omitting square units from area.

7. Examination tips

  • Write down \(r\), \(\theta\) and the required quantity before substituting.
  • Reduce \(\theta/360\) first to simplify arithmetic.
  • For segment questions, show the sector and triangle calculations separately.
  • For 60 degrees, identify the equilateral triangle; for 90 degrees, identify the right triangle; for 120 degrees, use the included-angle area relation.
  • Use the major angle for a major sector, or subtract the minor sector from \(\pi r^2\).
  • Keep \(\pi\) exact until the end unless a value is specified. Round only the final decimal answer.
  • Include units: lengths use cm or m; areas use cm\(^2\) or m\(^2\).

8. Quick revision

Circle: circumference \(2\pi r\); area \(\pi r^2\).

Sector: area \(\frac{\theta}{360}\pi r^2\); arc length \(\frac{\theta}{360}(2\pi r)\).

Sector perimeter: \(2r+\text{arc length}\).

Minor segment: sector area minus triangle area.

Major segment: \(\pi r^2-\text{minor segment area}\).

Complementary angles: \(\theta_{\text{major}}=360^\circ-\theta_{\text{minor}}\).

CBSE 2026–27 segment-angle scope: \(60^\circ,90^\circ,120^\circ\).

9. Self-practice

These are original revision questions. Keep exact values where appropriate and include units.

  1. A circle has radius \(21\text{ cm}\). Find the area of its \(90^\circ\) sector and arc length. Use \(\pi=\frac{22}{7}\).
  2. Find the area of a \(120^\circ\) sector of radius \(14\text{ cm}\), in terms of \(\pi\).
  3. Find the minor-segment area for \(r=7\text{ cm}\) and a \(90^\circ\) central angle. Use \(\pi=\frac{22}{7}\).
  4. For \(r=14\text{ cm}\), \(\theta=60^\circ\), express the minor-segment area exactly and approximately, using \(\pi=\frac{22}{7}\).
  5. Find the area and arc length of a \(300^\circ\) major sector of radius \(6\text{ cm}\).
  6. For \(r=14\text{ cm}\) and minor angle \(90^\circ\), find the major-segment area using \(\pi=\frac{22}{7}\).
Check your answers
  1. Area \(346.5\text{ cm}^2\); arc length \(33\text{ cm}\).
  2. \(\frac{196\pi}{3}\text{ cm}^2\).
  3. Sector \(38.5\text{ cm}^2\), triangle \(24.5\text{ cm}^2\); segment \(14\text{ cm}^2\).
  4. \(\frac{98\pi}{3}-49\sqrt3\text{ cm}^2\), approximately \(17.80\text{ cm}^2\).
  5. Area \(30\pi\text{ cm}^2\); arc length \(10\pi\text{ cm}\).
  6. \(560\text{ cm}^2\).
