Detailed Solutions
Solution 1

Source: CBSE Board Paper 2025 (Mathematics Standard, 30/1/1)

Concept Used: Substituting a given solution into a linear equation to find unknown constants.

Put \(x = 1\), \(y = 2\) into both equations:
\(2(1) - 3(2) + a = 0 \Rightarrow 2 - 6 + a = 0 \Rightarrow a = 4\).
\(2(1) + 3(2) - b = 0 \Rightarrow 2 + 6 - b = 0 \Rightarrow b = 8\).

Now \(2a = 2 \times 4 = 8 = b\), so \(2a = b\).

Final Answer: (B) \(2a = b\)
Solution 2

Source: CBSE Board Paper 2025 (Mathematics Standard, 30/4/1)

Concept Used: Comparing coefficients of \(a_1x + b_1y = c_1\) and \(a_2x + b_2y = c_2\) for infinitely many solutions \(\left(\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{c_1}{c_2}\right)\).

Here \(a_1 = p,\ b_1 = 3,\ c_1 = p - 3\) and \(a_2 = 12,\ b_2 = p,\ c_2 = p\).
\(\frac{p}{12} = \frac{3}{p} \Rightarrow p^2 = 36 \Rightarrow p = \pm 6\).

Now check which value also satisfies the ratio of the constant terms. The two equations in standard form are \(px + 3y - (p - 3) = 0\) and \(12x + py - p = 0\), so \(\frac{c_1}{c_2} = \frac{p - 3}{p}\).

For \(p = 6\): \(\frac{p - 3}{p} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2} = \frac{6}{12}\) ✓ — infinitely many solutions.
For \(p = -6\): \(\frac{p - 3}{p} = \frac{-9}{-6} = \frac{3}{2} \neq \frac{-6}{12} = -\frac{1}{2}\) ✗ — the pair has no solution.
Hence only \(p = 6\) works.

Final Answer: (B) \(p = 6\) only
Solution 3

Source: CBSE Board Paper 2024 (Mathematics Standard, 30/3/1)

Concept Used: Condition for infinitely many solutions: \(\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{c_1}{c_2}\).

Comparison: \(\frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}\), \(\frac{-1}{-k} = \frac{1}{k}\), and \(\frac{8}{16} = \frac{1}{2}\).
For infinitely many solutions all three ratios are equal:
\(\frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{k} = \frac{1}{2} \Rightarrow k = 2\).

Final Answer: (B) \(k = 2\)
Solution 4

Source: CBSE Board Paper 2024 (Mathematics Standard, 30/5/1)

Concept Used: Condition for parallel (inconsistent) lines: \(\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} \neq \frac{c_1}{c_2}\).

\(\frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}\), \(\frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}\) but \(\frac{5}{7} \neq \frac{1}{2}\).
The ratio of the \(x\)-coefficients equals the ratio of the \(y\)-coefficients, but the ratio of the constant terms is different. Hence the lines are parallel and the system has no solution.

Final Answer: (A) Parallel
Solution 5

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Geometry of a pair of linear equations — parallel distinct lines never meet.

A solution of a pair of linear equations is the point where the two lines meet. Two distinct parallel lines never meet, so the pair has no common point — that is, no solution. Such a system is called inconsistent.

Final Answer: (C) No solution
Solution 6

Source: Original question

Concept Used: The solution of a pair of linear equations is the intersection point of its graphs.

Two lines that meet at exactly one point represent a consistent pair with a unique solution — the coordinates of that single intersection point, \((3, -2)\).

Final Answer: (B) Exactly one solution
Solution 7

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Solving a pair by elimination (adding the equations).

Add the two equations: \((x + y) + (x - y) = 7 + 1 \Rightarrow 2x = 8 \Rightarrow x = 4\).
Then \(4 + y = 7 \Rightarrow y = 3\). The point is \((4, 3)\).

Final Answer: (B) \((4, 3)\)
Solution 8

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Condition for a unique solution: \(\frac{a_1}{a_2} \neq \frac{b_1}{b_2}\).

\(\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{k}{3}\) and \(\frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}\).
A unique solution requires \(\frac{k}{3} \neq \frac{1}{3} \Rightarrow k \neq 1\).

Final Answer: (C) \(k \neq 1\)
Solution 9

Source: CBSE Board Paper 2025 (Mathematics Standard, 30/2/1)

Concept Used: Consistency of a pair by comparing ratios of coefficients.

For \(px + 3y + 59 = 0\) and \(2x + 6y + 118 = 0\):
\(\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{p}{2}\), \(\frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}\), \(\frac{c_1}{c_2} = \frac{59}{118} = \frac{1}{2}\).

Assertion: Infinitely many solutions when all three ratios are equal, i.e. \(\frac{p}{2} = \frac{1}{2} \Rightarrow p = 1\). The Assertion is true.

Reason: When \(p \neq 1\), \(\frac{p}{2} \neq \frac{1}{2}\) so the pair has a unique solution. The Reason is also true.

However, the Reason only states when the pair is not consistent with infinitely many solutions; it does not explain why \(p = 1\) produces infinitely many solutions (which requires comparing all three ratios). Hence R is not the correct explanation of A.

Final Answer: (b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
Solution 10

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Coincident lines represent a dependent pair with infinitely many solutions.

Dividing \(4x + 10y = 22\) by 2 gives \(2x + 5y = 11\), which is exactly the first equation. So both equations represent the very same straight line (coincident lines). Every point of this line is a common solution, so the pair has infinitely many solutions. The Reason correctly explains the Assertion.

Final Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
Solution 11

Source: CBSE Board Paper 2024 (Mathematics Standard, 30/1/1)

Concept Used: Elimination method for a pair of linear equations, followed by verification.

Make the coefficients of \(y\) equal in magnitude:
Multiply the first equation by 7: \(49x - 14y = 35\).
Multiply the second equation by 2: \(16x + 14y = 30\).
Adding: \(65x = 65 \Rightarrow x = 1\).

Substitute \(x = 1\) in \(7x - 2y = 5\):
\(7(1) - 2y = 5 \Rightarrow 2y = 2 \Rightarrow y = 1\).

Verification: In \(8x + 7y = 15\): \(8(1) + 7(1) = 15\). ✓

Final Answer: \(x = 1,\ y = 1\)
Solution 12

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Substitution method — expressing one variable in terms of the other.

From \(3x - 2y = 5\): \(x = \frac{5 + 2y}{3}\).

Substitute into \(2x + 3y = 12\):
\(2\left(\frac{5 + 2y}{3}\right) + 3y = 12\)
Multiplying by 3: \(10 + 4y + 9y = 36 \Rightarrow 13y = 26 \Rightarrow y = 2\).

Then \(x = \frac{5 + 2(2)}{3} = \frac{9}{3} = 3\).

Check: \(3(3) - 2(2) = 5\). ✓

Final Answer: \(x = 3,\ y = 2\)
Solution 13

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Condition for no solution: \(\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} \neq \frac{c_1}{c_2}\).

For \(kx + 2y = 3\) and \(6x + 4y = 11\):
\(\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{k}{6}\), \(\frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}\), \(\frac{c_1}{c_2} = \frac{3}{11}\).

No solution requires \(\frac{k}{6} = \frac{1}{2} \Rightarrow k = 3\).
Then \(\frac{c_1}{c_2} = \frac{3}{11} \neq \frac{1}{2}\), so the pair is inconsistent (parallel lines, no solution).

Final Answer: \(k = 3\)
Solution 14

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Forming equations from a real-life (cost) situation and solving them.

Let the cost of one pen be ₹\(p\) and one eraser be ₹\(e\).
\(2p + 3e = 46\)  …(1)
\(3p + 2e = 54\)  …(2)

Adding (1) and (2): \(5p + 5e = 100 \Rightarrow p + e = 20\).  …(3)
From (3), \(p = 20 - e\). Putting this in (1):
\(2(20 - e) + 3e = 46 \Rightarrow 40 + e = 46 \Rightarrow e = 6\).
Hence \(p = 20 - 6 = 14\).

Check: \(2(14) + 3(6) = 46\) and \(3(14) + 2(6) = 54\). ✓

Final Answer: One pen costs ₹14 and one eraser costs ₹6.
Solution 15

Source: CBSE Board Paper 2024 (Mathematics Standard, 30/1/1)

Concept Used: Age problems — expressing age conditions as linear equations.

Let Rashmi's present age be \(R\) years and Nazma's be \(N\) years.

Three years ago: \(R - 3 = 3(N - 3) \Rightarrow R = 3N - 6\).  …(1)
Ten years later: \(R + 10 = 2(N + 10) \Rightarrow R = 2N + 10\).  …(2)

From (1) and (2): \(3N - 6 = 2N + 10 \Rightarrow N = 16\).
Then \(R = 2(16) + 10 = 42\).

Check: 3 years ago: 39 and 13 (thrice) ✓   10 years later: 52 and 26 (twice) ✓

Final Answer: Rashmi is 42 years old and Nazma is 16 years old.
Solution 16

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Solving a pair graphically and computing an area bounded by the lines.

(a) Plotting points for each line:
\(x - y = 1\): \((1, 0)\), \((0, -1)\), \((3, 2)\).
\(x + y = 5\): \((5, 0)\), \((0, 5)\), \((3, 2)\).

Both lines pass through \((3, 2)\), and they are not parallel, so the unique solution is \((3, 2)\).
Check: \(3 - 2 = 1\) and \(3 + 2 = 5\). ✓

(b) On the y-axis, \(x = 0\). The cuts are \((0, -1)\) for \(x - y = 1\) and \((0, 5)\) for \(x + y = 5\).
The vertical base of the triangle, along the y-axis, has length \(5 - (-1) = 6\) units.
The third vertex is \((3, 2)\), whose perpendicular distance from the y-axis is 3 units.
Area \(= \frac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 3 = 9\) square units.

Final Answer: (a) Solution \((3, 2)\)    (b) y-axis cuts \((0, -1)\) and \((0, 5)\); area \(= 9\) sq units
Solution 17

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Two-digit-number problems — expressing digits as variables.

(a) Let the tens digit be \(x\) and the units digit be \(y\). The number is \(10x + y\).
Product condition: \(xy = 12\).  …(1)
Interchange condition: \(10x + y + 36 = 10y + x \Rightarrow 9x + 36 = 9y \Rightarrow x - y = -4\).  …(2)

(b) From (2), \(y = x + 4\). Putting this in (1):
\(x(x + 4) = 12 \Rightarrow x^2 + 4x - 12 = 0 \Rightarrow (x + 6)(x - 2) = 0 \Rightarrow x = 2\) (rejecting \(x = -6\) as a digit).
So \(y = 6\), and the number is \(10(2) + 6 = 26\).

Check: Product \(2 \times 6 = 12\) and \(26 + 36 = 62\) (digits interchanged). ✓

Final Answer: (a) \(xy = 12\) and \(x - y = -4\)    (b) The number is 26.
Solution 18

Source: Original question

(a) Length \(y = 2\)(breadth) \(+ 3 \Rightarrow y = 2x + 3\).  …(1)
Perimeter \(2(x + y) = 36 \Rightarrow x + y = 18\).  …(2)

(b) Substitute (1) in (2): \(x + (2x + 3) = 18 \Rightarrow 3x = 15 \Rightarrow x = 5\).

(c) Area \(= 13 \times 5 = 65\) cm².

Solution 19(b)

Source: Original question

Concept Used: Fraction problems — setting up equations from given conditions.

Let the fraction be \(\frac{n}{d}\).

Adding 1 to the numerator gives \(\frac{4}{5}\): \(\frac{n + 1}{d} = \frac{4}{5} \Rightarrow 5n + 5 = 4d\).  …(1)
Adding 1 to the denominator gives \(\frac{1}{2}\): \(\frac{n}{d + 1} = \frac{1}{2} \Rightarrow 2n = d + 1\).  …(2)

From (2), \(d = 2n - 1\). Substitute in (1):
\(5n + 5 = 4(2n - 1) \Rightarrow 5n + 5 = 8n - 4 \Rightarrow 9 = 3n \Rightarrow n = 3\).
Then \(d = 2(3) - 1 = 5\).

Check: \(\frac{3 + 1}{5} = \frac{4}{5}\) and \(\frac{3}{5 + 1} = \frac{1}{2}\). ✓

Final Answer: (b) The fraction is \(\frac{3}{5}\).

Solution 20(b)

Source: CBSE Board Paper 2024 (Mathematics Standard, 30/2/1)

Concept Used: Test-marks problem — forming equations with right and wrong answers.

Let the number of right answers be \(x\), wrong answers be \(y\), so total questions \(= x + y\).

First scheme: \(3x - y = 40\).  …(1)
Second scheme: \(4x - 2y = 50\).  …(2)

From (1), \(y = 3x - 40\). Substitute in (2):
\(4x - 2(3x - 40) = 50 \Rightarrow 4x - 6x + 80 = 50 \Rightarrow 2x = 30 \Rightarrow x = 15\).
Then \(y = 3(15) - 40 = 5\).

Total questions \(= x + y = 15 + 5 = 20\).

Check: \(3(15) - 5 = 40\) and \(4(15) - 2(5) = 50\). ✓

Final Answer: (b) 20 questions (15 right and 5 wrong)
Solution 21

Source: CBSE Board Paper 2024 (Mathematics Standard, 30/5/1) — Case Study (scene text lightly abridged)

Concept Used: Interpreting a real-life situation as a pair of linear equations.

(a) With \(x\) children and \(y\) adults:
Total tickets: \(x + y = 300\).  …(1)
Total collection: \(150x + 250y = 55000\).  …(2)

(b) From (1), \(x = 300 - y\). Substitute in (2):
\(150(300 - y) + 250y = 55000 \Rightarrow 45000 + 100y = 55000 \Rightarrow y = 100\).
Then \(x = 300 - 100 = 200\).

Check: \(200 + 100 = 300\) tickets and \(150(200) + 250(100) = 30000 + 25000 = 55000\). ✓

Final Answer: (a) \(x + y = 300\) and \(150x + 250y = 55000\)    (b) 200 children visited the park.
