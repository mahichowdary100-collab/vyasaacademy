Class 10 Mathematics – Chapter 12: Surface Areas and Volumes

Build each composite solid from familiar shapes, then calculate its outside surface area and total volume. These notes follow the CBSE 2026–27 scope and use clear diagrams and original worked examples.

1. What the 2026–27 syllabus covers

CBSE lists surface areas and volumes of combinations of any two of these solids: cubes, cuboids, spheres, hemispheres, right circular cylinders and right circular cones. You should be able to picture the combined object, identify its dimensions and calculate its outside area and total volume.

Scope note

The formula recap below supplies the tools needed for combinations. These notes stay within the published 2026–27 combination-of-solids scope; they do not add frustums or conversion of one solid into another.

Use the same unit for every linear measurement before substituting. Surface area is measured in square units; volume is measured in cubic units.

2. Formula guide for the listed solids

SolidSurface areaVolume
Cube, side \(a\)Total: \(6a^2\)\(a^3\)
Cuboid, length \(l\), breadth \(b\), height \(h\)Total: \(2(lb+bh+hl)\)\(lbh\)
Sphere, radius \(r\)\(4\pi r^2\)\(\frac43\pi r^3\)
Hemisphere, radius \(r\)Curved: \(2\pi r^2\); total: \(3\pi r^2\)\(\frac23\pi r^3\)
Right circular cylinder, radius \(r\), height \(h\)Curved: \(2\pi rh\); total: \(2\pi r(h+r)\)\(\pi r^2h\)
Right circular cone, radius \(r\), height \(h\), slant height \(l\)Curved: \(\pi rl\); total: \(\pi r(l+r)\)\(\frac13\pi r^2h\)

For a right circular cone, the radius, perpendicular height and slant height make a right triangle: \[l^2=r^2+h^2,\qquad l=\sqrt{r^2+h^2}.\] Use the slant height for the cone's curved surface area and the perpendicular height for its volume.

3. How to combine solids

Divide the object into its component solids. Add their volumes when the solids are joined without overlap. For outside surface area, include only surfaces a person could touch from the outside. The joining faces are hidden.

Hemisphere on a cylinder

\[\text{Outside area}=2\pi r^2+2\pi rh+\pi r^2=3\pi r^2+2\pi rh.\]

\[\text{Volume}=\frac23\pi r^3+\pi r^2h.\]

Joined equal circular faces

If you add the total surface areas of both components, subtract the shared circular face twice: once because it was counted on each solid. It is often clearer to list the exposed curved surfaces and the remaining outer bases directly.

4. Step-by-step examples

Example 1: Cone joined to a cylinder

A cone is placed on top of a cylinder. Both have radius \(7\text{ cm}\). The cylinder height is \(10\text{ cm}\), and the cone height is \(24\text{ cm}\). Find the outside surface area and total volume. Use \(\pi=\frac{22}{7}\).

First find the cone slant height: \[l=\sqrt{7^2+24^2}=\sqrt{49+576}=25\text{ cm}.\]

The outside consists of the cone's curved surface, the cylinder's curved surface and the bottom circular base. The joined circle is hidden.

\[\begin{aligned}\text{Outside area}&=\pi rl+2\pi rh+\pi r^2\\&=\pi(7)(25)+2\pi(7)(10)+\pi(7^2)\\&=364\pi=1144\text{ cm}^2.\end{aligned}\]

Add the component volumes:

\[\begin{aligned}\text{Volume}&=\frac13\pi r^2(24)+\pi r^2(10)\\&=392\pi+490\pi=882\pi\\&=2772\text{ cm}^3.\end{aligned}\]

Answer: outside surface area \(1144\text{ cm}^2\); volume \(2772\text{ cm}^3\).

Example 2: Hemisphere on a cylinder

A solid has a hemisphere on top of a cylinder. Both radii are \(3\text{ cm}\), and the cylinder is \(8\text{ cm}\) high. Find its outside area and volume in terms of \(\pi\).

The exposed area is the hemisphere's curved area, cylinder's curved area and the bottom circle:

\[\begin{aligned}\text{Outside area}&=2\pi(3^2)+2\pi(3)(8)+\pi(3^2)\\&=18\pi+48\pi+9\pi=75\pi\text{ cm}^2.\end{aligned}\]

\[\begin{aligned}\text{Volume}&=\frac23\pi(3^3)+\pi(3^2)(8)\\&=18\pi+72\pi=90\pi\text{ cm}^3.\end{aligned}\]

Answer: outside area \(75\pi\text{ cm}^2\); volume \(90\pi\text{ cm}^3\).

Example 3: Cone joined to a hemisphere

A cone and a hemisphere share a circular base of radius \(3\text{ cm}\). The cone's perpendicular height is \(4\text{ cm}\). Find the outside area and volume.

The cone slant height is \(l=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5\text{ cm}\). The shared circular base is internal, so the exposed area is only the curved cone and curved hemisphere:

\[\text{Outside area}=\pi(3)(5)+2\pi(3^2)=15\pi+18\pi=33\pi\text{ cm}^2.\]

\[\text{Volume}=\frac13\pi(3^2)(4)+\frac23\pi(3^3)=12\pi+18\pi=30\pi\text{ cm}^3.\]

Answer: outside area \(33\pi\text{ cm}^2\); volume \(30\pi\text{ cm}^3\).

Example 4: Cube on a cuboid

A cube of side \(4\text{ cm}\) rests centrally on a cuboid with top \(8\text{ cm}\times8\text{ cm}\) and height \(3\text{ cm}\). Find the outside area and total volume.

The cuboid total surface area is \(2(8\cdot8+8\cdot3+8\cdot3)=224\text{ cm}^2\). The cube surface area is \(6(4^2)=96\text{ cm}^2\).

The square contact face has area \(4^2=16\text{ cm}^2\). It was counted once on each solid, so subtract \(2(16)\):

\[\text{Outside area}=224+96-32=288\text{ cm}^2.\]

\[\text{Volume}=8\cdot8\cdot3+4^3=192+64=256\text{ cm}^3.\]

Answer: outside area \(288\text{ cm}^2\); volume \(256\text{ cm}^3\).

5. Common mistakes

  • Counting a joined circular or square face in the outside surface area.
  • Subtracting the shared face only once after adding both total surface areas; it was counted twice.
  • Using cone slant height \(l\) in the volume formula instead of perpendicular height \(h\).
  • Using total surface area for a curved part whose flat base is joined inside the solid.
  • Adding lengths or areas when the question asks for volume; keep square and cubic units distinct.
  • Adding component volumes when the diagram shows overlapping solids rather than solids joined at a face.

6. Examination tips

  • Sketch or mark each component and label the dimensions you know.
  • Write the slant-height calculation before using the cone surface-area formula.
  • Trace the outside boundary and list every exposed curved surface and flat base.
  • For total area formulas, show explicitly which interface area is removed.
  • For volume, calculate each component separately, then add the results.
  • Keep answers in terms of \(\pi\) until a value is requested; write square or cubic units.

7. Quick revision

Combination volume: add component volumes when the solids are joined without overlap.

Combination outside area: add only exposed surfaces; omit hidden joining faces.

Cone: \(l^2=r^2+h^2\), curved area \(\pi rl\), volume \(\frac13\pi r^2h\).

Cylinder: curved area \(2\pi rh\), volume \(\pi r^2h\).

Hemisphere: curved area \(2\pi r^2\), volume \(\frac23\pi r^3\).

Sphere: surface area \(4\pi r^2\), volume \(\frac43\pi r^3\).

Cube: total area \(6a^2\), volume \(a^3\).

Cuboid: total area \(2(lb+bh+hl)\), volume \(lbh\).

These are original revision questions written for the 2026–27 scope.

  2. A hemisphere of radius \(3\text{ cm}\) sits on a cylinder of radius \(3\text{ cm}\) and height \(5\text{ cm}\). Find the outside area and volume in terms of \(\pi\).
  3. A cone of radius \(6\text{ cm}\), height \(8\text{ cm}\), is joined to a hemisphere of the same radius. Find the outside area and volume in terms of \(\pi\).
  4. A cube of side \(3\text{ cm}\) rests on a cuboid measuring \(6\text{ cm}\times6\text{ cm}\times2\text{ cm}\). Find the outside area and total volume.
  5. A cone has radius \(5\text{ cm}\) and perpendicular height \(12\text{ cm}\). Find its slant height before using it in a combined-solid surface-area question.
