Detailed Solutions
Solution 1

Concept Used: A rational number can be written as \(\frac{p}{q}\) with integers \(p, q\) \((q \neq 0)\); a number that cannot is irrational.

3, \(\frac{9}{2}\) and 15 can all be written as ratios of integers. Since 7 is not a perfect square, \(\sqrt{7}\) cannot be written as a ratio of integers.

Final Answer: (C) \(\sqrt{7}\)
Solution 2

Concept Used: Writing a number as a product of primes (Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic).

\(252 = 2 \times 126 = 2 \times 2 \times 63 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 21 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 7 = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 7\).

Final Answer: (A) \(2^2 \times 3^2 \times 7\)
Solution 3

Concept Used: HCF is the product of the smallest power of each common prime factor.

\(18 = 2 \times 3^2\), \(30 = 2 \times 3 \times 5\). Common primes are 2 and 3. HCF = \(2^1 \times 3^1 = 6\).

Final Answer: (B) 6
Solution 4

Concept Used: LCM is the product of the greatest power of each prime factor appearing in either number.

\(12 = 2^2 \times 3\), \(18 = 2 \times 3^2\). LCM = \(2^2 \times 3^2 = 36\).

Final Answer: (C) 36
Solution 5

Concept Used: A composite number has more than two factors.

23, 29 and 31 are prime (exactly two factors). \(21 = 3 \times 7\) has four factors, so it is composite.

Final Answer: (C) 21
Solution 6

Concept Used: For two positive integers \(a\) and \(b\), HCF(\(a, b\)) × LCM(\(a, b\)) = \(a \times b\).

LCM(56, 98) = \(\frac{56 \times 98}{\text{HCF}} = \frac{56 \times 98}{14} = \frac{5488}{14} = 392\).

Final Answer: (B) 392
Solution 7

Concept Used: Prime factorisation: 210 = \(2 \times 3 \times 5 \times 7\).

The distinct primes present are 2, 3, 5 and 7, i.e. 4 distinct primes.

Final Answer: (C) 4
Solution 8

Concept Used: Identifying irrational numbers and comparing square roots.

\(\sqrt{4} = 2\) is rational, \(\frac{5}{2}\) and \(\frac{9}{4}\) are rational. Since \(4 < 6 < 9\), we have \(2 < \sqrt{6} < 3\), and 6 is not a perfect square, so \(\sqrt{6}\) is an irrational number between 2 and 3.

Final Answer: (B) \(\sqrt{6}\)
Solution 9

Concept Used: Meaning and computation of HCF.

\(48 = 2^4 \times 3\) and \(60 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 5\). HCF = \(2^2 \times 3 = 12\), so Assertion is true. The Reason correctly defines HCF and explains why 12 is the answer. Both are true and R is the correct explanation.

Final Answer: (A)
Solution 10

Concept Used: Definitions of rational and irrational numbers.

By definition, an irrational number cannot be written as \(\frac{p}{q}\). So the Assertion is false. The Reason correctly defines a rational number, so it is true.

Final Answer: (D) A is false, but R is true.
Solution 11

Concept Used: Prime factorisation by repeated division.

\(156 = 2 \times 78 = 2 \times 2 \times 39 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 13\).

Final Answer: \(156 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 13\)
Solution 12

Concept Used: HCF of three numbers using prime factorisation.

\(72 = 2^3 \times 3^2\), \(108 = 2^2 \times 3^3\), \(144 = 2^4 \times 3^2\).
Common primes: 2 and 3. Smallest power of 2 is \(2^2\) and of 3 is \(3^2\).
HCF = \(2^2 \times 3^2 = 4 \times 9 = 36\).

Final Answer: HCF = 36
Solution 13

Concept Used: Perfect squares and surds.

(i) \(\sqrt{225} = 15\), an integer → rational.
(ii) \(3\sqrt{5}\): 5 is not a perfect square, so \(\sqrt{5}\) is irrational and \(3\sqrt{5}\) (non-zero rational × irrational) is irrational.
(iii) \(\frac{\sqrt{8}}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{8}{2}} = \sqrt{4} = 2\), an integer → rational.
(iv) \(4\sqrt{81} = 4 \times 9 = 36\), an integer → rational.

Final Answer: (i) rational, (ii) irrational, (iii) rational, (iv) rational
Solution 14

Given: LCM = 360, HCF = 15, one number = 45.

Concept Used: HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers.

Other number = \(\frac{\text{HCF} \times \text{LCM}}{45} = \frac{15 \times 360}{45} = \frac{5400}{45} = 120\).

Check: \(45 = 3^2 \times 5\), \(120 = 2^3 \times 3 \times 5\). LCM = \(2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5 = 360\) and HCF = \(3 \times 5 = 15\). ✓

Final Answer: The other number is 120.
Solution 15

Given: Intervals of 24, 36 and 48 minutes; all ring together at 12 noon.

Concept Used: LCM of the intervals gives the time after which all ring together again.

\(24 = 2^3 \times 3\), \(36 = 2^2 \times 3^2\), \(48 = 2^4 \times 3\).
LCM = \(2^4 \times 3^2 = 16 \times 9 = 144\) minutes.

(a) They ring together again after 144 minutes (2 hours 24 minutes).

(b) Number of times the 24-minute bell rings in 144 minutes = \(\frac{144}{24} = 6\) times (excluding 12 noon).

Final Answer: (a) 144 minutes    (b) 6 times
Solution 16

Given: 84 red, 120 blue, 156 green beads.

Concept Used: The maximum number of identical garlands = HCF of the three quantities.

\(84 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 7\), \(120 = 2^3 \times 3 \times 5\), \(156 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 13\).
HCF = \(2^2 \times 3 = 12\).

(a) Maximum number of garlands = 12.

(b) Red beads per garland = \({84}/{12} = 7\), blue = \({120}/{12} = 10\), green = \({156}/{12} = 13\).

Final Answer: (a) 12 garlands    (b) 7 red, 10 blue, 13 green
Solution 17

Given: \(A = 2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5\), \(B = 2^2 \times 3^3 \times 7\).

Concept Used: HCF takes the smallest power of each common prime; LCM takes the greatest power of every prime present.

(a) HCF = \(2^2 \times 3^2 = 36\).
LCM = \(2^3 \times 3^3 \times 5 \times 7 = 8 \times 27 \times 35 = 7560\).

(b) \(A \times B = (8 \times 9 \times 5) \times (4 \times 27 \times 7) = 360 \times 756 = 272160\).
HCF × LCM = \(36 \times 7560 = 272160\).
Therefore HCF × LCM = \(A \times B\). ✓

Final Answer: (a) HCF = 36, LCM = 7560    (b) HCF × LCM = \(A \times B\) (verified)
Solution 18

Concept Used: Closure properties of rational and irrational numbers.

(i) True. Example: \(\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{2} = 2\), a rational number.

(ii) False. Example: \(\left(\sqrt{2}\right)^2 = 2\), which is rational.

(iii) True. If \(r + s\) were rational (where \(r\) is rational and \(s\) irrational), then \(s = (r + s) - r\) would be rational (difference of rationals), which contradicts \(s\) being irrational.

Final Answer: (i) True    (ii) False    (iii) True
Solution 19

Given: Rectangular slab 48 cm by 54 cm.

Concept Used: (a) HCF decides the largest square tile; (b) LCM decides the smallest square covered by whole slabs.

(a) \(48 = 2^4 \times 3\), \(54 = 2 \times 3^3\). HCF = \(2 \times 3 = 6\).
Largest square tile side = 6 cm.
Number of tiles = area of slab ÷ area of tile = \(\frac{48 \times 54}{6 \times 6} = \frac{2592}{36} = 72\).
(Alternatively, \(8 \times 9 = 72\) tiles.)

(b) LCM(48, 54) = \(2^4 \times 3^3 = 16 \times 27 = 432\).
Smallest square side = 432 cm.
Number of slabs = \(\frac{432 \times 432}{48 \times 54} = \frac{186624}{2592} = 72\) slabs.
(Alternatively, \(9 \times 8 = 72\) slabs.)

Final Answer: (a) 6 cm, 72 tiles    (b) 432 cm, 72 slabs
Solution 20(a)

Given: To prove \(\sqrt{5}\) is irrational.

Concept Used: Proof by contradiction.

Solution:
Suppose, to the contrary, that \(\sqrt{5}\) is rational. Then \(\sqrt{5} = \frac{p}{q}\), where \(p\) and \(q\) are coprime integers and \(q \neq 0\).
Squaring both sides: \(5 = \frac{p^2}{q^2}\) ⇒ \(p^2 = 5q^2\).
So 5 divides \(p^2\). Since 5 is prime, 5 must divide \(p\). Let \(p = 5k\).
Then \(25k^2 = 5q^2\) ⇒ \(q^2 = 5k^2\), so 5 divides \(q^2\), and hence 5 divides \(q\).
Thus 5 divides both \(p\) and \(q\), contradicting that \(p\) and \(q\) are coprime.
Therefore our assumption was wrong: \(\sqrt{5}\) is irrational.

Final Answer: \(\sqrt{5}\) is irrational (proved by contradiction).
Solution 20(b)

Given: To prove \(5 + 2\sqrt{3}\) is irrational.

Concept Used: Rational + irrational = irrational; \(\sqrt{3}\) is irrational (proved by the same method as \(\sqrt{5}\)).

Solution:
Suppose, to the contrary, that \(5 + 2\sqrt{3}\) is rational, say equal to \(r\).
Then \(2\sqrt{3} = r - 5\). Since \(r\) and 5 are rational, \(r - 5\) is rational, so \(2\sqrt{3}\) is rational.
But the product of the non-zero rational number 2 and \(\sqrt{3}\) would then be rational, forcing \(\sqrt{3}\) to be rational; this contradicts the fact that \(\sqrt{3}\) is irrational.
Hence our assumption was wrong: \(5 + 2\sqrt{3}\) is irrational.

Final Answer: \(5 + 2\sqrt{3}\) is irrational (proved by contradiction).
Solution 21

Given: 96 students and 72 teachers; largest possible equal groups, same number of students and teachers per group, nobody left out.

Concept Used: HCF of 96 and 72 gives the maximum number of equal groups.

\(96 = 2^5 \times 3\), \(72 = 2^3 \times 3^2\). HCF = \(2^3 \times 3 = 24\).

(a) Maximum number of groups = 24.

(b) Students per group = \(\frac{96}{24} = 4\), teachers per group = \(\frac{72}{24} = 3\).
Total people per group = \(4 + 3 = 7\).

Final Answer: (a) 24 groups    (b) 7 people per group
