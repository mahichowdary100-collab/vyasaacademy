1. Syllabus and key ideas

The 2026–27 CBSE Class X syllabus includes mean, median and mode of grouped data. It names the direct, assumed-mean and step-deviation methods for the mean and algebraic methods for median and mode. Bimodal situations are excluded.

Mean

A weighted average: every class contributes according to its frequency.

Median

The central value, with half the observations on either side in the underlying ordered data.

Mode

The value around which observations are most concentrated; estimate it from the modal class.

Grouped data give intervals rather than every individual value. The calculated mean uses class midpoints, while median and mode use interpolation within a class. They are estimates of the measures for the original observations.

2. Classes, boundaries and class marks

The frequency \(f_i\) counts observations in class \(i\). The total is \(N=\sum f_i\). The class mark is the midpoint \(x_i=(\text{lower boundary}+\text{upper boundary})/2\). Class width is the difference between the boundaries.

Here, intervals such as 20–30 mean \(20\leq x<30\). Thus 30 belongs to the next class, avoiding double counting. For inclusive classes of values recorded to the nearest whole unit, such as 10–19 and 20–29, use continuous boundaries 9.5–19.5 and 19.5–29.5 when applying grouped median or mode formulas. This uses half the gap between consecutive class limits; do not subtract 0.5 from already continuous classes like 10–20 and 20–30.

Cumulative frequency

Add frequencies successively. For frequencies 4, 7 and 12, the cumulative totals are 4, 11 and 23. The last cumulative total must equal \(N\).

4. Mean by the direct method

\[\bar{x}=\frac{\sum f_ix_i}{\sum f_i}.\]

Multiply every class mark by its frequency, add the products and divide by the total frequency.

Example 1: Direct mean of reading time

  1. Class marks are \(5,15,25,35,45\) minutes.
  2. The products \(f_ix_i\) are \(20,105,300,350,315\).
  3. Their sum is \(1090\), and the frequency total is \(40\).

\[\bar{x}=\frac{1090}{40}=27.25\text{ minutes}.\]

Answer: the estimated mean reading time is 27.25 minutes.

Do not divide by the number of classes. The denominator is the number of observations, \(40\), rather than \(5\).

5. Mean by the assumed-mean method

Choose a convenient class mark \(a\) near the centre of the distribution. Put \(d_i=x_i-a\), retaining negative signs for class marks below \(a\).

\[\bar{x}=a+\frac{\sum f_id_i}{N}.\]

This follows from \(x_i=a+d_i\): \(\sum f_ix_i=aN+\sum f_id_i\). Choosing \(a\) simplifies arithmetic; it does not change the answer.

Example 2: Use \(a=25\) minutes

The deviations are \(-20,-10,0,10,20\). Their weighted sum is \(-80-70+0+100+140=90\).

\[\bar{x}=25+\frac{90}{40}=27.25\text{ minutes}.\]

Check: this agrees with the direct method.

6. Mean by the step-deviation method

When class marks are equally spaced, divide the deviations by the common class width \(h\). Set \(u_i=(x_i-a)/h\). This produces smaller numbers.

\[\bar{x}=a+h\frac{\sum f_iu_i}{N}.\]

Example 3: Use \(a=25\) and \(h=10\)

The values of \(u_i\) are \(-2,-1,0,1,2\). The weighted values are \(-8,-7,0,10,14\), giving \(\sum f_iu_i=9\).

\[\bar{x}=25+10\left(\frac9{40}\right)=27.25\text{ minutes}.\]

Check: all three methods give the same mean. Remember to multiply the correction by \(h\).

Use the direct method when products are simple, assumed mean when subtracting a central value helps, and step deviation when the common spacing makes \(u_i\) small integers. If widths vary, direct or assumed mean avoids assuming a common class width.

7. Median of grouped data

Find \(N/2\), then select the class whose cumulative frequency first exceeds \(N/2\). For a value exactly at a cumulative boundary, the interpolation gives that shared class boundary.

\[\text{Median}=l+\left(\frac{N/2-cf}{f}\right)h.\]

  • \(l\): lower boundary of the median class.
  • \(cf\): cumulative frequency before the median class.
  • \(f\): frequency of the median class, not its cumulative frequency.
  • \(h\): width of the median class.

Example 4: Median reading time

  1. \(N/2=40/2=20\).
  2. Cumulative totals are \(4,11,23,33,40\); the first exceeding 20 is 23, so the median class is 20–30.
  3. Use \(l=20,\ cf=11,\ f=12,\ h=10\).

\[\text{Median}=20+\frac{20-11}{12}\times10=20+7.5=27.5\text{ minutes}.\]

Answer: estimated median = 27.5 minutes. It lies within the selected class, as it should.

The expression \((N/2-cf)/f\) gives the fraction of the median class needed to reach the halfway point. Multiplying by \(h\) converts that fraction into a distance along the interval.

8. Mode of grouped data

For the equal-width distributions used here, the modal class has the highest frequency. The mode formula also uses the frequencies of the immediately preceding and succeeding classes.

\[\text{Mode}=l+\left(\frac{f_1-f_0}{2f_1-f_0-f_2}\right)h.\]

  • \(l\): lower boundary of the modal class; \(h\): its width.
  • \(f_1\): modal-class frequency.
  • \(f_0\): preceding-class frequency; \(f_2\): succeeding-class frequency.

Example 5: Modal reading time

The largest frequency is 12 in class 20–30. Therefore \(l=20,\ h=10,\ f_1=12,\ f_0=7,\ f_2=10\).

\[\text{Mode}=20+\frac{12-7}{2(12)-7-10}\times10=20+\frac{50}{7}\approx27.14\text{ minutes}.\]

Answer: estimated mode = 27.14 minutes. The modal class is an interval; the mode is an estimated value inside it.

The mean (27.25), median (27.5) and mode (about 27.14) need not be equal. The approximate empirical relation \(\text{Mode}\approx3\text{Median}-2\text{Mean}\) is a supplementary rule of thumb for some distributions, not an exact identity or a replacement for these formulas.

9. Finding a missing frequency

Example 6: A given mean

For the same intervals 0–10, 10–20, 20–30, 30–40 and 40–50, a new distribution has frequencies \(3,p,8,5,2\). Its grouped mean is 25. Find \(p\).

  1. The total frequency is \(N=18+p\).
  2. Using class marks \(5,15,25,35,45\), the weighted sum is \(15+15p+200+175+90=480+15p\).
  3. Substitute in the mean formula and solve:

\[25=\frac{480+15p}{18+p}\Rightarrow450+25p=480+15p\Rightarrow p=3.\]

Verification: \(N=21\), \(\sum f_ix_i=525\), and \(525/21=25\). The frequency is a non-negative integer.

10. Revision and examination tips

  • Label every calculation column: \(f_i,x_i,f_ix_i\), or \(d_i,f_id_i\), or \(u_i,f_iu_i\).
  • Use class marks for the mean and class boundaries for the median and mode.
  • For median, use the cumulative frequency before the median class. For mode, use ordinary neighbouring frequencies.
  • Keep negative deviations and brackets in the mode denominator.
  • Check \(\sum f_i=N\) and the last cumulative frequency \(=N\).
  • Retain fractions during calculation and round only the final result. Mean, median and mode carry the data's original units.
  • For less-than cumulative data, recover ordinary frequencies by subtracting consecutive cumulative totals before using the mean or mode formulas.

11. Self-practice with answers

Original questions. For Questions 1–3, use classes 0–10, 10–20, 20–30, 30–40 and 40–50 minutes with respective frequencies \(2,5,9,6,3\).

  1. Find the mean by the direct method, then check using \(a=25,\ h=10\).
  2. Find the median class and estimate the median.
  3. Identify the modal class and estimate the mode.
  4. Less-than cumulative frequencies at upper boundaries 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 are \(3,8,17,23,25\). Recover the five class frequencies.
  5. Values recorded in whole units are grouped as 10–19, 20–29 and 30–39. Give continuous class boundaries and the class mark of the second class.
  6. In Example 6, explain why \(18+p\), rather than 5, is the denominator in the mean formula.
Check your answers and working
  1. \(N=25,\ \sum f_ix_i=655\), so \(\bar x=26.2\) minutes. Step values are \(-2,-1,0,1,2\); \(\sum f_iu_i=-4-5+0+6+6=3\). Hence \(25+10(3/25)=26.2\).
  2. Cumulative totals \(2,7,16,22,25\) put \(N/2=12.5\) in 20–30. Median \(=20+[(12.5-7)/9]10\approx26.11\) minutes.
  3. 20–30 is modal. Mode \(=20+[(9-5)/(18-5-6)]10\approx25.71\) minutes.
  4. Frequencies: \(3,5,9,6,2\); their sum is 25.
  5. Boundaries: 9.5–19.5, 19.5–29.5, 29.5–39.5. Second class mark \(=(19.5+29.5)/2=24.5\).
  6. The denominator counts observations. Five is only the number of classes; \(18+p\) is the sum of their frequencies.
