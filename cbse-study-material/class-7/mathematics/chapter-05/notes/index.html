1. Points, lines and what a diagram represents

A point marks a location. A line is straight and extends endlessly both ways. A segment is the part between two endpoints. A ray starts at one endpoint and extends endlessly in one direction. In \(\angle ABC\), the middle letter B names the vertex. A plane is an ideal flat surface. Points on one line are collinear; three points that do not all lie on one line are non-collinear.

Tracks and notebook lines

Idealised straight rails and notebook rules model parallel lines on the same surface.

Road maps and bridges

A junction models a crossing. A brace can cross two parallel rails and let us transfer an angle.

Frames, poles and floors

A square window corner models a right angle. A vertical pole is perpendicular to a level ground plane; a vertical line and a ground line through its foot model the right angle in a planar view.

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish intersecting, parallel and perpendicular lines and interpret notation.
  • Identify and justify angle relationships, then calculate missing measures.
  • Construct parallel or perpendicular lines and test whether lines are parallel from given angles.

2. Intersections and perpendicular lines

Two distinct straight lines in a plane meet in at most one point. If they shared two distinct points they would be the same line. Two segments can cross in their interiors, meet at an endpoint, or meet where an endpoint lands on the midpoint of another segment.

At a crossing there are four angles. When one angle is 90°, every angle there is a right angle. The lines are perpendicular, written \(l\perp m\), and a small square marks the right angle. A 60° crossing shows why an intersection does not by itself establish perpendicularity.

3. Angle types, linear pairs and vertical angles

Adjacent angles share a vertex and one arm and have no overlapping interiors. A linear pair is an adjacent pair with outside arms that are opposite rays. Its sum is 180°. Separate angles may also be supplementary: two angles measuring 50° and 130° still sum to 180° even if their vertices differ. Complementary angles sum to 90°.

Why vertically opposite angles are equal

In the four-sector diagram, ∠1 + ∠2 = 180° and ∠2 + ∠3 = 180°, since each sum is a linear pair. Subtract ∠2 from both: ∠1 = ∠3. The same argument gives ∠2 = ∠4. Rotating a line changes the angle sizes while preserving both equalities.

4. Parallel lines, construction and paper folding

Two distinct parallel lines lie in the same plane and never meet, even when extended. Write \(l\parallel m\). Identical arrow marks identify a parallel pair. The perpendicular distance between the two lines is constant. Measure across at right angles, since a slanted path measures a different length. Lines drawn on different surfaces are not automatically parallel just because they do not meet.

When two lines are perpendicular to the same line in one plane, they have equal corresponding right angles. If distinct, they are parallel. Parallel tracks can appear to converge in perspective; decide from the geometric data rather than appearance.

  1. Parallel through a point: align an edge of a set square with the given line. Hold a ruler against another edge and slide the set square to the point without rotating. Draw along the original edge.
  2. Perpendicular through a point: align one right-angle edge of a set square with a line and slide the ruler or set square until the other right-angle edge passes through the point. Draw along the second edge.
  3. Dot paper: copy the same rightward and upward step between two pairs of dots for parallel directions. A horizontal grid line and a vertical grid line meet at right angles. Oblique segments can be perpendicular too; check with a set square.

Paper experiment: Fold a square sheet in half, then fold in the same direction through each layer again. Each complete halving doubles the bands. Counting the outer edges, the first three steps have 3, 5 and 9 parallel boundaries. Unfold and make a crosswise crease to see perpendicular lines. Compare mirrored corner folds and track which edges run in the same direction. To test parallelism, extend the creases with a ruler or check equal perpendicular gaps.

5. Transversals and the eight-angle diagram

A transversal intersects two or more lines at distinct points. It can cross parallel or nonparallel lines. The four sectors at each of two crossings give eight angles. In diagram P, the interior angles are 3, 4, 5 and 6; the exterior angles are 1, 2, 7 and 8. The same diagram labels apply throughout these notes.

Co-interior3 & 6, 4 & 5Sum 180° if l ∥ m. Example: 122° + 58°.

Linear pair1 & 2, 2 & 3, and other neighbouring sectors at one crossingSum 180° with or without parallel lines.

At each crossing, vertical angles match and linear pairs supplement regardless of parallelism. So each crossing supplies at most two distinct measures, and both together supply at most four. On parallel lines, all eight measures reduce to one value and its supplement, or just 90° when the transversal is perpendicular.

F, Z and C help you find the pair

A rotated or mirrored F points to corresponding positions, a Z to alternate interior positions and a C to co-interior positions. The shapes identify the angles. Before using a measure relationship, check whether parallelism was given or proved.

6. Properties, their converses and how to use them

The chapter investigates corresponding angles through measurements and angle transfer. If the lines are parallel, the matching positions give equal angles. For the converse, two crossings with equal corresponding angles must have the same direction relative to the same transversal, which makes the lines parallel. Our eyes can suggest a relationship, but a pair of measured or stated angles provides evidence.

Other rules follow from the same diagram. When \(l\parallel m\), ∠3 = ∠7 by correspondence and ∠7 = ∠5 by vertical opposition, so alternate interior ∠3 = ∠5. Reversing that chain proves the alternate-interior converse. Similarly, ∠2 = ∠6 = ∠8 establishes alternate-exterior equality and can be reversed. For co-interior angles, ∠3 and ∠2 make a linear pair; replacing ∠2 by corresponding ∠6 gives ∠3 + ∠6 = 180°. In reverse, if ∠3 + ∠6 = 180°, then ∠6 equals ∠2 and the lines are parallel.

Nonparallel angles still have corresponding, alternate and co-interior positions. Their measures do not automatically follow the parallel-line rules. One unequal corresponding pair is enough to rule out parallelism.

Write a reason on each line

At one crossing, use vertical equality or the straight-line sum. Across two crossings, establish parallelism before transferring measures. With a zigzag, a diagonal, or several transversals, extend a useful line or draw an auxiliary parallel. Apply each rule to one transversal at a time and add or subtract adjacent pieces at a common vertex.

7. Measuring, optical illusions and real drawings

Overlapping strokes can make parallel lines seem tilted. Railway photography also shows apparent convergence because of perspective. On a flat diagram, cover a distracting pattern, then check with a straightedge and a perpendicular measuring strip. Use matching arrow marks for parallel lines, a small square for 90°, and matching coloured arcs for the identified pair. A schematic marked as such conveys relationships; its drawn lengths are not data.

To measure with a protractor, centre it at the vertex, align the baseline with one arm, and read the other arm from the scale whose zero starts on the baseline ray. Before writing the answer, decide whether the visible angle is acute or obtuse. The correct reading should match that classification.

9. Step-by-step solved examples

30 original examples, with 9 basic, 12 intermediate, and 9 advanced questions. Read the figures as schematics and use the stated angle measures and markings.

Basic · Examples 1–9

Example 1 · Find an opposite angle

Given and required: Two lines cross and ∠2 is 48°. Find ∠4.

  1. Property: Vertical angles
  2. Work and reason: The two labelled sectors are opposite at the same vertex, so they have equal measures.

Final answer: 48°

Example 2 · Use a straight line

Given and required: ∠2 is 67°. Find its linear-pair neighbour ∠1.

  1. Property: Linear pair
  2. Work and reason: The outside arms are opposite rays. Subtract 67° from 180°.

Final answer: 113°

Example 3 · Four equal angles

Given and required: Two intersecting lines form four angles of equal measure. Find each and name the relationship.

  1. Property: Full turn
  2. Work and reason: The four angles total 360°, so each is 360° ÷ 4 = 90°. A right angle makes the lines perpendicular.

Final answer: 90° each; perpendicular

Example 4 · Classify a measured angle

Given and required: A protractor reads 34° from the correct zero. Classify ∠2.

  1. Property: Acute angle
  2. Work and reason: 34 is more than 0 and less than 90.

Final answer: Acute

Example 5 · Classify an obtuse angle

Given and required: ∠2 measures 120°. Classify it and find the reflex angle made by the same rays.

  1. Property: Complete turn
  2. Work and reason: 120° is between 90° and 180°. The longer turn is 360° − 120°.

Final answer: Obtuse; reflex measure 240°

Example 6 · Distinguish complements and supplements

Given and required: Find the complement and supplement of the marked 60° angle.

  1. Property: Angle sums
  2. Work and reason: A complement completes 90°, giving 90 − 60. A supplement completes 180°, giving 180 − 60.

Final answer: 30° and 120°, respectively

Example 7 · Identify a linear pair

Given and required: Which marked pair forms a linear pair, ∠1 with ∠4 or ∠1 with ∠3?

  1. Property: Adjacency and opposite rays
  2. Work and reason: 1 and 4 share a ray and their outer arms form a line. 1 and 3 are opposite and share no arm.

Final answer: ∠1 and ∠4

Example 8 · Transfer a corresponding angle

Given and required: For the marked parallel lines, ∠2 = 56°. Find ∠6.

  1. Property: Corresponding angles
  2. Work and reason: 2 and 6 are in matching positions at the two intersections.

Final answer: 56°

Example 9 · Find an alternate angle

Given and required: For the parallel lines, ∠4 = 64°. Find ∠6.

  1. Property: Alternate interior angles
  2. Work and reason: Both angles are between the lines, on opposite sides of the transversal.

Final answer: 64°

Intermediate · Examples 10–21

Example 10 · Alternate exterior angles

Given and required: Given ∠2 = 72° and parallel lines, find ∠8.

  1. Property: Alternate exterior angles
  2. Work and reason: The angles lie outside, on opposite sides. Also, ∠2 = ∠6 by correspondence and ∠6 = ∠8 by vertical opposition.

Final answer: 72°

Example 11 · Same-side interior angles

Given and required: The lines are parallel and ∠6 = 62°. Find ∠3.

  1. Property: Co-interior supplements
  2. Work and reason: ∠3 and ∠6 are interior on the same side. Compute 180 − 62.

Final answer: 118°

Example 12 · Use two properties

Given and required: Two parallel lines are crossed as shown. If ∠2 = 47°, find ∠7.

  1. Property: Corresponding angles, then linear pair
  2. Work and reason: ∠6 = 47° by correspondence. ∠7 is adjacent to ∠6 on a line, so 180 − 47 = 133.

Final answer: 133°

Example 13 · Combine correspondence and a linear pair

Given and required: With parallel lines, ∠3 = 104°. Find ∠8.

  1. Property: Correspondence and adjacency
  2. Work and reason: ∠7 equals ∠3, so ∠7 = 104°. ∠8 forms a linear pair with ∠7.

Final answer: 76°

Example 14 · Give a nonparallel counterexample

Given and required: In the diagram ∠2 = 61° and ∠6 = 46°. Could the two lines be parallel?

  1. Property: Corresponding angle test
  2. Work and reason: If parallel, the corresponding pair would have equal measures. 61 differs from 46.

Final answer: No

Example 15 · Prove parallel from corresponding angles

Given and required: ∠2 = ∠6 = 58°. No parallelism has been assumed. What can be proved?

  1. Property: Corresponding-angle converse
  2. Work and reason: The pair has equal measures in matching positions, so the two lines must be parallel.

Final answer: The two lines are parallel.

Example 16 · Prove parallel from alternate interior angles

Given and required: ∠4 = ∠6 = 53°. Can you prove the lines parallel?

  1. Property: Vertical angles and the corresponding converse
  2. Work and reason: ∠4 = ∠2 because they are vertically opposite. Therefore ∠2 = ∠6, an equal corresponding pair.

Final answer: The two lines are parallel.

Example 17 · Prove parallel from alternate exterior angles

Given and required: ∠2 = ∠8 = 68°. Prove the line relationship.

  1. Property: Corresponding-angle converse
  2. Work and reason: At the lower crossing ∠8 = ∠6 by vertical opposition. Thus ∠2 = ∠6, so the lines are parallel.

Final answer: Parallel

Example 18 · Prove parallel from supplementary co-interior angles

Given and required: ∠3 = 117° and ∠6 = 63°. They are on the same interior side. Prove parallelism.

  1. Property: Linear pair and the corresponding converse
  2. Work and reason: ∠2 = 180° − ∠3 = 63°. Hence ∠2 equals its corresponding angle ∠6.

Final answer: Parallel

Example 19 · An algebraic linear pair

Given and required: Let ∠1 = (2x + 10)° and ∠2 = (x + 20)°. Find x and both angles.

  1. Property: Linear-pair sum 180°
  2. Work and reason: 2x + 10 + x + 20 = 180 gives 3x = 150 and x = 50. Substitute into both expressions.

Final answer: x = 50; 110° and 70°

Example 20 · Algebra in vertically opposite sectors

Given and required: ∠2 = (3x + 5)° and ∠4 = (5x − 35)°. Find their measure.

  1. Property: Vertical equality
  2. Work and reason: 3x + 5 = 5x − 35, so 2x = 40 and x = 20. Substitute: 3 × 20 + 5 = 65.

Final answer: 65°

Example 21 · Use algebra across parallel lines

Given and required: ∠2 = (2x + 12)° and ∠6 = (3x − 12)°, with parallel lines. Find x.

  1. Property: Corresponding equality
  2. Work and reason: 2x + 12 = 3x − 12. Therefore x = 24, and each expression is 60°.

Final answer: x = 24

Advanced and application · Examples 22–30

Example 22 · Solve a co-interior problem

Given and required: ∠3 = (3x + 10)° and ∠6 = (2x − 5)°, with parallel lines. Find the angles.

  1. Property: Co-interior supplement
  2. Work and reason: Add: 5x + 5 = 180, hence x = 35. The angles are 115° and 65° and sum to 180°.

Final answer: 115° and 65°

Example 23 · Find all eight measures

Given and required: If ∠2 = 55° and the lines are parallel, label all eight angles.

  1. Property: Vertical, linear and corresponding
  2. Work and reason: 2 and 4 are 55°. 1 and 3 are 125°. Transfer all four positions to the lower crossing.

Final answer: 2, 4, 6, 8: 55°; 1, 3, 5, 7: 125°.

Example 24 · Count distinct angles without parallelism

Given and required: In the shown crossings ∠2 = 63° and ∠6 = 48°. How many distinct measures occur?

  1. Property: Vertical pairs and their supplements
  2. Work and reason: At the top the values are 63° and 117°. At the bottom they are 48° and 132°. All four are distinct.

Final answer: Four measures

Example 25 · Use a perpendicular as a test

Given and required: Two intersecting lines have ∠2 = ∠4 and also ∠2 = ∠1. Show they are perpendicular.

  1. Property: Linear pair and equality
  2. Work and reason: Because 1 and 2 form a linear pair, their sum is 180°. They are equal, so each is 90°. The opposite measures are also 90°.

Final answer: The lines are perpendicular.

Example 26 · A transversal perpendicular to one parallel

Given and required: A cross brace is perpendicular to the upper rail. Prove it is perpendicular to the lower rail.

  1. Property: Corresponding angles
  2. Work and reason: The upper right-angle sector is 90°. Parallelism transfers that measure to the corresponding lower sector.

Final answer: The brace is perpendicular to both rails.

Example 27 · Two different transversals

Given and required: Two parallel rails are cut by different braces. Brace t rises right making 49° with the right-going upper rail; brace u falls right making an acute 63° there. At the lower rail, compare their acute angles.

  1. Property: Correspondence applied to each brace
  2. Work and reason: Transfer each upper acute angle along its own brace. They remain 49° and 63°. The difference is 63 − 49.

Final answer: Brace u’s acute angle is 14° larger.

Example 28 · Angles between two bracing lines

Given and required: At a common point, one brace rises 51° and one descends 37° from the same horizontal ray (both rays point right). Find the angle between these rays.

  1. Property: Add adjacent angles
  2. Work and reason: The horizontal ray lies inside the angle, splitting it into 51° and 37° without overlap. Add the measures.

Final answer: 88°

Example 29 · Check an incomplete proof

Given and required: A student sees ∠3 = 137° and says its co-interior partner is also 137° on parallel lines. Correct the claim.

  1. Property: Co-interior supplement
  2. Work and reason: An equal pair would total 274°, not 180°. The corresponding interior partner is 180° − 137°.

Final answer: 43°

Example 30 · Design with a set square

Given and required: A ramp line and a reference rail form a 53° angle. Through a point off the rail, draw a line parallel to the rail and predict its corresponding angle on an intersecting ramp extension.

  1. Property: Parallel construction and angle transfer
  2. Work and reason: Keep the ruler fixed, slide a set square from the reference rail to the chosen point, and draw along the same edge. Corresponding 53° angles then agree. If the chosen point is not on the ramp, extend the ramp to meet the new rail.

Final answer: Parallel construction; corresponding angle 53°.

11. Quick revision and exam preparation

Identify → Mark

Find the vertices, the transversal and any parallel arrows or right-angle squares. Label each given angle.

At one crossing → Use local rules

Opposite sectors are equal; a linear pair sums to 180°. A right angle makes the lines perpendicular.

Across parallel lines → Transfer

F for corresponding, Z for alternate interior, C for co-interior. Congruent positions or 180° sums require the right conditions.

Prove → Give a reason

Show a matching pair is equal or an interior same-side pair is supplementary to prove parallelism. Check each algebraic result between 0° and 180°.

Last-minute checklist

  1. Have I separated a linear pair from vertical angles and complementary angles?
  2. Which rules apply without knowing whether the lines are parallel?
  3. Do the stated measures prove parallelism, or was parallelism already given?
  4. Did each calculation include a geometrical reason and a degree symbol?
  5. For an assertion–reason pair, check both statements before deciding whether one explains the other.

Revisit the angle chart, the numbered angle-pair chart, and the worked examples for a final recap.

Assertion–reason · Questions 56–60

Choose A if both are true and the reason explains the assertion, B if both are true without that explanation, C if only the assertion is true, or D if only the reason is true.

  1. Assertion: Vertically opposite angles are equal. Reason: Each is supplementary to the same neighbouring angle.
  2. Assertion: Two lines perpendicular to the same line in a plane are parallel if they are distinct. Reason: Vertically opposite angles are equal.
  3. Assertion: A linear pair totals 180°. Reason: Every pair of adjacent angles is a linear pair.
  4. Assertion: Corresponding angles are equal whenever any two coplanar lines are cut. Reason: Correspondence describes position, even with nonparallel lines.
  5. Assertion: For parallel lines, co-interior angles sum to 180°. Reason: One equals the corresponding neighbour of the other, with which it forms a linear pair.
