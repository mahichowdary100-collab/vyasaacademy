2.1 · A quick recap of integers

Integers include the positive whole-number values, their negatives and zero. A temperature of −4°C, a floor labelled −2 and an account balance of −80 rupees use negative numbers to tell us which side of a reference point we are on. Zero is neither positive nor negative. Here we count natural numbers from 1, and whole numbers from 0.

Learn to model signed moves, use positive and negative counters, explain multiplication and division signs, and evaluate expressions with brackets. Operations with Integers is Chapter 2 of Ganita Prakash Part II and Chapter 10 in the website’s continuous sequence.

Order, magnitude and opposites

Integers are equally spaced on the number line. Greater numbers are farther right. Therefore −3 > −7 even though 7 > 3. In ascending order we could write −7, −3, 0, 3. Descending order reverses the list. Magnitude is distance from zero. Both −3 and 3 have magnitude 3 and are opposites. Each is the additive inverse of the other because their sum is zero. Zero is its own additive inverse. The sign in −a denotes the opposite of a and can give a positive value when a is negative.

The sum-and-difference guessing game

Goal. Find two integers using their sum and the first minus the second.

Materials. Paper and pencil.

  1. Choose a pair whose sum is 6. Create a table with the two numbers, sum and difference.
  2. Keep the sum fixed by increasing the first number by one and decreasing the second by one.
  3. Stop when the difference is 14.
Reveal the observation and reasoning

The difference increases by two at each step. The pair 10 and −4 satisfies 10 + (−4) = 6 and 10 − (−4) = 14. Swapping the pair changes the difference to −14 without changing the sum.

Addition as signed movement

On a carrom track, choose right as positive and left as negative. Two successive displacements a and b give the final displacement a + b. Starting at −4 and adding 7 moves the coin right to 3. Starting at 4 and adding −7 moves left to −3.

Add same-sign magnitudes and keep their common sign. For opposite signs, pair off equal amounts. The result has the sign of the unmatched counters, or is zero if exactly balanced.

Subtraction undoes an addition

Think of a − b as the number that must be added to b to make a. Taking away a positive counter lowers the value. Taking away a negative counter raises it. Start with two positive counters to model 2 − (−3). Add three zero pairs, which leave the value at 2. Remove the three negative counters. Five positive counters remain.

Addition and subtraction are closed on integers. Addition allows switching and regrouping. Subtraction generally allows neither. For example, 3 − 7 = −4 while 7 − 3 = 4. Also, (9 − 4) − 2 = 3 while 9 − (4 − 2) = 7. Adding 0 leaves a number unchanged.

2.2 · Multiplication of integers

Adding and removing groups

For a positive group count, multiplication is repeated addition. Five groups of 4 give 20, and five groups of −4 give −20. Interpret a negative multiplier as removing groups from a neutral total. For (−3) × 2, add six zero pairs to an empty board, then remove three groups of two positive counters. Six negatives remain. For (−3) × (−2), remove the negative counters instead. Six positives remain.

Why two negative factors give a positive result

Keep the factor −4 fixed. As the first factor falls by 1, the product rises by 4. This trend must continue past zero because each new product differs by removing one group worth −4.

The distributive property confirms it. Since 4 + (−4) = 0, the two products (−3) × 4 and (−3) × (−4) must sum to zero. The first is −12, so the second is +12.

\((-3)\times [4+(-4)]=(-3)\times 4+(-3)\times (-4)=0\)

Nonzero factors with the same sign give a positive product. Different signs give a negative product. In a product of several nonzero factors, every pair of negative signs contributes a positive factor, so an even negative count gives a positive product and an odd count gives a negative product. One zero factor makes the whole product zero.

\((-2)\times (-3)\times 4=24\)

\((-2)\times (-3)\times (-4)=-24\)

\((-2)\times (-3)\times (-4)\times (-5)=120\)

Properties and mental multiplication

Integer products are integers. Multiplication is commutative and associative, so you can switch factors or regroup them. Multiplicative identity 1 leaves the number unchanged. Multiplication by −1 gives the additive inverse, and multiplication by 0 gives 0 for every integer.

Distribution splits a product into simpler products. For example, (−8) × 103 = (−8) × (100 + 3) = −800 − 24 = −824. Over a difference, distribute the multiplier to each term and keep the subtraction: (−6) × (20 − 1) = −120 − (−6) = −114.

Brahmagupta recorded rules for products and quotients of signed values in the Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta (628 CE), using wealth and debt to describe positive and negative values. For a modern example, a descent of 4 metres each minute for 5 minutes is a height change of 5 × (−4) = −20 metres.

Division of integers

To find a ÷ b, with b ≠ 0, find the number q such that b × q = a. For example, (−24) ÷ (−6) = 4 because (−6) × 4 = −24. If the dividend and divisor have the same nonzero sign, the quotient is positive. With different nonzero signs, the quotient is negative. Divide magnitudes, then put the appropriate sign on the result.

Dividing by 1 preserves the dividend. Dividing by −1 gives its opposite. Zero divided by any nonzero integer is zero. Dividing by zero is undefined. For 5 ÷ 0, no quotient multiplied by 0 gives 5. For 0 ÷ 0, every quotient would give 0 when multiplied by 0, so there is no unique result.

Integers are not closed under division. For example, 7 ÷ (−2) = −7/2, which is rational and lies between −4 and −3. Use the fraction instead of rounding it to an integer. See Working with Fractions for exact quotients. Division is neither commutative nor associative. For example, (24 ÷ 6) ÷ 2 = 2 but 24 ÷ (6 ÷ 2) = 8. Even a whole-number divisor need not make the signed result smaller: (−10) ÷ 2 = −5 and −5 > −10.

Expressions using integers

Use BODMAS to remember the order while keeping equal-priority operations together. Evaluate the innermost brackets first, then multiplication and division from left to right, then addition and subtraction from left to right. Use brackets around a signed number after an operation, as in 5 − (−3). A minus that belongs to a number or marks its opposite has a different job from a subtraction sign between terms. Review Arithmetic Expressions for the order of operations.

\((-9)+18\div (-3)\times 2\)

\(=(-9)+(-6)\times 2=(-9)+(-12)=-21\)

Multiplication distributes over sums and differences. When removing a bracket after a minus, think of multiplying all terms inside by −1. Associativity changes brackets only within an unbroken chain of the same associative operation. An expression such as 24 ÷ 6 × 2 must be evaluated left to right.

\(18-[7-(2-5)]=18-[7-(-3)]=18-10=8\)

Important rules at a glance

PropertyAdditionSubtractionMultiplicationDivision
Closure in integersYesYesYesNo
CommutativeYesNoYesNo
AssociativeYesNoYesNo
Two-sided identity0None1None

A two-sided identity leaves the number unchanged in either order. Subtracting 0 on the right preserves a number, but 0 − 6 is −6. Dividing by 1 on the right also preserves it, while 1 ÷ 6 = 1/6. To calculate mentally, group additive inverses, choose easy factors, split large amounts using distribution, and check division by multiplying back.

Add signed values

a+(−a)=0

For opposite signs cancel equal counts first.

7 + (−5) = 2

Watch for. The product sign rule does not apply to sums.

Subtract the opposite way

a−b=a+(−b)

Use for any integer b.

8 − (−3) = 11

Watch for. Change the subtracted value to its opposite.

Multiply and divide signs

(+)(−) gives −

For two nonzero values, agree gives positive and differ gives negative. Division requires a nonzero divisor.

(−24) ÷ (−6) = 4

Watch for. Decide the sign separately from magnitude.

Closure

a+b, a−b and a×b are integers

Integer addition, subtraction and multiplication stay in the set.

7 ÷ 2 = 7/2

Watch for. Do not round a non-integer quotient.

Identities and opposites

a+0=a; a×1=a; a×(−1)=−a

Use the identity for the operation you are performing.

(−7) × 1 = −7

Watch for. Zero is an additive identity, and a factor of zero makes a product zero.

Switch or regroup

a×b=b×a; (a×b)×c=a×(b×c)

The same two properties hold for addition.

4 × (−5) = (−5) × 4

Watch for. Subtraction and division do not generally allow either change.

Distribute multiplication

a×(b±c)=a×b±a×c

Multiply every term inside the bracket.

(−8) × 103 = −800 − 24

Watch for. Keep the subtraction sign before a negative product.

Count negative factors

Even → positive; odd → negative

Use only for a product with no zero factor.

(−2) × (−3) × 4 = 24

Watch for. Check for zero before counting signs.

Work in order

Brackets → × and ÷ → + and −

Work left to right within each equal-precedence pair.

24 ÷ 6 × 2 = 8

Watch for. Keep the parentheses when reporting negative values.

30 step-by-step worked examples

Basic

Example 1 · Compare signed integers

Given. −8 and −3

Find. Choose the greater integer.

Rule. Numbers increase to the right.

  1. Both are left of zero.
  2. −3 is 3 units left of zero, while −8 is 8 units left.

Answer. −3 > −8.

Example 2 · Combine negative moves

Given. (−7) + (−5)

Find. Find the sum.

Rule. Add same-sign magnitudes.

  1. Move left 7 units, then another 5 units left.
  2. The total distance to the left is 7 + 5 = 12.

Answer. −12.

Example 3 · Cancel zero pairs

Given. 7 + (−5)

Find. Find the sum.

Rule. One positive and one negative make zero.

  1. Pair five positives with five negatives.
  2. Remove the five zero pairs. Two positives remain.

Answer. 2.

Example 4 · Begin below zero

Given. (−4) + 7

Find. Find the new position.

Rule. Adding a positive number moves right.

  1. Start at −4 and move 4 steps to reach 0.
  2. Move the remaining 3 steps right.

Answer. 3.

Example 5 · Subtract a negative

Given. (−8) − (−3)

Find. Find the difference.

Rule. Subtract by adding the opposite.

  1. The opposite of −3 is 3.
  2. Start at −8 and move three steps right.

Answer. −5.

Example 6 · Take away from zero

Given. 0 − 7

Find. Find the difference.

Rule. Subtracting 7 is adding −7.

  1. Change subtraction of 7 to addition of −7.
  2. Adding a number to 0 leaves that number.

Answer. −7.

Example 7 · Multiply groups of negatives

Given. 5 × (−4)

Find. Find the product.

Rule. Positive group count gives repeated addition.

  1. Add −4 five times.
  2. The magnitude is 5 × 4 = 20, with a negative sign.

Answer. −20.

Example 8 · Use inverse multiplication

Given. (−24) ÷ (−6)

Find. Find the quotient.

Rule. The quotient is the missing factor.

  1. The magnitude of the missing factor is 24 ÷ 6 = 4.
  2. (−6) × 4 = −24, so the quotient is positive.

Answer. 4.

Example 9 · Check a zero quotient

Given. 0 ÷ (−8)

Find. Find the quotient.

Rule. A nonzero divisor times zero gives zero.

  1. The divisor −8 is nonzero.
  2. The only factor q with (−8) × q = 0 is 0.

Answer. 0.

Example 10 · Travel with several signed changes

Given. Start at −6, then move +9, −4 and +2.

Find. Find the endpoint.

Rule. Add the signed displacements in order.

  1. −6 + 9 = 3.
  2. 3 + (−4) = −1.
  3. −1 + 2 = 1.

Example 11 · Remove negative counters

Given. 2 − (−3)

Find. Find the difference.

Rule. Adding zero pairs keeps the starting value.

  1. Start with two positives. Add three positive–negative pairs.
  2. Remove the three negative counters.
  3. Five positive counters remain.

Answer. 5.

Example 12 · Check if subtraction allows regrouping

Given. (9 − 4) − 2 and 9 − (4 − 2)

Find. Compare the values.

Rule. Brackets decide which subtraction happens first.

  1. The first expression is 5 − 2 = 3.
  2. The second is 9 − 2 = 7.
  3. Different results disprove associativity.

Answer. 3 ≠ 7.

Example 13 · Explain a double negative using tokens

Given. (−3) × (−2)

Find. Find and explain the product.

Rule. A negative multiplier removes groups.

  1. Prepare six zero pairs.
  2. Remove two negatives three times.
  3. Six positives remain.

Answer. 6.

Example 14 · Predict the next product from a pattern

Given. 3 × (−4), 2 × (−4), 1 × (−4), 0 × (−4), (−1) × (−4)

Find. Find the last entry.

Rule. The product rises by 4 when the first factor drops by 1.

  1. The known products are −12, −8, −4 and 0.
  2. Add 4 to continue the pattern through zero.

Answer. 4.

Example 15 · Count negative factors

Given. (−2) × (−3) × (−4) × 5

Find. Find the sign and value.

Rule. Three negative nonzero factors give a negative product.

  1. There are three negative factors.
  2. Multiply their magnitudes with 5: 2 × 3 × 4 × 5 = 120.

Answer. −120.

Example 16 · Notice a zero factor early

Given. (−8) × 0 × (−11)

Find. Find the product.

Rule. Any product with a zero factor equals zero.

  1. Group 0 with either neighbour.
  2. The first product is 0 and multiplying it by the remaining factor still gives 0.

Answer. 0.

Example 17 · Distribute over a sum

Given. (−8) × 103

Find. Find the product mentally.

Rule. Multiplication distributes over a sum.

  1. Write 103 as 100 + 3.
  2. The two products are −800 and −24.
  3. Add the two negative results.

Answer. −824.

Example 18 · Distribute over a difference

Given. (−6) × 19

Find. Calculate using 20 − 1.

Rule. Distribute to both terms and subtract the second product.

  1. (−6) × 20 = −120 and (−6) × 1 = −6.
  2. −120 − (−6) = −120 + 6.

Answer. −114.

Example 19 · Keep an exact quotient

Given. 7 ÷ (−2)

Find. State the result and whether it is an integer.

Rule. Opposite signs give a negative quotient.

  1. The magnitude is seven halves.
  2. Multiply −7/2 by −2 to check that it gives 7.

Answer. −7/2, a rational number outside the integers.

Example 20 · Find a missing factor

Given. (−6) × n = 42

Find. Find n.

Rule. Undo multiplication by dividing by −6.

  1. The magnitude of n is 42 ÷ 6 = 7.
  2. n is negative because multiplying by −6 must give a positive product.

Answer. n = −7.

Example 21 · Check left-to-right order

Given. 36 ÷ (−6) × 2

Find. Evaluate.

Rule. Multiplication and division have equal precedence.

  1. Divide 36 by −6 first to get −6.
  2. Multiply −6 by 2.

Answer. −12.

Advanced and competency-based

Example 22 · Evaluate a grouped expression

Given. (−4) × [7 − (−2)] + 5

Find. Evaluate.

Rule. Brackets, multiplication, then addition.

  1. 7 − (−2) = 9.
  2. (−4) × 9 = −36.
  3. −36 + 5 = −31.

Answer. −31.

Example 23 · Estimate a cooling trend

Given. Start at 6°C and cool 4°C each hour for 4 hours.

Find. Find the final temperature and the first below-zero reading at a whole-hour check.

Rule. Multiply the signed change by time.

  1. The total change is 4 × (−4) = −16°C, giving 6 + (−16) = −10°C.
  2. After one hour it is 2°C. After two hours it is −2°C.
  3. Thus the first below-zero reading at a whole-hour check is after two hours.

Answer. −10°C after four hours; first below zero at the two-hour check.

Example 24 · Follow a lift itinerary

Given. Start at floor −3. Rise 7 floors, descend 5, then rise 2.

Find. Find the final floor and lowest floor reached.

Rule. Add signed moves to the current floor.

  1. −3 + 7 = 4.
  2. 4 − 5 = −1.
  3. −1 + 2 = 1. Among these floors the lowest is the start.

Answer. Floor 1; lowest floor −3.

Example 25 · Follow a running balance

Given. Start with 40 rupees. Withdraw 65, deposit 18, then withdraw 12.

Find. Find the final balance.

Rule. Credits add and debits subtract.

  1. 40 − 65 = −25.
  2. −25 + 18 = −7.
  3. −7 − 12 = −19.

Answer. −19 rupees.

Example 26 · Recover the count of wrong answers

Given. A score of 14 comes from five +4 answers and some −2 answers.

Find. Find the number of wrong answers.

Rule. Subtract the known contribution, then find the missing factor.

  1. Five correct answers contribute 20.
  2. The negative contribution is 14 − 20 = −6.
  3. Divide −6 by −2 to find the count.

Answer. 3 wrong answers.

Example 27 · Follow a signed number machine

Given. The machine uses a − b × c on (−5, 4, −3).

Find. Find its output.

Rule. Multiply before subtracting.

  1. 4 × (−3) = −12.
  2. −5 − (−12) = −5 + 12.

Answer. 7.

Example 28 · Infer both numbers from sum and difference

Given. x + y = 6 and x − y = 14.

Find. Find x and y.

Rule. Add the two statements to cancel y.

  1. The sum of the left sides is 2x and the right sides give 20.
  2. x = 10, so y = 6 − 10 = −4.
  3. Check that 10 − (−4) = 14.

Answer. 10 and −4.

Example 29 · Construct two coin combinations

Given. A positive coin is +7 and a negative coin is −4.

Find. Make −1 in two ways.

Rule. Adding a zero-value bundle preserves the total.

  1. One +7 coin and two −4 coins give 7 − 8 = −1.
  2. Four +7 coins and seven −4 coins make zero.
  3. Add that bundle to obtain five +7 coins and nine −4 coins.

Answer. Counts (1, 2) and (5, 9).

Example 30 · Diagnose both zero-divisor cases

Given. Compare 5 ÷ 0 and 0 ÷ 0.

Find. Explain why each is undefined.

Rule. Division asks for a unique missing factor.

  1. For 5 ÷ 0 no factor makes 0 × q = 5.
  2. For 0 ÷ 0 every factor makes 0 × q = 0.
  3. Neither calculation gives a unique quotient.

Answer. Both are undefined.

Common mistakes and how to fix them

Order negative numbers by magnitude

Incorrect approach. Write −9 > −3.

Correction. Compare the positions. −9 is left of −3, so −9 < −3.

Mix up the sign of a number and subtraction

Incorrect approach. Read both minus signs in 8 − (−3) as the same operation.

Correction. The first minus asks you to subtract. The bracketed −3 is the signed number being removed.

Add opposite signs without cancelling

Incorrect approach. Write 7 + (−5) = 12.

Correction. Cancel five positive–negative pairs. Two positives remain.

Give two negatives a positive sum

Incorrect approach. Write (−7) + (−5) = 12.

Correction. Moving left twice combines the distances. The sum is −12.

Remove a negative by moving left

Incorrect approach. Write 2 − (−3) = −1.

Correction. Add three zero pairs and remove three negatives. Five positives remain.

Use product rules for sums

Incorrect approach. Write (−3) + (−2) = 5.

Correction. These are two negative displacements. Add them to get −5.

Keep a negative sign when multiplying two negatives

Incorrect approach. Write (−3) × (−2) = −6.

Correction. Removing the six negatives from zero pairs leaves six positives.

Assume a product must be larger

Incorrect approach. Expect 8 × (−2) to exceed 8.

Correction. The product is −16, which lies left of 8. Decide the sign before comparing.

Assume a quotient must be smaller

Incorrect approach. Expect (−10) ÷ 2 to lie left of −10.

Correction. The answer is −5, which is greater than −10.

Ignore a zero factor

Incorrect approach. Treat (−8) × 0 × (−11) as 88.

Correction. Start with the zero factor. Zero times any other factor gives zero.

Divide by zero

Incorrect approach. Treat 5 ÷ 0 as zero.

Correction. No factor multiplied by zero gives 5, so the expression is undefined.

Swap zero with the nonzero divisor

Incorrect approach. Treat 0 ÷ 5 and 5 ÷ 0 alike.

Correction. 0 ÷ 5 = 0 since 5 × 0 = 0. In 5 ÷ 0 the divisor is zero.

Assume every quotient is an integer

Incorrect approach. Round 7 ÷ 2 to 4.

Correction. The exact quotient is 7/2. Keep the fraction unless an estimate is asked for.

Swap subtraction terms

Incorrect approach. Treat 4 − 7 and 7 − 4 as equal.

Correction. Calculate each in its stated order. The results are −3 and 3.

Regroup division freely

Incorrect approach. Treat (24 ÷ 6) ÷ 2 and 24 ÷ (6 ÷ 2) as equal.

Correction. Respect brackets. The values are 2 and 8.

Distribute to one term only

Incorrect approach. Write −8 × (100 + 3) as −800 + 3.

Correction. Multiply both terms, giving −800 − 24 = −824.

Drop a minus before brackets

Incorrect approach. Write 10 − (3 − 7) as 10 − 3 − 7.

Correction. Evaluate the bracket (−4) first or distribute −1 to both terms. The total is 14.

Count every factor for sign parity

Incorrect approach. Call (−2) × 3 × (−4) negative because there are three factors.

Correction. Only the two negatives count, so the product is +24.

Put multiplication first in every expression

Incorrect approach. Read 24 ÷ 6 × 3 as 24 ÷ 18.

Correction. Process × and ÷ left to right. The value is 4 × 3 = 12.

Use the wrong reference for a word problem

Incorrect approach. Treat a 7 m rise from −12 m as a change to +7 m.

Correction. Add the rise to the starting height. The new height is −5 m.

Exam preparation and quick revision

Show the signs and the order

Write the starting value and the signed change before calculating. Use parentheses around negative factors and divisors. For division give an exact quotient and check by multiplication. Give the answer with units where appropriate.

CompareFarther right is greater. Magnitude measures distance from zero.

Multiply or divideFor nonzero values, same signs give positive and different signs give negative.
Several factorsCheck for zero first. Then count negative factors.

OrderWork inside brackets first, then × and ÷ left to right, then + and − left to right.
