Before you start

A sack and two unit weights balance seven units. How heavy is the sack? Can you build a matchstick pattern using exactly 21 sticks? If two friends save at different rates, when will their savings match? Equations help describe what you know and find what you don't.

What you’ll learn

  • Translate a situation into an equation.
  • Preserve equality while isolating an unknown.
  • Substitute to verify a solution.
  • Create equivalent equations and explain number tricks.

Plan your study

Allow 100–120 minutes for the core notes and demonstrations, then practise in short sessions. Review Arithmetic Expressions, Expressions Using Letter-Numbers and Operations with Integers when needed.

7.1 · Find the Unknowns

Unknown weights and equality

A level scale with identical arms shows equality of total weights. A lower pan indicates a heavier side. If two identical sacks and three units balance thirteen units, let each sack weigh x units. The equation is 2x + 3 = 13, and x = 5 makes both totals equal. Treat identical unknown objects as equal only when the question or diagram says they are.

Expressions and equations

A variable or letter-number, such as x, stands for an unknown quantity. A coefficient is the multiplier of a variable, as 3 is in 3x. A constant is a number without the variable. In 3x + 5, the terms are 3x and 5. An expression represents a value. An equation states that two expressions have equal values, with a left-hand side (LHS) and right-hand side (RHS).

ExpressionEquation
3x + 53x + 5 = 20
2a − 72a − 7 = 9
5m5m = 30

Matchstick patterns

In the textbook sequence, positions 1, 2, 3 and 4 use 3, 5, 7 and 9 sticks. Each added triangle shares an edge and contributes two sticks, so the rule is 2n + 1. To test 21 sticks, solve 2n + 1 = 21 to get n = 10. The position must be a positive whole number.

A different pattern joins squares in a row. The first square needs four sticks, and every added square shares one side and adds three. The rule is 4 + 3(n − 1) = 3n + 1.

7.2 · Solving Equations Systematically

Solutions and trial

A solution is a value that makes LHS and RHS equal. Substitute 3, 4 or 5 into 2x + 3 = 13. The LHS values are 9, 11 and 13, so x = 5. Here the positive coefficient means the LHS increases as x increases. A systematic method avoids guessing repeatedly.

xLHSRHSResult
3913Too small
41113Too small
51313Equal

The balance principle and inverse operations

Add or subtract the same quantity on both sides. Multiply or divide both sides by the same nonzero number. The operation must be reversible to keep exactly the same solutions. Multiplying by zero throws away information, and division by zero is undefined.

GivenOperation on both sidesResult
x + 8 = 15Subtract 8x = 7
x − 6 = 9Add 6x = 15
4x = 28Divide by 4x = 7
x/3 = 5Multiply by 3x = 15

Two steps and signed numbers

For 3x + 6 = 21, subtract 6 to get 3x = 15, then divide by 3 to get x = 5. Check 3 × 5 + 6 = 21. For −4x + 7 = 19, subtract 7 to get −4x = 12, then divide by −4 to get x = −3. Signed tiles model positive and negative terms. A physical scale represents nonnegative masses only.

Unknowns on both sides and short transposition

Solve 5x + 2 = 2x + 14 by subtracting 2x from both sides, then subtracting 2 from both sides. This gives 3x = 12, so x = 4. A short line such as 5x − 2x = 14 − 2 records these same inverse operations. A factor multiplying the whole variable term is undone by division.

Brackets, fractions and decimals

Distribute across every term: −2(x − 5) = −2x + 10. Then collect like terms. For \(\frac{x}{3}+\frac{1}{2}=\frac{5}{2}\), multiply every term by 6 to get 2x + 3 = 15, hence x = 6. For 0.4x + 1.2 = 3.2, multiply every term by 10 to get 4x + 12 = 32, so x = 5.

When the variable terms cancel

If simplifying gives 0 = 0, every allowed value works, as with 2(x + 3) = 2x + 6. This is an identity. If it gives 0 = 1, no value works, as with 2x + 6 = 2x + 7. Check the original problem’s domain even when the algebra permits every value or gives a unique answer.

7.2 · Framing Equations and Solving Problems

Define the unknown with its unit and any restrictions. Translate both sides of the relationship. Solve and substitute into the original equation, then answer in words. In a word problem, ‘5 more than twice a number’ means 2x + 5, whereas ‘twice the sum of a number and 5’ means 2(x + 5).

StatementEquation
A number increased by 5 is 12x + 5 = 12
A number decreased by 7 is 10x − 7 = 10
Three times a number is 243x = 24
Half a number is 15x/2 = 15
Five more than twice a number is 192x + 5 = 19
Subtract 3 from four times a number to get 214x − 3 = 21

Numbers, ages and linked unknowns

Two friends have 54 marbles. The second has six more than the first. Write x + (x + 6) = 54, so 2x = 48, x = 24 and the second has 30. Express the second unknown in terms of the first. For ages, add the same elapsed years to both ages. If Mira is x and her sister is x + 4, their combined age in three years is (x + 3) + (x + 7).

Shopping, savings and travelling

Three identical books plus a fixed ₹30 charge cost ₹210. Use 3x + 30 = 210 for price x. Two savings plans, starting at ₹180 and ₹300 and adding ₹40 and ₹20 per month, meet when 180 + 40m = 300 + 20m. The answer is 6 months. A fixed fare plus a cost per kilometre gives the same equation structure.

Geometry and growing patterns

A rectangle has width x cm, length x + 3 cm and perimeter 30 cm. Write 2x + 2(x + 3) = 30, so x = 6. In a triangle with angles x, x + 10° and 2x − 10°, the angle sum gives 4x = 180 and x = 45. Check that all angles are positive. In a repeated grill or tiled pattern, count all repeated gaps and fixed lengths separately.

A target in a growing pattern

Materials. Use tiles or draw connected pieces.

  1. Set out one fixed tile and three arms of n tiles.
  2. Write the count in more than one way, such as 3n + 1 or n + n + (n + 1).
  3. Test targets 34 and 35.
Reveal the observation and reasoning

For 34, 3n + 1 = 34 gives 11. For 35 it gives 34/3, which is not a whole-number step.

7.2 · Generating Equations and Equivalent Chains

Start with a chosen answer, such as x = 8. Add 3 to both sides to get x + 3 = 11. Multiply both sides by 2 to get 2(x + 3) = 22. Add x to both sides, expand and combine to get 3x + 6 = x + 22. The last equation still has solution 8. Reverse the operations to solve it.

Two equations are equivalent when they have the same solution set. Adding the same variable term to both sides also preserves equality and can be reversed. Multiplying both sides by a variable expression is outside this chapter’s safe rule because it may be zero for some values.

Make a chain from a solution

Materials. Paper and pencil.

  1. Pick x = −2.
  2. Add 6 to both sides, then multiply both sides by 3.
  3. Try reversing those operations and verify by substitution.
Reveal the observation and reasoning

One chain is x = −2, then x + 6 = 4, then 3(x + 6) = 12. Dividing by 3 and subtracting 6 restores the starting equation.

7.3 · Mind the Mistake, Mend the Mistake

Look for the first line that stops being equivalent. Repair that step, solve and check in the original equation.

Undo the constant
Incorrect. 4x + 6 = 22 becomes 4x = 22 + 6.
Correction. Subtract 6 from both sides, then divide 16 by 4. Check x = 4.
Divide every term
Incorrect. 6x + 9 = 33 becomes x + 9 = 11.
Correction. Divide both terms on the left and the right by 3 to get 2x + 3 = 11. Then x = 4.
Keep the negative sign
Incorrect. −5x = 20 gives x = 4.
Correction. Use the signed divisor −5. Then x = −4.
Distribute to each term
Incorrect. 3(x + 2) = 21 becomes 3x + 2 = 21.
Correction. The constant is also multiplied: 3x + 6 = 21, x = 5.
Combine only like terms
Incorrect. 2x + 3 becomes 5x.
Correction. The constant and variable term are unlike. Keep them separate.
Move the variable term correctly
Incorrect. 5x + 2 = 2x + 14 becomes 7x = 12.
Correction. Subtract 2x from both sides, then subtract 2. Thus 3x = 12 and x = 4.
Clear both denominators
Incorrect. x/3 + 1/2 = 5 becomes 2x + 1/2 = 30.
Correction. Multiply every term by 6. Then 2x + 3 = 30 and x = 27/2.
Classify cancellation
Incorrect. 3x + 1 = 3x + 2 proves x = 0.
Correction. Subtract 3x to obtain 1 = 2, so there is no solution.
Keep equality reversible
Incorrect. Multiply x = 9 by 0 and conclude any x is allowed in the original.
Correction. Zero multiplication loses the original restriction. Use only nonzero factors.
Check the context
Incorrect. Report 13/4 squares as a pattern position.
Correction. Check that a number of full squares is a whole number and state when the target cannot be reached.

7.4 · A Pinch of History

The chapter introduces bījagaṇita, an Indian term for algebra. In the Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta (628 CE), Brahmagupta used symbols for unknowns and gave methods for working with them. The chapter also discusses commercial problems and later transmission of algebraic ideas, including al-Khwārizmī’s work on restoration and balancing.

Brahmagupta’s rule

Write the simplified equation as Ax + B = Cx + D. Subtract Cx and B from both sides to get (A − C)x = D − B. If A ≠ C, divide to get \(x=\frac{D-B}{A-C}\). If A = C, compare B and D: equal constants give an identity, and different constants give no solution. For 7x − 4 = 3x + 16 the two differences are 20 and 4, so x = 5.

A mathematical magic trick

Choose any number x. Triple it, add 12, divide by 3 and subtract the original x. The running expression simplifies from \(\frac{3x+12}{3}\) to x + 4. Removing x leaves 4. The final result is fixed because the variable cancels.

Design another trick

Materials. Choose a nonzero number k and a target t.

  1. Multiply the starting value by k.
  2. Add k × t, then divide by k.
  3. Subtract the starting value.
Reveal the observation and reasoning

The result is always t. The operation before subtracting the original value simplifies to x + t.

Write a complete answer

  1. Define the variable and the unit or allowed values.
  2. Write the equation from the relationship in the problem.
  3. Simplify, then show each operation on both sides.
  4. Check by substitution into the original equation.
  5. State the answer in context and keep exact fractions where appropriate.

Exam tip. Put a line break between long algebra steps. A wrong sign can be located by checking the first equality that no longer holds.

Equation. x + 2 = 7

  1. Collect variable terms and constants separately on each side. x + 2 = 7
  2. Subtract 2 from both sides. x = 5
  3. Substitute x = 5 into both original sides to verify. LHS = RHS = 7

  1. Solve and check: 4x − 7 = 17. Intermediate

    Show hint

    Reveal solution

    Show hint

    Subtract y from both sides.

    Reveal solution

  3. Solve and check: 2(x − 4) = 18. Intermediate

    Show hint

    Expand or divide by 2 first.

    Reveal solution

  4. Solve and check: −3(t + 2) = 12. Intermediate

    Show hint

    Keep the negative factor on both terms.

    Reveal solution

    Expand brackets using distribution, then collect like terms on each side. −3t − 6 = 12. Add 6 to both sides. −3t = 18. Divide every term on both sides by −3. t = −6. Substitute t = −6 into both original sides to verify. LHS = RHS = 12.

  5. Solve and check: x/2 + 1/3 = 7/3. Intermediate

    Show hint

    Show hint

    The first change is wrong. Divide both sides by 5 to get x = 6. Verify 5 × 6 = 30.

Long answer · Questions 46–50

  1. A parcel service charges 25 rupees plus 15 rupees per km. The bill is 145 rupees. Find the distance. Intermediate

    Show hint

    Separate fixed and per-kilometre costs.

    Reveal solution

    Let x be the distance. 15x + 25 = 145, so 15x = 120 and x = 8 km. Check 25 + 15 × 8 = 145.

  2. Two numbers total 71. The larger is 13 more than the smaller. Find both. Intermediate

    Show hint

    Use x and x + 13.

    Reveal solution

    2x + 13 = 71. Subtract 13 and divide by two to get 29. The other is 42. Check 29 + 42 = 71.

  3. Can 50 sticks make some number of joined squares in a row? Intermediate

    Show hint

    Solve 3n + 1 = 50 and check the domain.

    Reveal solution

    3n = 49, so n = 49/3. The value is not a whole number, so this exact target is impossible.

  4. Solve and check: 4(2x − 3) + 5 = 3x + 18. Intermediate

    Show hint

    Expand, collect, then keep x terms together.

    Reveal solution

    Expand brackets using distribution, then collect like terms on each side. 8x − 7 = 3x + 18. Subtract 3x from both sides. 5x − 7 = 18. Add 7 to both sides. 5x = 25. Divide every term on both sides by 5. x = 5. Substitute x = 5 into both original sides to verify. LHS = RHS = 33.

  5. Explain why adding 3x and then dividing by 2 on both sides of x = 6 gives an equivalent equation. Intermediate

    Show hint

    Write both intermediate equations.

    Reveal solution

    Adding 3x gives 4x = 6 + 3x. Dividing gives 2x = 3 + (3x)/2. Multiplying by 2 and subtracting 3x restores x = 6.

HOTS · Questions 63–67

  1. Create a bracket equation whose only solution is −4 and an identity that includes −4 as a solution. Advanced

    Show hint

    Start with x = −4 or two equivalent expressions.

    Reveal solution

    For example, 3(x + 1) = −9 reduces to x = −4. The identity 2(x − 4) = 2x − 8 works for every value.

    Show hint

  3. Design a trick that always returns −3. Advanced

    Show hint

  4. Find an integer k for which 3x + k = x + 11 has solution x = 5. Advanced

    Show hint

    Reveal solution

  5. Why is squaring both sides of x = 3 unsafe as a solution-preserving step? Advanced

    Show hint

    Check x = −3 after the change.

    Reveal solution

    Squaring gives x² = 9, which also permits −3. The original permits only 3, so the new equation has an extra solution.

Quick revision and flashcards

Download the revision map

What is a solution?

A value that makes the original left and right expressions equal.

How do you undo a constant?

Add its opposite to both sides.

How do you undo multiplication?

Divide every term on both sides by the same nonzero value.

Why check for zero?

Multiplying by zero can lose information and dividing by zero is undefined.

What does a cancelled variable tell you?

A true numerical equality gives all allowed values; a false one gives none.

How does a number trick fix the result?

Its operations make the starting variable cancel.
