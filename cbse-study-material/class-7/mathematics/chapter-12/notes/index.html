Before you start

4.1 · A Quick Recap of Decimals

In 27.538, 27 is the whole-number part and 0.538 is the fractional part. The digits 5, 3 and 8 represent 5 tenths, 3 hundredths and 8 thousandths.

\(\frac{5}{10}=\frac{50}{100}=\frac{500}{1000}\), so 0.5 = 0.50 = 0.500. Compare 0.58 with 0.60. The tenths decide, so 0.60 is larger. Each tick in the number line below adds 0.01.

Use an equivalent denominator of 10, 100 or 1,000 when possible. For example, \(\frac34=\frac{75}{100}=0.75\). Use division in other cases. In a market, 250 g equals 0.250 kg because 1 kg contains 1,000 g.

Addition and subtraction refresher

Align the points so each column contains the same unit. Add 12.75 and 3.80 to get 16.55. To subtract 8.76 from 25.40, regroup a tenth into ten hundredths and a whole into ten tenths. The result is 16.64.

Exam tip. Trailing zeros keep a value unchanged. An internal zero holds a place, as in 0.05.

4.2 · Decimal Multiplication

A decimal times a counting number

2.4 × 3 means three groups of 2.4. Each has 24 tenths. Three groups have 72 tenths, or 7.2. Likewise, 0.25 × 8 gives 200 hundredths, or 2.

A decimal times a decimal

Think of 0.4 × 0.3 as four tenths of three tenths. A unit square split into ten columns and ten rows has 100 cells. Shade four columns and three rows. The 12 overlapping cells cover 0.12 square units.

With fractions, \(\frac4{10}\times\frac3{10}=\frac{12}{100}\). A tenth times a hundredth gives a thousandth. Two hundredth units multiplied give a ten-thousandth. For 1.2 × 0.5, the rectangle includes 0.5 from the first square and 0.1 from the extra 0.2 unit.

The written method

  1. Multiply the whole-digit versions. For 2.5 × 1.4, write 25 × 14 and find the partial products 100 and 250.
  2. Count both original decimal places. The total is two, so 350 represents 350 hundredths.
  3. Write 3.50, then simplify to 3.5. Check that the result lies between 2.5 and 5.

Powers of ten and product size

Multiplying by 10, 100 or 1,000 makes each digit worth 10, 100 or 1,000 times as much. For 3.456 the results are 34.56, 345.6 and 3456. For a positive starting amount, multiplying by more than 1 increases it, multiplying by 1 preserves it, and multiplying by a factor from 0 up to but excluding 1 decreases it.

Positive factorsProduct compared with both factors
Both above 1Larger than both. 2.4 × 1.5 = 3.6.
Both below 1Smaller than both. 0.6 × 0.5 = 0.3.
One on each side of 1Between the factors. 0.6 × 4 = 2.4.
One equals 1Equal to the other factor. 1 × 2.4 = 2.4.

Commutative and associative properties still apply. For example, 0.4 × 5 = 5 × 0.4, and (0.5 × 2) × 0.3 = 0.5 × (2 × 0.3). Distribute for mental maths. 4.8 × (10 + 2) = 48 + 9.6 = 57.6.

Reveal the observation

4.5 × 2.1 = 9.45. Compare whole parts first, then calculate promising candidates.

Follow-up. How can you produce a product above 12? Try 4.1 × 5.2.

Exam tip. Add leading zeros if the digit product is too short. For example, 0.08 × 0.02 = 0.0016.

4.3 · Decimal Division

Sharing and grouping

Sharing asks for the size of each part. Sharing 8.4 L among four people gives 84 tenths ÷ 4 = 21 tenths = 2.1 L each. Grouping asks how many parts of a given size fit. Three metres contains six lengths of 0.5 m.

Regroup across the decimal point

For 7.5 ÷ 6, give each group one whole, leaving one whole. Combine it with the five tenths to get 15 tenths. Give each group two tenths, leaving three tenths. Regroup to 30 hundredths and give each group five hundredths. Check: 1.25 × 6 = 7.50.

Write the quotient point above the dividend point when the divisor is a whole number. Append zeros to continue regrouping. A zero can hold a place in the quotient, as in 8.08 ÷ 4 = 2.02.

Division by a decimal

Change both quantities to the same smaller unit. For 4.8 ÷ 0.6, use tenths: 48 ÷ 6 = 8. For 0.84 ÷ 0.07, use hundredths: 84 ÷ 7 = 12. Scaling dividend and divisor by the same nonzero factor preserves the quotient because the units cancel.

Dividing 345.6 by 10, 100 or 1,000 gives 34.56, 3.456 and 0.3456. Dividing by 0.1, 0.01 or 0.001 is equivalent to multiplying by 10, 100 or 1,000 because smaller units give more groups.

Terminating and recurring quotients

A zero remainder ends long division. When a nonzero remainder returns, the same digits repeat. For 5 ÷ 6, the tenths digit is 8 and the remainder is 2. At the hundredths place, 20 ÷ 6 gives digit 3 and remainder 2 again. Write \(0.8\overline{3}\) exactly or 0.83 to the nearest hundredth. A reduced fraction terminates in base ten if the denominator has only 2 and/or 5 as prime factors, or is 1. Then the denominator divides a power of ten.

To divide fractions, multiply by the reciprocal. For example, \(\frac34\div\frac18\) gives 6, as does 0.75 ÷ 0.125. For a positive dividend, dividing by more than 1 makes it smaller, by 1 preserves it, and by a positive number under 1 makes it larger. A zero divisor is undefined.

Investigate reciprocal patterns

Try it. Write 1/2, 1/4, 1/8 and then 1/5, 1/25, 1/125 as decimals.

Reveal the observation

Each step divides by 2 or 5. Multiplying matching terms gives 1/10, 1/100 and 1/1000 because 2 × 5 = 10.

Follow-up. Predict the next pair: 1/16 = 0.0625 and 1/625 = 0.0016.

Cyclic numbers

Try it. Use long division to find the repeating block of 1 ÷ 7. Multiply that block, as a whole number, by 2 and 3.

Reveal the observation

1 ÷ 7 = 0.(142857). The products 285714 and 428571 are rotations of 142857. Multiplying by 7 gives 999999. Try 1 ÷ 17 for a longer block.

Follow-up. Why must a remainder repeat when long division never reaches zero? With a divisor of 7 there are only six possible nonzero remainders.

4.4 · Look Before You Leap!

Use 365.2422 days as an approximate seasonal year. A calendar of only 365-day years falls behind this model by about 0.2422 days per year. After 100 years the gap is about 24.22 days.

  1. Add one day in every fourth year. In years 1–100 this adds 25 days, giving 36,525 days. The solar model needs about 36,524.22 days, so the calendar is about 0.78 day too long.
  2. Skip century years. That gives 24 additions in the first 100 years. In years 1–1000, the calendar then has 365,240 days and falls about 2.2 days short.
  3. Restore the extra day in years divisible by 400. In years 1–1000, add 250 − 10 + 2 = 242 leap days. The calendar has 365,242 days, about 0.2 day short.

February has 29 days in a leap year. Years 1900 and 2100 are ordinary years, while 2000 and 2400 are leap years. The Gregorian average over 400 years is 365 + 97 ÷ 400 = 365.2425 days.

Calendar investigation

Try it. Compare the three rules over years 1–400. Predict which count comes closest to 400 × 365.2422.

Reveal the observation

The first rule adds 100 days. The second adds 96. The Gregorian rule adds 97. The solar model needs about 96.88 extra days, so 97 is closest.

Follow-up. Use the leap-year lab to test 2096, 2100 and 2400.

Extend the calendar model

Try it. Use 25 cycles of 400 years to estimate the drift over 10,000 years. Compare with 10,000 × 365.2422.

Reveal the observation

The Gregorian calendar has 25 × 146,097 = 3,652,425 days. The seasonal-year model gives about 3,652,422 days, a difference of about three days. The model treats the seasonal year as fixed, so this is a classroom estimate.

Follow-up. Could skipping an additional leap day reduce the gap in this model? For a history project, compare how traditional Indian calendars use observations of the Sun or Moon to keep months and seasons in step.

Formula and method cards

Equivalent decimals

Append zeros after the last decimal digit.

0.6 = 0.60

Keep internal zeros.

Addition and subtraction

Align equal units and regroup as needed.

12.75 + 3.80 = 16.55

Line up points before writing the last digits.

Decimal product

Add both place counts in the untrimmed product.

0.4 × 0.3 = 0.12

Tenths times tenths gives hundredths.

Multiplying by powers of ten

Each digit moves to a place with higher value.

3.456 × 100 = 345.6

Two powers of ten shift each digit two columns.

Dividing by powers of ten

Each digit moves to a place with lower value.

345.6 ÷ 1,000 = 0.3456

Use a zero for an empty ones place.

Long division

Regroup into the next smaller unit.

7.50 ÷ 6 = 1.25

Keep quotient and dividend points aligned after scaling.

Equivalent division

Scale both numbers by the same nonzero factor.

0.84 ÷ 0.07 = 84 ÷ 7

Express both in hundredths. A zero divisor is undefined.

Factors and divisors near one

For positive inputs, a number between 0 and 1 decreases a product and increases a quotient.

8 × 0.5 = 4 and 8 ÷ 0.5 = 16

Think half of 8 versus how many halves fit in 8.

Fractions and decimals

Write place-value units over a power of ten, or divide top by bottom.

0.375 = 375/1000 = 3/8

Reduce before deciding whether the decimal terminates.

Estimate and round

Use convenient nearby numbers or round once at the end.

19.8 × 0.49 ≈ 20 × 0.5

State the accuracy of an approximation.

Operation order

Evaluate brackets, then × and ÷, then + and −.

12 ÷ 0.5 × 2 = 48

Read operations of equal priority left to right.

Common mistakes and how to fix them

Line up addition
Incorrect: 3.8 written under the last digits of 12.75.
Correction: Write 3.80 and align the points, so the sum is 16.55.
Borrow across places
Incorrect: Treat 25.4 − 8.76 as if 4 were a hundredths digit.
Correction: Write 25.40, then regroup into hundredths and tenths to get 16.64.
Use multiplication
Incorrect: 0.4 × 0.3 = 0.7.
Correction: Four tenths of three tenths gives 12 hundredths.
Count places
Incorrect: 0.8 × 0.2 = 1.6.
Correction: 8 × 2 = 16 and a tenth times a tenth is a hundredth. Write 0.16.
Add leading zeros
Incorrect: 0.08 × 0.02 = 0.016.
Correction: Use four places to write 0.0016.
Predict a product
Incorrect: Multiplication always makes the original number larger.
Correction: For a positive number, multiplying by 0.5 halves it.
Predict a quotient
Incorrect: 5 ÷ 0.5 = 2.5.
Correction: There are two halves in each whole, so there are 10 halves in 5.
Scale both terms
Incorrect: 3.75 ÷ 1.5 becomes 3.75 ÷ 15 or 37.5 ÷ 1.5.
Correction: Multiply both by 10 to get 37.5 ÷ 15 = 2.5.
Keep an interior zero
Incorrect: 8.08 ÷ 4 = 2.2.
Correction: The 8 hundredths become 2 hundredths. The zero tenths stay, giving 2.02.
Check the digit's unit
Incorrect: 0.5 = 0.05.
Correction: These mean five tenths and five hundredths. 0.5 is ten times 0.05.
Use a power of ten
Incorrect: 3/8 = 0.38.
Correction: Rewrite as 375/1000, giving 0.375.
Round at the end
Incorrect: Round 0.375 to 0.4, then multiply by 8 for an exact answer of 3.2.
Correction: Multiply the original amount by 8 to get exactly 3.
Mark approximations
Incorrect: 1 ÷ 3 = 0.33.
Correction: Write 1 ÷ 3 ≈ 0.33, or use the repeating form for equality.
Check the divisor
Incorrect: 7 ÷ 0 = 0.
Correction: No number times 0 gives 7. Division by zero is undefined.
Keep the unit
Incorrect: 2.5 kg at 84.50 rupees/kg costs 211.25 kg.
Correction: The kg units cancel when rate is multiplied by mass, leaving 211.25 rupees.
Choose the operation
Incorrect: Multiply total bill by mass to get price per kg.
Correction: Divide the total by kilograms to get the one-kilogram price.

How do you place the point after multiplication?

Add the decimal places of the two factors before trimming final zeros.

How do you make a decimal divisor whole?

Multiply both divisor and dividend by the same power of ten.

What does a repeated remainder mean?

The subsequent decimal digits start repeating.

How do 0.5 × 8 and 8 ÷ 0.5 differ?

One finds half of eight; the other counts sixteen halves.

When is a century year a leap year?

When it is divisible by 400.

How do you check a quotient?

Multiply it by the divisor. In a division with remainder, add the remainder using the same units.

Before submitting an answer

  • Check multiplication with place counts and division by multiplying back.
  • State the rounding place for an approximation.
